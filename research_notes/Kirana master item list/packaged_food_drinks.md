# Kirana master item list: packaged foods and beverages (India, 2026)

## What GST rates apply to packaged food and drinks after the September 2025 changes?

### Takeaway
GST 2.0 took effect on 22 Sep 2025. Most packaged foods are now at 5%: biscuits, namkeen, chocolate, sauces, pasta and noodles, butter, ghee, cheese, juices, coffee, malt drinks and packaged water. UHT milk, pre-packaged paneer and Indian breads are at 0%. Aerated, caffeinated and carbonated fruit drinks are at 40%, and the compensation cess was removed.

### Cited Findings
- New rates took effect 22 Sep 2025. Biscuits and namkeen moved to 5%, and select luxury and sin goods to 40% — [Tally Solutions](https://tallysolutions.com/gst/new-gst-rates-september-2025-business-impact/)
- UHT milk, pre-packaged paneer and Indian breads (roti, chapati, paratha) are now nil. Aerated, caffeinated and carbonated fruit drinks are now 40% — [Petpooja](https://blog.petpooja.com/finance-compliance/new-gst-rates-on-food/); [Kanakkupillai](https://www.kanakkupillai.com/learn/gst-2-0-reforms/)
- Aerated drinks went from 28% plus 12% cess to 40% with no cess, so shelf prices barely changed — [Enkash](https://www.enkash.com/resources/blog/types-of-gst-rates-in-india-new-rate-list-and-updates); [Petpooja](https://blog.petpooja.com/finance-compliance/new-gst-rates-on-food/)
- Butter, ghee, chocolates and cold drinks: the list of what got cheaper and what got costlier — [Curly Tales](https://curlytales.com/india/ct-scoop/butter-ghee-chocolates-cold-drinks-what-got-cheaper-costlier-after-gst-rate-revision/amp/); [The News Minute](https://www.thenewsminute.com/news/gst-20-what-gets-cheaper-and-costlier-from-sep-22)
- Sources disagree on the earlier rates for some items (for example, chocolate at 18% versus "12-18%"). The new 5% rate is consistent across sources — [Kanakkupillai](https://www.kanakkupillai.com/learn/gst-2-0-reforms/) vs [Tally](https://tallysolutions.com/gst/new-gst-rates-september-2025-business-impact/)

### Inferences
- GST rates by HSN chapter used in the table below. These come from the summaries above plus my knowledge of the GST 2.0 schedule; each needs to be checked against the CBIC notification. Items marked "5?" or "18?" are not confirmed.
  - **5%:** 1905 (biscuits, cakes, rusk), 1902 (noodles and pasta), 1904 (cornflakes and oats preparations), 1806 (chocolate), 1704 (sugar confectionery), 2103 (sauces, ketchup, mayonnaise), 2104 (soups), 2007 (jam), 2001 (pickles), 2005 and 2008 (chips and peanuts), 2106 (namkeen and mixes), 2101 (instant coffee), 0901 and 0902 (coffee and tea), 0405 and 0406 (butter, ghee, cheese), 0402 (milk powder), 2009 and 2202 non-aerated (juices and fruit drinks), 2201 (packaged water)
  - **0%:** 1905 90 (plain bread and papad), 0401 (fresh and UHT milk), 0406 (paneer)
  - **40%:** 2202 (aerated, sugared and caffeinated drinks, including energy drinks)
- Pack sizes and price-point SKUs come from general market knowledge of 2024-25 shelves and were not checked against sources one by one. Brands change grammage often (shrinkflation), so the app should let shops edit pack sizes.
- The Hindi names are the common shop terms used for brands. A brand name is usually written the same way in Devanagari.

### Gaps
- I did not confirm from a primary source (CBIC rate notification 9/2025-CT(Rate)) the rates for: pre-packaged curd, lassi and buttermilk (believed to stay at 5%); plain unsweetened soda (believed 5%, possibly 18%); chewing gum (believed 5%); and pizza bread. These are marked with "?".
- Each pack size is not sourced. Marketing changes them often.
- I did not check the 8-digit HSN codes item by item. The table gives 4- to 8-digit codes from the standard tariff, and these need verification.
