# Put every medicine on a numbered map

Grocery Khata should give the medical shop type a free-form, per-shop location map: a short location code on every item (such as "A-R3-S2-B14" or "Fridge"), built from optional parts (almirah, rack, shelf, box), and a small set of special zones with built-in rules: Fridge, Schedule X locked cupboard, Expiry/Return shelf and Back store. The location should show next to the medicine name on the billing screen, appear on pick lists and stock-count sheets sorted by rack, and come in through an Excel import. No Indian law or standard fixes a layout or a code format. Chemists mostly group stock by manufacturer, by dosage form or by a mix of both, so the app must copy whatever is painted or stuck on the shop's racks, not impose a scheme. The law cares only about segregation. Schedule X must be under lock and key or in a restricted area, cold-chain items must be stored as the label says, and H1 sales need their own register. Those rules map neatly onto zones, and each zone can drive a warning. Competitors show the gap. GoFrugal shows rack, shelf and box on the bill. Marg has a single rack field, rack-wise stock and rack-to-rack transfer, but no direct import of rack numbers. Vyapar has only godowns. No vendor documents locations per batch or a stock count done rack by rack, so those are where Grocery Khata can lead. The benefit is real but unmeasured: the only evidence is vendor claims of fewer "where is it?" queries and fewer stock mismatches, with no published speed or error figures.

## No rule fixes the layout, so the app must copy the shop's own

Indian chemists arrange stock in several ways. A long-standing habit is grouping by manufacturer, for example all Glaxo products in one cupboard ([drugxpert](https://drugxpert.blogspot.com/2019/04/medical-product-arrangement-community-retail-pharmacy.html)). Stores often mix methods. **Dosage form is called the best single method, and pure alphabetical-by-brand order has drawbacks** ([Remedial Health](https://medium.com/@remedialhealth/how-to-organize-and-manage-inventory-in-your-pharmacy-or-patent-medicine-store-6a808f43034b)). The same source gives the advice that matters most for design: keep fast movers within easy reach, label every shelf and section, and keep the layout consistent so new staff can find things. No survey says how common each layout is. No published convention covers almirah letters or "dabba" drawer numbers, though both are almost certainly in use.

So the location field must be optional and free text, with structure as a help rather than a demand. A shop with nothing numbered must still be able to bill. A shop that paints "R3" on a rack and "14" on a box should be able to type "R3-14" and never see a form with four mandatory dropdowns. The software precedent backs this up. **Marg uses a single "Rack No" text field on the item master** ([Marg Care](https://care.margcompusoft.com/margerp/inventory-master/107301/1/How-to-disable-rack-no.)). **GoFrugal names three levels: rack, shelf and box** ([GoFrugal KB](https://community.gofrugal.com/portal/en/kb/articles/view-item-name-location-on-sales-screen-rack-shelf-box-details)). The recommended model takes the best of both. Store one display code that the shopkeeper sees and types. Behind it, keep optional parsed parts (almirah, rack, shelf, box) so the app can sort and filter. The app builds the code from the parts when they are given, and accepts the code alone when they are not.

| Element | Recommendation | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Location master | Per-shop list of places, each with a code, an optional name, a parent (almirah > rack > shelf > box) and a zone type | Shops number differently, and the hierarchy lets the app sort counts and pick lists |
| Item link | Primary (counter) location plus extra locations; optional location per batch | Same medicine kept at the counter and in the back store; a new batch may sit in a different box |
| Zone types | Normal, Fridge, Schedule X locked, Expiry/Return, Back store (godown) | Each special zone carries a rule or a warning |
| Entry | Typed code, picker, Excel import, and a "put away" step on distributor bill entry | Getting codes in quickly decides whether shops use the feature at all |

## Special zones turn legal segregation into app rules

The Drugs and Cosmetics Rules set the few hard constraints. **Schedule X stock at retail must be under lock and key or in a restricted area**, it needs its own licence forms (20F/21G), and records are kept two years. In practice a commercial guide says a marked carton on a separate rack, checked monthly, is enough ([OkCredit](https://okcredit.in/blog/dos-and-donts-for-medical-store-owners/)), although that is a secondary, commercial source. Storage conditions come from Rule 96, Schedule P and the label ([thehealthmaster](https://thehealthmaster.com/2021/11/02/duties-of-retail-chemists-medical-store-pharmacy)). **Each H1 supply goes into a separate register** with prescriber, patient, drug and quantity ([GKToday](https://www.gktoday.in/schedule-h-schedule-x-and-schedule-h1-drugs/)). The Health Ministry has proposed **mandatory CCTV at stores selling H, H1 and X drugs**, but that is not confirmed as in force ([BioSpectrum](https://www.biospectrumindia.com/news/73/28496/health-ministry-proposes-amendment-to-drugs-rules-1945.html)).

Since the app already has schedule flags, a cold-chain flag and expiry returns, the zones can check consistency rather than just store labels. When an item flagged Schedule X is given a location outside a "Schedule X locked" zone, the app should warn. It should also warn when a cold-chain item is placed outside the Fridge zone. Billing a Schedule X line can show "Locked cupboard" in red, which reminds staff that the item needs a key and a prescription. Expired or near-expiry batches marked for return should move, in the app, to the Expiry/Return zone. This keeps them out of the sellable stock at billing and gives a ready list for the distributor return. Treat these as warnings, not blocks: no primary text was found for the exact 2–8°C fridge requirement or for a mandated expired-stock shelf, even though both are standard practice. The Back store zone works like a godown. Stock there counts in total stock but is not shown as "at counter". A low counter quantity with stock in the back store should prompt "refill from back store" rather than "reorder".

## Billing and stock counts are where locations pay off

GoFrugal shows the value most directly. A setting under Sales, **"Show Item position (Rack/Shelf/Box) along with item name"**, puts the location on the billing screen. GoFrugal says this cuts customer queries about where items are and reduces stock mismatches ([GoFrugal KB](https://community.gofrugal.com/portal/en/kb/gofrugalretaileasy/configurations/sales/articles/view-item-name-location-on-sales-screen-rack-shelf-box-details)). Billing software guides make the same generic claim that rack-wise records help staff find items while billing ([Refrens](https://www.refrens.com/grow/best-billing-software-for-medical-shops/)). These are vendor claims. **No measured figures exist for picking speed or wrong-medicine errors at Indian chemists**, so Grocery Khata should not promise numbers. If it wants a figure later, it should measure from its own data.

For Grocery Khata this means four surfaces. In the search results at billing, show the location code next to the name and batch. Because FEFO already picks the batch, show that batch's location when it differs from the item's primary place. On a multi-line bill, offer a pick list sorted by location, so a helper walks the shop once. Marg has a help article titled "Rack wise indexing of items in bill", but its content could not be confirmed ([Marg Care](https://care.margcompusoft.com/margerp/inventory-master/107301/1/How-to-disable-rack-no.)). For stock counts, generate count sheets rack by rack. Staff then count one shelf, enter the quantities and close it, and variances are reported per location. No competitor documents this. When a distributor bill is entered, add a "put away" step that suggests each line's usual location and lets staff confirm or change it. That keeps locations current without extra work.

## Import, move and renumber close the competitor gaps

Existing tools show what to copy and what to fix. **Marg manages stock rack-wise and lets users transfer stock from one rack to another** ([Marg Care](https://care.margcompusoft.com/margerp/inventory/1633/1/-How-to-transfer-Stock)). However, **Marg has no direct import for rack numbers**. Its documented workaround loads them through the Barcode field and renames that field "Rackno" in reports ([Marg Care](https://care.margcompusoft.com/margerp/copy/184076/1/how-to-import-rack-number-in-all-item-of-marg-software)). **Vyapar supports only godown-to-godown transfer** and has no rack field ([Vyapar](https://vyaparapp.in/guides/how-to-add-and-manage-godowns-in-vyapar)). Public listings for eVitalRx and Medeil mention inventory and multi-location features but no rack field ([Capterra eVitalRx](https://www.capterra.com/p/237227/eVitalRx/); [Capterra Medeil](https://www.capterra.com/p/117249/Medeil/)). Their apps may have one that is simply not documented.

Grocery Khata's existing Excel import screen is the natural place to add a "Location" column, so a shop switching from Marg can bring its rack numbers across in one file. The app also needs three operations that no vendor documents. The first is a move (a single item, or a whole shelf, from one place to another) that keeps a history. The second is bulk renumbering, such as "R3 → R5", for when an almirah is added or racks are rearranged. The third is a "no location yet" filter, so the owner can fill gaps over time. How often chemists renumber is unknown, but a bulk rename costs little and saves a shop from retyping hundreds of items.

## Conclusion

Location tracking looks like a single text field, and Marg and GoFrugal mostly treat it that way. What makes it valuable is linking it to what Grocery Khata already knows about each medicine. Linked to schedule flags, a location becomes a compliance check: a Schedule X item outside the locked cupboard, or a vaccine outside the fridge, is a warning. Linked to batches and FEFO, it shows the exact box for the batch being billed. Linked to expiry returns, it gives a physical return shelf that matches the credit-note list. Linked to stock counts, it lets a count be split into short rack-by-rack jobs. No documented competitor does batch-level locations or counts by rack. The sensible order of work is: the optional code and Excel import, then display at billing, then zone warnings, then rack-wise counts. Because no benefit has been measured, the app should record its own before-and-after data (time to bill, count variances) so it can prove the value to the next shop.
