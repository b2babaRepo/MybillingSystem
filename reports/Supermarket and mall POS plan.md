# Start with small-town supermarkets, then mall kiosks

**Yes, but only for one segment to begin with.** Apna Kaarobar can sell a POS to **independent supermarkets and mini-marts with 1 to 3 billing counters, mainly in tier-2 and tier-3 towns**. It should not try to sell to large supermarket chains or to mall fashion and electronics chains yet. The code review found that the app already does most of what a single supermarket counter needs: barcode scanning, a weighing scale on desktop Chrome, keyboard shortcuts, offline billing, MRP enforcement, batches and expiry, purchase orders, goods receipts, stocktake and cross-counter exchange slips. What is missing is the work of running several tills in one shop. That means cashier shifts with a cash float for each till, held bills that any till can pick up, labels printed by weighing scales, a bill-number design that does not make tills wait for each other, and a catalogue that stays fast at 20,000 items. That work comes to roughly **two to three months of Small and Medium items**, and none of it needs new architecture. Mall stores are a sensible second step, because single-store mall tenants and kiosks need one extra thing the app lacks: an automatic daily sales feed to the mall (called **ADSR**, "automated daily sales reporting"). Chains are a later market, because head-office control over many stores is a **Large** rebuild of how the app separates shops. On price, the main competitor is **Zoho POS at ₹649 per store per month** (about ₹7,800 a year). A flat **₹6,000 to ₹7,500 per store per year for up to 3 counters, plus a paid setup fee**, would undercut most rivals while staying far above kirana-app pricing. The tool that gathered the market and price figures could not open full web pages, so most outside numbers in this report come from search-result summaries. They are marked as unverified where it matters.

> **About the evidence.** Four of the five research notes say page fetching was blocked in their session. Their competitor prices, market counts and mall facts come from search-engine snippets, not from reading the vendor or government pages. Check every price against the live vendor page before you publish your own price list. The fifth note, the app gap analysis, comes from reading this repository's code. The code was not run, so the gaps are informed estimates and have not been tested.

## The target is the "missing middle", roughly 8,000 independent stores

The market splits into three tiers. Kirana and small-shop tools (Vyapar, Marg, Busy, Tally, Khatabook) sit at the bottom. Retail POS specialists (GoFrugal, LOGIC ERP, Zoho POS, Petpooja Invoice, Ginesys) serve supermarkets and mid-size chains. Enterprise suites (Oracle Xstore, SAP, LS Retail) serve the big mall chains through IT consultancies ([Petpooja blog](https://blog.petpooja.com/industry-business-guides/petpooja-invoice-vs-gofrugal-pos/); [Infosys](https://www.infosys.com/services/oracle/offerings/revolutionize-retail-operations.html); [LS Retail on Capterra](https://www.capterra.in/software/1015455/ls-retail)). The gap between the bottom two tiers is the opening. Stores with 1 to 3 counters outgrow kirana apps, which break "the moment a second counter is added" ([Hedgethink](https://www.hedgethink.com/best-pos-billing-software-for-small-multi-counter-shops-in-india-real-cost-staff-training-test/)). Chain-grade tools are priced well beyond them: Ginesys's Zwing cloud POS needs at least 4 users at ₹4,000 per user per month, plus implementation fees ([Ginesys pricing](https://www.ginesys.in/pricing)).

The segment is real but small, so plan for steady growth, not a land grab. Mint, citing the loyalty firm Love in Store, counted **7,914 independent modern-trade stores at the end of 2023**, and other estimates run to about 10,000 ([Mint via Magzter](https://www.magzter.com/en/stories/newspaper/Mint-Mumbai/MODERN-TRADE-STORES-POP-UP-ACROSS-INDIA)). Statista's broader definition gives over 22,000 for 2022 ([Statista](https://statista.com/statistics/1048779/india-number-of-grocery-retail-stores)) (unverified, and the two definitions do not match). Kiranas still hold about **91% of grocery** ([Tribune India on Redseer](https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/consumption-patterns/indias-mass-grocery-continues-to-rely-on-kiranas-report)). The biggest pool of buyers is therefore the "upgraded kirana", the self-service general store that is turning into a mini-mart, and those owners are exactly the people who already use your app. No reliable figure exists for spending on POS *software* in India. The analyst figures found count devices and payment terminals, and they disagree with each other by about nine times ([IMARC](https://imarcgroup.com/india-pos-terminals-market); [Mordor via MarketResearch.com](https://marketresearch.com/Mordor-Intelligence-LLP-v4018/India-POS-Terminals-Share-Trends-38118938)). Do not use them.

Small towns are the better place to start. In DMart's Q1 FY27 results, mature metro stores were flat while non-metro stores kept growing. Brokers blame quick-commerce apps for pulling metro shoppers away ([Outlook Business](https://www.outlookbusiness.com/corporate/dmart-shuts-online-grocery-operations-in-7-cities-focuses-on-metros); [HDFC Sky](https://hdfcsky.com/news/dmart-shares-fall-3-6percent-as-q1-results-miss-expectations)). Mall building is also moving to smaller cities: JLL expects over 26 million sq ft of tier-2/3 mall space by 2030 ([Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/amp/finance/personal-finance/mall-boom-ahead-india-s-retail-reits-may-become-80-000-cr-market-by-2030-125101700095_1.html)). One signal shows how poorly the market is served: SuperK, a grocery chain of 130 stores in small Andhra Pradesh towns, built its own POS rather than buy one ([Inc42](https://inc42.com/?p=524295)).

The biggest threat is **Zoho**. It sells Zoho POS with a free tier ([Zoho POS pricing](https://www.zoho.com/en-in/pos/pricing.html)), and GoFrugal says it is "now a part of Zoho" ([GoFrugal announcement](https://www.gofrugal.com/announcements/CEO-letter-announcement.html)). One research note found no confirmation of that merger, so treat it as reported, not settled. Payment companies such as Pine Labs, Paytm and PhonePe are a second threat. They already have devices in millions of shops and are adding billing software on top ([Inc42 on Pine Labs](https://inc42.com/features/how-pine-labs-shifted-gears-from-pos-devices-to-an-ai-powered-fintech-stack/)).

## The app already covers the single counter; the work is in running many tills at once

Supermarket buyers expect these basics: barcode scanning and billing of loose items by weight, several counters selling from one live stock count, held bills, shift-wise cash control, split payment across cash, card and UPI, returns at any counter, and above all billing that keeps working when the internet or power fails ([LOGIC ERP](https://www.logicerp.com/retail/supermarket-retail-software); [TheHobbyist](https://www.thehobbyist.in/blog/free-billing-software-kirana-india.html)). Against that list the app starts well. It already treats a scanned barcode as an item, scans with the phone camera, reads a scale over a serial cable (desktop Chrome only), has full keyboard shortcuts (F2 to F9, Ctrl+Enter), saves bills offline first, splits a bill across up to four payment modes, enforces the MRP cap on the server, picks the batch that expires first, and has purchase orders, goods receipts, stocktake and reorder. Its exchange slips, which carry a secret code and can be spent at any counter but only once, are already better than many rivals ([BillScreen.tsx](../frontend/src/screens/BillScreen.tsx); [outbox.ts](../frontend/src/lib/outbox.ts); [ExchangeSlips.cs](../backend/src/GroceryKhata.Api/Ledger/ExchangeSlips.cs)).

The gaps show up once a shop runs several tills. **Bill numbers are the hidden risk.** The app keeps one bill series per shop per year, and the code locks that counter row for the whole time a bill is being saved, so two tills "queue on the counter row". At 5 to 20 tills, every bill waits for the one before it ([LedgerService.Bills.cs](../backend/src/GroceryKhata.Api/Ledger/LedgerService.Bills.cs)). The only load test so far simulates 25 shops with 2 cashiers each, about half a bill per second per shop. No test has run 10 or more tills in one shop ([loadtest/evening-rush.mjs](../loadtest/evening-rush.mjs); [docs/runbook.md](../docs/runbook.md)). Several other gaps sit alongside it. Held bills live on one device only. Weighing-scale labels, the barcodes starting with "2" that carry a price or a weight, are not read at all. Each product can have only one barcode. The whole catalogue downloads to every device and is searched item by item, which works for a few thousand items but not the 5,000 to 10,000 or more a supermarket carries ([BillingFast](https://www.billingfast.com/free/grocery-store-billing-software); [SwilERP](https://www.swindia.com/product/supermarket-software)). The day-end cash count covers the whole shop's drawer, not each till or each cashier, and nothing opens a cash drawer ([CashCounts.cs](../backend/src/GroceryKhata.Api/Ledger/CashCounts.cs); [escpos.ts](../frontend/src/lib/escpos.ts)).

The list below puts the work in priority order. Sizes come from the code review: **Small** is days, **Medium** is 1 to 3 weeks, **Large** is longer or needs new architecture.

Phase 1 is about 6 to 10 weeks of Small and Medium items, adding up the sizes above. That is enough to sell honestly to a 1 to 3 counter store whose offers are simple percentage or rupee discounts, which the app already does. The promotion engine is the one Large item a supermarket buyer will ask about early. Start it in parallel, because GoFrugal and LOGIC both lead with it.

Compliance adds little extra work for this segment. The rule requiring a dynamic QR code on every customer bill applies only above ₹500 crore turnover ([Tally](https://tallysolutions.com/gst/dynamic-quick-response-qr-code-on-b2c-invoices)), which no target customer will reach. E-invoicing (a government number, the IRN, on each business-to-business invoice) applies above ₹5 crore turnover ([Tally](https://tallysolutions.com/gst/e-invoicing-threshold/)), and the code review could not confirm that the app really generates IRNs. That matters only for stores with large sales to other businesses. If a bill series is added per counter, it must still feed GSTR-1 Table 13, which lists the invoice series issued and how many bills were cancelled ([GSTN advisory](https://www.mahagst.gov.in/public/uploads/gstnadvisory/1760596735_345%20Reporting%20of%20HSN%20codes%20in%20Table%2012%20and%20list%20of%20documents%20in%20table%2013%20of%20GSTR%201%201A.pdf)). The loyalty feature (item 11) will need consent notices and a way to erase data before India's new data protection rules (DPDP) take full effect around **May 2027** ([PIB](https://static.pib.gov.in/WriteReadData/specificdocs/documents/2025/nov/doc20251117695301.pdf)). A warning when one customer's cash payments in a day reach ₹2 lakh would help with the Section 269ST limit ([Income Tax Dept](https://www.incometaxindia.gov.in/w/section-269st)).

## Mall tenants need a daily sales feed, and the app has none

Mall rent in India is usually a **fixed minimum rent plus a share of the store's sales, often 6 to 12%, whichever is higher** ([Orion Malls](https://www.orionmalls.com/blogs/retail-leasing-models-fixed-rent-vs-revenue-sharing); [IIFL/JLL](https://www.indiainfoline.com/knowledge-center/share-market/how-revenue-share-works-and-evaluating-a-mall-jll)). Phoenix Mills, for example, combines a minimum rent with a revenue share that kicks in once sales pass the threshold ([smallcase](https://www.smallcase.com/blog/phoenix-mills-business-model/)). The mall therefore needs every tenant's sales every day. In India this is mostly done through **ADSR**, short for automated (or "all day") sales reporting. A third-party ADSR company either pulls sales from any tenant's POS without changing it, or the POS vendor sells a "mall integration" module. Onintouch says it serves 16 or more malls, including the Phoenix portfolio, and works with Pine Labs, Petpooja, GoFrugal, Tally and custom systems ([Onintouch](https://onintouch.com/faq); [Onintouch about](https://onintouch.com/about-us)). These are the vendor's own claims (unverified). On the POS side, GoFrugal sells a licensed "ADSR Mall Integration" add-on that gives the mall a login which can see sales data only ([GoFrugal KB](https://community.gofrugal.com/portal/en/kb/articles/adsr-mall-integration-license-sync-in-rpos)). VasyERP and QueueBuster sell similar modules ([VasyERP](https://www.vasyerp.com/retailing/pos-software-shopping-malls); [QueueBuster](https://queuebuster.co/pos-software-partners/adsr)).

Apna Kaarobar has the raw material but no way to deliver it. The day report already breaks down totals by payment mode and by till, the sales register CSV includes the counter and the user, cancelled bills are never hidden, and bill numbers have no gaps. Those are exactly the honesty features malls check for. But every export needs the owner's own login, no outside system can be given an access key, and nothing sends the data automatically each night ([GstExport.cs](../backend/src/GroceryKhata.Api/Ledger/GstExport.cs); [DayReport.cs](../backend/src/GroceryKhata.Api/Ledger/DayReport.cs)). A mall feed needs three parts. The first is a read-only access key for one shop that shows sales and nothing else, because tenants protect their margin and customer data. The second is bill-level and daily figures: bill number, time, gross, discount, returns and voids, sales net of GST, and the payment split. The third is a scheduled delivery, as an SFTP file, CSV, REST push or email, in each mall's own format. The code review sizes this at **Medium for the common part plus Small for each mall's format**. Malls use these feeds to catch under-reporting and they flag unusual refunds, so "net sales" must be defined the way the lease defines it: excluding GST and after returns ([Mallsense](https://project7421800.tilda.ws/mallsense-sales-data-capture)).

One question needs answering before you build. **No Indian mall operator publishes its tenant data format, deadline or penalties.** These sit in lease annexures and fit-out manuals that are not public. Outside India, in Singapore and Malaysia, a missed daily submission can lead to estimated, higher rent, and each tenant gets a mall-issued machine ID ([HitPay](https://docs.hitpayapp.com/pos/mall-gto); [SHOPLINE](https://help.shopline.com/hc/en-001/articles/18237228352537-Automated-GTO-Transmission-for-Malls)). Indian practice is unconfirmed. The cheapest first step is to talk to Onintouch and to one target mall's tenant team, and to build the format they already accept. If ADSR companies mostly pull data, as Mallsense says about 90% of connections do, a clean read-only API may be all you need for many malls. The best mall customers to start with are single-store tenants and kiosks, which need a small, mobile, quick-to-set-up POS that handles weekend rushes ([Fynd/Wildstone case](https://www.fynd.com/customer-stories/when-you-go-from-0-to-25-mall-kiosks-wildstone-x-fynd-pos)). Mall *chains* buy centrally at head office and expect enterprise features, so they belong after the head-office work (item 18). Two warnings apply. About 20% of Indian malls are "ghost assets" ([Knight Frank](https://content.knightfrank.com/research/2830/documents/en/think-india-think-retail-2024-11173.pdf)), so some tenants will close. Mall gift cards such as Phoenix's are Visa cards and are taken on the normal card machine, so they need no work ([HDFC Phoenix gift card](https://hdfcbank.com/personal/pay/cards/prepaid-cards/hdfc-phoenix-gift-card/faq)).

## Price at about ₹6,000 to ₹7,500 per store per year, below Zoho, with a paid setup fee

Published prices for a single store (all from search summaries; check each on the vendor's own page before use) put the floor and the ceiling of the market here:

|---|---|---|
| Vyapar | Desktop ₹3,399/yr ([Capterra](https://www.capterra.com/p/180579/Vyapar/pricing/)) (unverified; sources conflict) | Per device |
| Petpooja Invoice | From ₹5,000/yr, "regardless of counters" ([Software Advice](https://www.softwareadvice.com/product/530705-Petpooja-Invoice/)) (unverified) | Flat per business |

| GoFrugal | About ₹8,999/yr starter ([Petpooja blog](https://blog.petpooja.com/industry-business-guides/pos-software-grocery-store-india/)) (unverified, figure from a competitor) | Per store, extra counters charged |

| Shopify POS Pro | ₹7,000/mo per location ([Shopify India](https://shopify.in/pos/pricing)) | Per location |

A 3-counter supermarket therefore pays a few thousand rupees a year on Vyapar, about ₹9,000 to ₹26,000 on GoFrugal or Marg once extra counters are added, and ₹1.4 to ₹2.4 lakh on Zwing. That wide band is the opportunity. Competitor blogs say rivals usually add 50 to 100% of the base price for each extra counter ([Petpooja blog](https://blog.petpooja.com/industry-business-guides/retail-billing-software-price-india/)) (unverified, and from a vendor that sells against per-counter pricing). Owners complain about these surprise charges.

My recommendation, which is a judgement and not a sourced figure, is a separately named **"Kaarobar POS" plan at about ₹6,000 to ₹7,500 per store per year (or ₹599 to ₹699 a month), covering up to 3 counters with no per-counter charge**. That is below Zoho Standard's ₹7,800 a year for a single register and below GoFrugal's starter, and it keeps the kirana plan's low price separate. Add a **one-time setup fee** for loading the item master, printing barcode labels and training the cashiers. The research found no market rate for this, so start in the low thousands of rupees and adjust from what partners charge. Sell the mall feed as an **add-on** priced per store per year, as GoFrugal does with its ADSR licence; there is no public price to anchor it, so test it with your first mall tenants. Keep the plan above kirana pricing because cheap SMB billing loses money: Vyapar spent **₹2.04 for every ₹1 of revenue** in FY25 ([Entrackr](https://entrackr.com/fintrackr/vyapar-posts-rs-63-cr-loss-in-fy25-cash-reserve-fades-93-10819211)), and supermarkets cost far more to support than kiranas. Hardware is the customer's own cost. A tablet, scanner and printer kit runs about ₹12,000 to ₹18,000, and a full counter with a drawer and a desktop terminal runs ₹35,000 to ₹75,000 or more ([Commmerce](https://commmerce.com/blog/retail-hardware-kirana-stores-india-pos-barcode-scanner-guide-2026/); [Razorpay](https://razorpay.com/blog/retail-pos-system-guide-india/)). Card-machine rental is ₹325 to ₹800 a month, and the card fee for grocery stores is about 1.3% ([Razorpay](https://razorpay.com/blog/pos-machine-charges/); [ICICI](https://www.icicibank.com/business-banking/cash-management-services/eazypay-schedule-of-charges)).

## Sell through local partners who install, not through app downloads

Supermarket POS is sold by people who visit the store. GoFrugal's partner programme has "Sales partners" who handle the demo, installation, activation and 24x7 support, alongside referral-only partners ([GoFrugal partners](https://www.gofrugal.com/partners)). Vyapar reports 27,000 partners ([Tribune India](https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/business/1-cr-businesses-27000-partners-and-growing-how-vyapar-is-powering-indias-msme-revolution)) (unverified). Petpooja's founder calls partners its growth engine ([Ascendants](https://ascendants.in/the-ascendants/from-local-kitchens-to-global-cloud-the-petpooja-story/)). The practical plan for you is to recruit local computer and POS-hardware dealers in two or three tier-2 towns as installation partners. Pay them from the setup fee and a share of the yearly subscription; no competitor's partner margins were found, so you set this yourself. Have them sell a hardware-plus-software bundle. Your existing kirana customers who are upgrading to self-service are the warmest leads, and the move from khata app to POS keeps their customer credit (udhaar) history, which no rival can offer. Listings on IndiaMART and Techjockey work as lead sources ([IndiaMART](https://m.indiamart.com/impcat/supermarket-billing-software.html)).

Sell on the weaknesses competitors are criticised for. GoFrugal reviews complain about support that does "not [come] on right time" ([Software Advice](https://www.softwareadvice.com/erp/gofrugal-pos-profile/reviews/)). Marg's prices change from one dealer to the next ([ERP Research](https://www.erpresearch.com/pricing/marg-erp)). Owners also complain about sync failures between counters and about wrong profit reports ([Google Play reviews](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.webkul.mobikul.pos&hl=en_IN)). So promise one printed price, fast WhatsApp support in Hindi and the local language, offline billing that has actually been tested at many counters (which is why item 1 comes first), and an easy import from Marg, Busy or Tally. The Pine Labs integration (item 14) also gives a partnership angle: LOGIC ERP already markets the same link ([LOGIC ERP x Pine Labs](https://www.logicerp.com/pinelabs-integration)).

## Conclusion

You do not need to build a new product to enter this market. You need to make the current one dependable at three tills in one shop, and then prove it with a load test before telling any customer. The bill-number lock is the most important item on the list. It barely matters in a kirana, but it decides whether a supermarket's evening rush runs smoothly or tills sit waiting. The khata data the app already keeps can become the reason a growing kirana upgrades to your POS instead of moving to Zoho. Mall stores are worth the extra Medium build only after a real mall or ADSR company confirms the data format, because that format is the one requirement nobody publishes.

Several things are still unknown and could change these recommendations. How many 1 to 3 counter stores exist in your first towns, and what they will pay, has not been measured. Indian mall lease terms for sales reporting were not found. Every competitor price came from search summaries. The cheapest way to settle all three is to visit 10 to 15 supermarkets in your first towns and call one mall leasing office before committing the 6 to 10 weeks of Phase 1.
