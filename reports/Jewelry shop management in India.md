# Weight, purity and fine gold power jewellery billing

Grocery Khata can build a credible jewellery section by adding three things its current item-and-quantity model lacks. The first is a **weight-and-purity billing engine**: net weight × the day's karat rate, plus wastage, making, stones and hallmark charge, at **3% GST on the whole value**. The second is a **dual-ledger stock model** that tracks tagged pieces one by one alongside loose metal held as fine (24K-equivalent) grams. The third is a **compliance layer** covering BIS hallmark lines and HUID, the ₹2 lakh cash and PAN limits, and PMLA cash aggregation. That is enough to match Vyapar's jewellery claims. To beat Vyapar, the section also needs the three features that separate dedicated jewellery ERPs from general billing apps, none of which Vyapar's jewellery pages mention: **girvi (pledge loans), gold savings schemes, and a karigar fine-gold ledger**. Dedicated ERPs such as Omunim, Marg and GehnaERP already have these, but they are mostly desktop-first, dealer-sold, priced on request and carry AMC fees, and users complain about slow support and dated interfaces. Grocery Khata's existing khata, WhatsApp, Hindi/Gujarati and phone-plus-computer setup puts it in the gap between "cheap but shallow" and "deep but costly". Auromine and SthirApp already target that gap, so it is not empty. One finding should shape the build from the start: several competitors and popular blogs, including Vyapar's own page, describe "3% on gold, 5% on making". The CBIC FAQ position is 3% on the full transaction value for a retail sale, with 5% only for karigar job work. Getting this right is both a compliance duty and a selling point.

## A jewellery bill is a weight calculation, not a price lookup

Every line on a jewellery bill is built from weight, not from a stored price. The base formula is **(net weight × the day's rate for that purity) + wastage + making + stone value + hallmark charge = taxable value**, followed by GST and rounding ([Fyond](https://fyond.com/blog/jewellery-shop-billing-huid-gram-pricing-india); [myBillBook](https://mybillbook.in/blog/how-to-create-gst-bills-for-gold-and-silver-jewellery/)). Net weight is gross weight minus stone weight, and diamonds convert at **1 carat = 0.2 g** ([myBillBook](https://mybillbook.in/s/?p=28838)). The rate for lower karats comes from the 24K rate: 22K is 24K × 0.916 ([Jainam](https://www.jainam.in/?p=39007)). IBJA publishes AM and PM benchmark rates **per 10 g for 999, 995, 916, 750 and 585 purity, excluding GST and making**, and some days have no fixing ([5paisa](https://www.5paisa.com/blog/gold-rate-today-09-nov-2026)). Rates move a lot. Day-to-day changes in Oct–Nov 2026 ran from about ₹200 to ₹1,200 per 10 g ([5paisa](https://www.5paisa.com/blog/gold-rate-today-28-oct-2026)). So each bill must save the rate it used, the rate must be editable on a single bill, and the rate board needs a history with more than one version per day ([Fyond](https://fyond.com/blog/jewellery-shop-billing-huid-gram-pricing-india)).

Shops charge making in three ways: **per gram, as a percentage of metal value** ([ClearTax](https://cleartax.in/s/gold-making-charges); [CaratLane](https://www.caratlane.com/blog/how-to-calculate-the-price-of-your-gold-jewellery)), or as a **fixed amount per piece**. The fixed per-piece method is common on shop bills, but no source in the research documents it. Wastage is the metal lost in making. It is typically **5–15%** depending on design ([Bluestone](https://blog.bluestone.com/how-to-calculate-the-cost-of-your-gold-jewellery-at-home/)). In South India it is often folded into a single "VA" (value addition) percentage, roughly 8–12% for machine-made chains and 15–25% for bridal sets ([DigiRake](https://knowledge.digirake.com/lifestyle/va-in-gold-jewellery)). Silver is quoted per kg and billed per gram, with making of about **₹10–50/g** ([Riyo Gems](https://riyogems.com/blogs/925/understanding-silver-making-charges-a-comprehensive-guide-to-jewelry-manufacturing-costs)). Standard silver articles are often priced per piece, for example ₹300 making per piece ([TallyAtCloud](https://www.tallyatcloud.com/article/silver-price-in-madhya-pradesh-indore-bhopal-markets-on-4-december-2025-how-to-record-silver-rates-in-tally-prime-complete-guide/515/0/1)). Each item therefore needs a `sale_mode` of by-weight or by-piece.

### Three worked bills

The examples below use the IBJA 24K rate of **₹1,41,082 per 10 g (27 Oct 2026)** ([5paisa](https://www.5paisa.com/blog/gold-rate-today-27-oct-2026)), which is ₹14,108.20/g. That gives 22K = ₹12,923.11/g and 18K = ₹10,581.15/g. The wastage, making and diamond figures are illustrative. The arithmetic has been checked.

**Example A: 22K plain chain, 8% wastage plus ₹350/g making**

| Line | Calculation | ₹ |
|---|---|---|
| Gross = net weight 20.450 g (no stones) | | |
| Gold value | 20.450 × 12,923.11 | 2,64,277.60 |
| Wastage 8% | 1.636 g × 12,923.11 | 21,142.21 |
| Making ₹350/g | 20.450 × 350 | 7,157.50 |
| **Taxable value** | | 2,92,577.31 |
| CGST 1.5% + SGST 1.5% | 4,388.66 × 2 | 8,777.32 |
| Total, rounded | +0.37 round-off | **3,01,355** |

Under the "5% on making" practice, GST would be ₹8,920.47 instead of ₹8,777.32. That is ₹143 more on this one bill, charged on an interpretation the CBIC FAQ does not support. This bill is also above ₹2 lakh, so the app must ask for PAN and must stop cash payment at ₹1,99,999 (see the compliance section).

**Example B: 18K diamond ring, 15% making, with an old-gold exchange**

| Line | Calculation | ₹ |
|---|---|---|
| Gross 4.200 g incl. 0.30 ct diamonds (0.060 g) | net gold 4.140 g | |
| Gold value | 4.140 × 10,581.15 | 43,805.96 |
| Making 15% of gold value | | 6,570.89 |
| Diamonds 0.30 ct × ₹60,000/ct | | 18,000.00 |
| **Taxable value** | | 68,376.85 |
| GST 3% | | 2,051.31 |
| Invoice total | | 70,428.16 |
| Less old gold: 5.000 g at 87.5% XRF reading = 4.375 g fine × 14,108.20, minus 2% melting deduction | 61,723.38 − 1,234.47 | −60,488.91 |
| **Balance payable** | | **9,939** |

**Example C: silver payal pair.** 120 g × ₹235/g ([Lemonn, Jaipur rate](https://lemonn.co.in/silver-rates/silver-rate-today-in-jaipur)) = ₹28,200. Making at ₹20/g adds ₹2,400, for a taxable value of ₹30,600. GST at 3% is ₹918, giving a total of **₹31,518**.

Each bill line therefore needs these fields: gross, stone and net weight to 3 decimals; purity as karat and fineness; rate per gram; wastage as % or grams; making type and value; one or more stone lines; hallmark charge; HUID; discount; taxable value; CGST/SGST or IGST; and round-off. This fits Grocery Khata's existing GST invoice. It needs a different line model, not a different invoice. Rounding to the nearest rupee versus the nearest ₹10 is not documented anywhere and should be a shop setting.

### Old gold, orders and schemes are part of everyday billing

Old-gold exchange happens daily, not as an edge case. The shop weighs the piece, deducts stones and dirt, tests purity (now usually with a non-destructive XRF karatmeter ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karatmeter))), and credits fine weight × the day's rate less a deduction. Software commonly applies **3–5%** ([Fyond](https://fyond.com/blog/jewellery-shop-billing-huid-gram-pricing-india)). One jeweller claims 5–12% plus a buy/sell spread is typical elsewhere, but that is a seller's claim ([Londe Jewellers](https://londejewellers.com/gold-exchange)). Making and VA are never recovered on resale ([DigiRake](https://knowledge.digirake.com/lifestyle/va-in-gold-jewellery)). The rate board therefore needs a separate **buy rate**, and old gold must go into its own fine-weight stock.

Made-to-order pieces are booked with a design, an approximate weight, a karat, an advance in cash or old gold, and a due date. The order becomes a karigar job card, and the final bill uses the actual delivered weight ([JEWELKAM](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/-/id1591046772)). It is not clear how many small shops let a customer lock today's rate against the advance, so the app should offer both "rate fixed" and "rate on delivery" as options.

Savings schemes run for 10–11 monthly instalments, with a bonus of about one instalment. The balance can only be redeemed as jewellery, and missed payments can forfeit the bonus ([BasuNivesh on Tanishq](https://www.basunivesh.com/tanishq-golden-harvest-savings-scheme-review/); [BankBazaar on Kalyan](https://www.bankbazaar.com/gold-rate/kalyan-jewellers-gold-schemes.html)). Grocery Khata's WhatsApp reminders map directly onto instalment collection.

## Seven rule sets the app must enforce, two of them still moving

**BIS hallmarking.** Gold jewellery sold in notified districts by jewellers with turnover above **₹40 lakh** must carry a three-part mark: the BIS logo, purity in carat and fineness, and a **6-character HUID**. The bill must separately show each article's description, **net precious-metal weight, purity in carat and fineness, and hallmarking charge**, plus a statement that the customer can verify purity at any BIS-recognised assaying and hallmarking centre (AHC) ([BIS](https://www.bis.gov.in/hallmarking-jewellers/?lang=en)). BIS itself says printing HUID on the invoice is currently voluntary, so the app should support it and switch it on by default. Exemptions are by product: articles **under 2 g**, Kundan, Polki and Jadau, bullion and coins, and a few other categories ([Corpseed](https://www.corpseed.com/law-update/gold-hallmarking-fourth-amendment-order-2026)). The district list keeps changing. The Fourth Amendment Order of **2 Nov 2026 raised it from 392 to 398 districts**, with no transition period, following an earlier figure of 380 in March 2026 ([Corpseed](https://www.corpseed.com/law-update/gold-hallmarking-fourth-amendment-order-2026); [IMPRI](https://www.impriindia.com/?p=74995)). The district list must be server-side data, not hard-coded, and the warning "HUID missing on non-exempt item" must check it. Silver HUID applies from 1 Sept 2025 only when a piece is hallmarked, since silver hallmarking remains voluntary ([World Trade Scanner](https://worldtradescanner.com/Update%20on%20Mandatory%20HUID%20for%20Silver%20Jewellery.htm), a secondary source). Penalties under the BIS Act start at a fine of ₹1–2 lakh, and Rule 49 gives customers **2× the purity shortfall** as compensation ([BIS](https://www.bis.gov.in/hallmarking-jewellers/?lang=en)).

**GST.** A retail jewellery sale is taxed at **3% (HSN 7113) on the total transaction value, whether or not making is shown separately**. This is the CBIC FAQ position as quoted by TaxGuru; the FAQ PDF itself was not opened ([TaxGuru](https://taxguru.in/?p=618100); [TaxAJ](https://www.taxaj.com/learn/?p=91066)). The rate was left unchanged by the GST 2.0 rationalisation in Sept 2025 ([Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/industry/news/gst-rate-for-jewellery-unchanged-at-3-pc-but-industry-sees-indirect-gains-125090401225_1.html)). Other activities have their own rates. **Karigar job work is 5% under SAC 9988**, paid by the jeweller under reverse charge (RCM) if the karigar is unregistered. Loose cut diamonds are 0.25%. Repairs are reportedly 18% ([TaxAJ](https://www.taxaj.com/learn/?p=91066)). Gold retained by a job worker counts as part of the job-work value ([AAR Aabhushan](https://gstcouncil.gov.in/sites/default/files/AAR/aabhushan_jewellers_pvt_ltd_advance_ruling.pdf)). Old gold bought from a consumer carries no GST, and the dominant view is that **GST applies to the full value of the new jewellery**, not the net after exchange. The old gold is recorded as a separate purchase voucher and set off as a payment ([TaxAJ](https://www.taxaj.com/learn/?p=91066)). Some consumer blogs claim only the difference is taxed ([Bajaj Finserv](https://www.bajajfinserv.in/insights/4-ways-gst-affects-gold-jewellery)). The margin scheme for resale of second-hand jewellery has a favourable Karnataka AAR ruling, while the Kerala AAR and a later Karnataka AAR on melted gold went against it ([TaxGuru](https://taxguru.in/goods-and-service-tax/gst-sale-second-hand-gold-jewellery-purchased-individuals.html)). It should only ever be an opt-in mode with a warning. Chapter 71 goods are exempt from the national e-way bill, but states can require intra-state e-way bills under Rule 138F. Kerala has done so since 20 Jan 2025 for consignments of ₹10 lakh or more ([Taxscan](https://www.taxscan.in/kerala-sgst-dept-notifies-e-way-bill-mandatory-for-intra-state-movement-of-gold-and-precious-stones/486687)).

**PAN, cash and SFT.** PAN or Aadhaar KYC is required only for purchases of **₹2 lakh or more** ([Deccan Herald](https://www.deccanherald.com/business/no-new-kyc-disclosure-for-jewellery-purchase-kyc-only-for-high-value-cash-purchases-revenue-department-936757.html)). Receiving **₹2 lakh or more in cash from one person in a day, for one transaction, or for one event** is barred, with a **100% penalty**. Since 1 April 2026 this rule sits in s.186 of the Income-tax Act, 2025 (formerly s.269ST), and the penalty in clause 451 ([TaxHeal](https://www.taxheal.com/new-cash-transaction-limits-under-the-income-tax-act-2025.html); [eFileTax](https://www.efiletax.in/blog/clause-451-vs-section-271da-new-penalty-rules-for-cash-transactions)). One blog cites s.221 instead, which is an outlier. The cap covers all three tests, so a shop cannot get around it by splitting a bill, so the app must add up cash across bills, advances and scheme instalments for the same customer and day. Cash receipts above ₹2 lakh are SFT-reportable ([Business Standard/CBDT](https://business-standard.com/article/government-press-release/reporting-cash-transactions-under-rule-114e-of-income-tax-rules-1962-116122300464_1.html)). The 2026 Rules reportedly move this to Form 165, which is unverified ([HYDICAI](https://hydicai.org/assets/uploads/newsletters/84a5e3e3-7031-4a22-b270-9dfff93219dc.pdf)). TCS on cash jewellery sales existed only from June 2016 to March 2017, so no TCS logic is needed ([TaxHeal](https://taxheal.com/no-tcs-sale-jewellery-w-e-f-01-04-2017.html)).

**PMLA.** Since 28 Dec 2020, dealers in precious metals and stones are reporting entities for **cash transactions of ₹10 lakh or more, in a single operation or several linked ones**, and must keep transaction records ([RSM India](https://www.rsm.global/india/sites/default/files/media/RSM%20India/Publications/2021/rsm_india_newsflash_-_record_of_cash_transactions_in_gems_jewellery_over_and_above_rs._10_lakhs_mandatory_under_pmla.pdf)). The ₹2 lakh KYC limit does not override this trigger ([CalcGuru](https://calcguru.in/?p=4955)). One vendor frames the threshold as per customer per financial year ([Innoviti](https://innoviti.com/blogs/payment-faqs-for-jewellery-and-gold-retail-business-owners-in-india/)). The safest build flags both a monthly and a yearly per-customer cash total.

**Girvi licensing.** Pledge lending falls under state Pawn Brokers and Money-Lenders Acts. These require a licence for each shop, a register, and in Karnataka a security deposit and the lender's name displayed over the shop. Karnataka caps interest at **18% a year** ([Deccan Herald](https://www.deccanherald.com/india/karnataka/bengaluru/private-lenders-can-levy-interest-up-to-18-yearly-2965830); [Karnataka Pawn Brokers Act](https://www.indiacode.nic.in/bitstream/123456789/7163/1/13%20of%201962%20%28E%29.pdf)). Maharashtra requires licence and interest-rate display outside the premises ([Hindustan Times](https://www.magzter.com/es/stories/newspaper/Hindustan-Times-Mumbai/FARMERS-FIRST-RULES-FRAMED-TO-CHECK-WILY-LOAN-SHARKS)). Caps for other states come only from blogs and are unverified. The girvi module therefore needs per-state settings for licence number and maximum rate, and it must refuse or warn when a loan's rate goes above the cap.

**Weighing scales.** Trade scales must be verified and stamped under the Legal Metrology Act. A scale can be used to bill to **0.001 g only if its verification interval e equals its display division d**; if e = 10d, only to 0.01 g ([Mettler-Toledo](https://www.mt.com/in/en/home/applications/Laboratory_weighing/legal-for-trade.html)). Indian vendors sell stamped 10 mg counter scales and 1 mg balances with RS-232 output, and say stamping is valid for one year ([Hindustan Scale](https://www.hindustanscale.com/product/jewellery-weighing-scale-jewe2000/)). Some iScale models are labelled "Not A Legal Scale for Commercial use" ([iScale](https://iscaleindia.com/?p=12052)). No official Legal Metrology text was read. The app should keep a scale master with stamping expiry and a reminder, and round billed weight to each scale's verification interval.

**Consumer rights.** Beyond the BIS bill lines and Rule 49 compensation, the research found no mandatory central rule on disclosing buy-back or exchange terms. Printing the shop's exchange policy in the bill footer is good practice.

## Stock runs on two ledgers: tagged pieces and fine grams

Indian jewellery stock is two books kept side by side. Finished ornaments are **tagged one at a time**. Each tag holds gross, stone and net weight, purity, design, making, HUID and a barcode, and the tag is what staff scan at billing and stock check ([myBillBook](https://mybillbook.in/blog/choose-jewellery-billing-software-inventory-management/)). Loose metal (bullion, old gold, scrap, karigar balances) is kept **by weight converted to 24K-equivalent fine grams**. For example, 1,000 g of 22K scrap is 916.6 g fine ([Terra Insight](https://www.terra-insight.com/insights/wastage-loss-gold-jewellery-manufacturing-inventory-reconciliation)). Stock is tallied by counter or tray, checking both piece count and total gross weight ([JSoft](https://alphaebarcode.com/jsoft-extreme-wholesale-erp); [BUSY](https://busy.in/gems-and-jewellery/)). Grocery Khata's current stock is quantity-based, so it needs a new `Tag` entity with a status lifecycle (in stock, on approval, sold, melted, returned) and a `MetalAccount` keyed by metal, purity and location. Every tag status change must also post a weight movement, so the gram-wise summary always equals the sum of the tags. Small items sold from a box, such as studs or chains by weight, need a lot record holding total pieces and total weight.

The main theft control is the **approval (memo) challan**. A piece sent out to a customer or agent leaves saleable stock but stays the shop's property, and when it comes back it is re-weighed. Opening and closing tray counts complete the picture ([Marg help](https://care.margcompusoft.com/margerp/jewellery/1859/1/how-to-create-approval-entry-in-jewellery-setup-of-marg-software); [DEV Community](https://dev.to/jewellery_software/how-does-the-system-manage-approval-items-returned-late-or-damaged-697)). An append-only movement log, recording user and time for every tag move, should sit under all of this.

### Tags, printers and RFID fit the existing barcode work

Jewellery tags are small thermal-transfer **dumbbell (barbell) labels**. They carry the tag number, a barcode or QR code, gross and net weight, and purity, and usually no price, because the rate changes daily. They print on TSC TE244 or TTP-244 Pro printers, which cost about **₹10,300–12,000 on IndiaMART** ([IndiaMART](https://m.indiamart.com/dhitsolutions/barcode-printer.html)), or on Zebra models. The barbell gap means label height, gap and offset must be configurable ([Zebra](https://support.zebra.com/cpws/docs/eltron/epl_jewel.htm)). A QR code fits small tags better than Code128. RFID tags (UHF Gen2) and RFID trays are used by chains such as SreeThangam and Bhima for whole-tray counts and exit alarms ([IndiaRetailing](https://www.indiaretailing.com/?p=167159); [RFID Journal](https://www.rfidjournal.com/?p=81856)). No source shows RFID adoption in small shops, so v1 should store an optional EPC field and leave out a bulk-reconcile screen.

### The scale link is the biggest time-saver at the counter

Counter scales read to 0.01 g, and 0.001 g balances are used for stones and small items. Stamped Indian models from Hindustan Scale and iScale output weight over **RS-232 or USB** ([Hindustan Scale](https://www.hindustanscale.com/); [UP Scales](https://upscales.buyweighingmachine.com/blog/jewelry-gold-weighing-scales-0001g-accuracy-for-precious-metals-up-scales-delhi-ncr)). Vyapar and Omunim both advertise reading weight from the scale straight into the bill ([Vyapar](https://vyaparapp.in/free/inventory-management-software/jewellery-app); [Omunim](https://omunim.com/)). On computer, Grocery Khata can use the Web Serial API in Chrome or a small bridge app. On phone, Bluetooth scales are the route. Each weight entry should store whether it came from the scale or was typed, plus the scale ID, for audit. There is no public spec for scale serial output formats, which vary by model, so each supported model will need its own parser.

### Karigar job work is a fine-gold loan account

Each karigar has a running fine-metal account. The shop issues metal by weight and purity against a job card. The karigar returns finished pieces plus scrap, which are weighed and tested. The difference is set against an agreed wastage allowance, and labour is paid per gram or per piece ([Fyond](https://fyond.com/blog/best-jewellery-shop-software-india-buyers-guide); [Tally](https://tallysolutions.com/accounting-software/jewellery/)). Industry wastage bands run from **1–2% for machine-made chains to 6–8% for kundan or hand-set work**, and melting loss runs **0.3–0.7%**. No BIS or CBIC norm sets these. Reconciliation follows: input = output + wastage + melting loss + retention, and a gap above about **0.3–0.5% of input** gets flagged ([Terra Insight](https://www.terra-insight.com/insights/wastage-loss-gold-jewellery-manufacturing-inventory-reconciliation)). Indicative labour is ₹200–400/g for simple work and ₹800–1,500+/g for intricate work ([Joolet](https://joolet.com/?p=625)). A worked settlement, using the research team's own illustrative numbers: the karigar is issued 100.000 g at 99.5 (99.500 g fine) and returns 104.000 g at 91.6 touch (95.264 g fine). A 3% allowance on 104 g gives 3.120 g, so the karigar owes **1.116 g fine**. One unverified convention sits in this example. The allowance is applied to gross returned weight and then subtracted as if it were fine weight. Some shops apply it to fine weight instead (2.858 g fine here), which would leave 1.378 g owed. Local "touch" settlement customs were not documented, so the allowance basis must be a per-karigar setting. On GST, karigar inputs must come back within **one year** under s.143 CGST, and ITC-04 has a loss column ([Masters India](https://www.mastersindia.co/gst/gst-on-gold/)). That means the app needs a job-work challan register with return-due alerts.

Each tagged piece can also hold diamond and stone lines: type, natural or lab-grown, count, carat, shape, colour, clarity, cut, certificate lab and number, and rate per carat. The lab-grown flag matters because IGI grades both natural and lab-grown stones ([IGI](https://www.igi.org/reports/jewelry-reports/); [DEV Community](https://dev.to/jewellery_software/can-jewellery-software-manage-certifications-and-lab-reports-for-diamonds-and-gemstones-6m3)).

First, Vyapar's pages claim karigar management, HUID and karat pricing, while Codingclave (a custom-build vendor, so biased, and possibly out of date) says they are missing or manual. The fair reading is that Vyapar covers a basic retail jewellery bill, with the depth of those claims unverified. Second, Vyapar's stated **3% on gold plus 5% on making** split goes against the CBIC FAQ for a retail sale. A Grocery Khata bill that applies 3% to the whole value is both more compliant and cheaper for the customer, as Example A shows.

User complaints about dedicated ERPs are consistent: slow support, AMC fees, dated Windows interfaces, weak mobile apps, long training (4–10 weeks for WinGold and GoFrugal), and cloud tools with no reliable offline mode ([SoftwareSuggest](https://www.softwaresuggest.com/jewellery-software); [Codingclave](https://codingclave.com/guides/best-jewelry-shop-software-india-2026)). Omunim already offers Hindi and Gujarati, and SthirApp offers 9+ languages with offline-first sync ([Omunim](https://omunim.com/); [SthirApp](https://sthirapp.com/)). Language alone is therefore not an edge. What sets Grocery Khata apart is the combination: one app across trades, khata and WhatsApp already in place, jewellery-correct GST, transparent low pricing, and girvi, schemes and karigar in the same customer book.

### Points flagged as conflicting or unverified

The CBIC FAQ was quoted second-hand, not read directly. The 3%-on-full-value rule is the best-supported position, but GST on making for customer-supplied gold (one TaxGuru author argues 18%) and on repairs (18%) needs a CA's sign-off. GST on exchange (full value versus net) and margin-scheme eligibility are contested. The BIS district count changed three times in 2026 (380, 392, 398). The Income-tax Act 2025 section numbers (s.186, clause 451, Form 165) come from secondary sites, with one outlier citing s.221. The PMLA threshold framing (linked or monthly versus per financial year) differs between sources. FIU-IND reporting due dates and record-retention periods (commonly said to be 5 years) were not verified. State girvi interest caps beyond Karnataka's 18% are unverified. Annual scale re-stamping comes from vendor pages only. Composition-scheme eligibility for jewellers, the e-invoice threshold, and the Companies (Acceptance of Deposits) limits on savings schemes (commonly reported as a 12-month maximum) were not researched. Every Vyapar and ERP feature claim is vendor-stated, and no hands-on demo or Play Store review was examined. The fixed-per-piece making charge, rounding conventions, typical old-gold deductions at small shops, and local touch-settlement customs all lack citable sources and should be checked through field interviews.

## Conclusion

The product decision that matters most is the data model, not the screens. If the tag-plus-fine-gram ledger is built properly in Phase 1, girvi, schemes, karigar and old gold all become rupee-and-gram postings to the same customer book. That is a unified khata which neither Vyapar (shallow on jewellery) nor desktop ERPs (siloed modules, AMC-driven) offer cleanly today. A rupee-only model would need rebuilding when Phase 3 arrives. A second, less obvious point is that compliance is the cheapest way to stand out. A bill that taxes making at 3% instead of 5%, blocks a split cash bill at ₹2 lakh, and warns about a missing HUID using a district list that changed as recently as November 2026 makes "correct by default" a selling point that matters most to the sub-₹40 lakh shops that Vyapar and myBillBook currently serve.

Several regulatory points are still moving: districts, income-tax section numbers, state e-way bill rules and girvi caps. Grocery Khata should therefore treat its jewellery rules as server-side configuration that it can update without an app release, and should budget for a CA review and five to ten shop interviews (covering touch practice, deductions, rounding and girvi customs) before Phase 2 is finalised.
