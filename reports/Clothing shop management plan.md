# Build clothing on styles, sizes and per-piece GST

Grocery Khata should support a full-range clothing shop by adding one structural change, a **style-to-variant model (one style, many size x colour SKUs, each with its own barcode, MRP and stock)**, and then building counter billing that works out GST per piece automatically. Today `Clothes` is already a shop type, but it gets only **8 generic starter items, no clothes master list, no size or colour fields, no bill discounts, no barcode scanning into the bill, and a fixed GST rate per product** (code audit: `backend/src/GroceryKhata.Api/Catalogue/Product.cs`, `Catalogue/StarterItems.cs`, `Ledger/LedgerEntities.cs`). That fixed rate is now a compliance risk. Since 22 September 2025, apparel is taxed at **5% when a piece sells for up to Rs 2,500 before GST, and 18% above that**, and the test runs on each piece's value after discount, so one bill often carries two rates ([Upstox](https://upstox.com/news/business-news/tax/gst-rejig-garments-priced-above-2-5-k-to-be-costlier-will-hurt-middle-class-affordability-say-industry-bodies/article-180738); [Accountune](https://accountune.com/gst-on-garments-above-2500)). Competitors split into cheap mobile apps that claim variants but do little beyond them, desktop suites with deep reports, and chain ERPs sold through dealers. That leaves room for a low-cost, **offline-first, phone-based garment product** that adds things few rivals advertise: per-piece GST, discreet billing for innerwear and bridal nightwear, exchange in one screen, salesperson tracking, size-ratio purchasing and season clearance. The rest of this report covers how to organise the catalogue, where competitors stand, which features to build, and how to make the bill accurate and hard to tamper with.

## Organise by department, then tag occasion, season and brand

A shop that sells everything from baby rompers to bridal sets needs a short tree and rich tags, not a deep tree. Third-party guides to Myntra show the same groups again and again: Women's Ethnic (Sarees, Kurtas & Suits, Kurtis, Lehengas), Western wear, Lingerie/Sleepwear and Plus size. Sarees are sub-grouped by fabric or occasion ([Barkha Boutique](https://barkhaboutique.com/guides/best-ethnic-wear-brands-myntra)). Myntra reports "occasion wear" as a growth segment it tracks ([Apparel Resources](https://apparelresources.com/business-news/retail/myntra-reports-50-growth-in-womens-occasion-wear-demand/)). Meesho lists kids' ethnic items such as baby-girl sarees and lehenga cholis ([Meesho](https://www.meesho.com/kids-lehenga-choli/p/443a0j)). The design should therefore have **three departments (Women, Men, Kids)**, with **Occasion (Daily, Party, Festive, Bridal/Honeymoon), Season (Summer, Winter, Monsoon, All-season) and Brand as filterable tags**. Treating "Winter" or "Branded" as a branch would split one jacket across two places.

| Department | Categories (sub-categories) |
|---|---|
| Women | Ethnic (saree, kurti, salwar suit/dress material, lehenga, dupatta, blouse); Western (tops, dresses, jeans, leggings/palazzo); Innerwear (bras, panties, slips/camisoles, shapewear); Nightwear (nighties, night suits, bridal/honeymoon sets); Winter (shawls, cardigans, sweaters, thermals) |
| Men | Topwear (shirts, T-shirts, kurtas); Bottomwear (jeans, trousers, track pants, shorts, pyjama/dhoti); Ethnic (kurta-pyjama, sherwani); Innerwear (briefs, trunks, vests); Winter (jackets, sweaters, thermals) |
| Kids | Baby 0-2 y (girl, boy, unisex: rompers, jhabla, sets); Girls 2-16 y; Boys 2-16 y; Kids ethnic |

Sizes need **one size scale per category, not one global list**. Indian bra charts combine a band with a cup (32A-D, 34A-D, 36A-D), and sister sizes such as 32C, 34B and 36A fit differently ([Shyaway](https://www.shyaway.com/bra-size-chart-by-experts/)). Men's briefs are sized by waist in centimetres, with Jockey listings showing ranges like "70-75" and "90-95" ([Flipkart Jockey](https://www.flipkart.com/jockey-men-brief/p/itm36e5923b67056)). Baby clothes run in month bands, where 0-3M is roughly 3.5-5.5 kg, and weight is a better guide than age ([Luvlap](https://www.luvlap.com/blogs/kids-fashion/baby-clothing-size-chart-india)). A saree is about 5.5 m with a blouse piece of about 80 cm ([Utsav Fashion](https://help.utsavfashion.com/support/solutions/articles/14000005655-what-are-the-fabric-dimensions-of-saree-salwar-lehenga-)). The seed scales should be: alpha XS-4XL; waist 28-44 in; kids 0-3M through 15-16Y; bra 30B-40D, extendable; briefs S-XXL or cm ranges; and Free Size. Because sizing differs between brands, the size label belongs to each SKU and the app should not try to convert sizes across brands.

At item level, three more attributes are worth storing: **fabric, pattern and construction (knitted or woven)**. Construction matters because it sets the HSN chapter: chapter 61 is knitted garments, chapter 62 is woven, and heading 6212 covers brassieres ([Taxclue 6212](https://taxclue.in/hsn-code/6212); [Eximpe](https://eximpe.com/hsncode-finder/6212)). The clothes master list (the earlier *Master item list per trade* work calls for roughly 300-600 rows) should carry these attributes and a default HSN code. It can reuse the CSV loader that already serves the beauty, hardware, kirana, medical and paint lists. Two cautions. The finer HSN headings in the notes come from general HS knowledge and are not checked one by one. Sarees are often classified as fabric, not garments, so a CA should confirm that before the list ships.

Season deserves real tooling, because winter stock is where shops lose money. After mild winters, chains including H&M, Uniqlo and Lifestyle started year-end clearance about two weeks early ([DFU Publications](https://dfupublications.com/index.php/news/apparel/apparel-retailers-launch-year-end-clearance-sales-two-weeks-ahead-of-schedule)), with discounts of up to 70% and margins down 6-8% ([Third Eyesight](https://www.thirdeyesight.in/tag/apparel-industry/)). Stock-age reports by season tag and a bulk "mark down this season" action turn that risk into a weekly decision for the owner, instead of a surprise in February.

## Rivals either stop at variants or hide depth behind dealers

The market has three tiers, and each leaves a gap. **Mobile-first SMB apps** advertise size and colour tracking. myBillBook markets S-XXL, colour, style and brand tracking, barcode and thermal printers, and seasonal reports. It costs **Rs 3,490-3,990 a year**, with loyalty only in Pro Max ([myBillBook](https://mybillbook.in/s/garment-shop-billing-software/)). Yet a public feature request on its own board asks for size and colour variants at item creation, which suggests the feature is not smooth ([myBillBook Featurebase](https://mybillbook.featurebase.app/p/feature-request-add-product-variants-size-and-color-while-creating)). Vyapar tracks each size and colour as its own stock entry and bills offline on Android and Windows ([Vyapar](https://vyaparapp.in/pos-software/clothing)). Its Capterra reviews (4.3/5, 181 reviews) complain about data that disappeared at year end, unresolved barcode printing problems, and too much clicking at the counter ([Capterra](https://www.capterra.com/p/180579/Vyapar/reviews/)). Accountune, from **Rs 799 a year**, says it has a native size/colour matrix and automatic 5%/18% GST per piece, but its own page admits it is **cloud-only with no offline mode** ([Accountune](https://www.accountune.com/best-billing-software-for-garment-shop)). Zoho's Zakya starts at Rs 649 a month ([Capterra Zakya](https://www.capterra.in/software/1063821/zakya)).

**Desktop suites** have depth but are heavy to run. Busy's boutique edition covers size, colour, shade and design number, label printing, festive and clearance schemes, and reports on size-wise stock, slow movers, season sales and stock ageing. It controls price overrides through user permissions, but it lists no salesman module and publishes no prices ([Busy](https://busy.in/accounting-software/retail-shop/boutique/)). Marg gets size-wise stock by renaming its "Batch" field to "Size", which is a workaround, not a matrix ([Marg Care](https://care.margcompusoft.com/margerp/size/45865/1/How-to-enable-Size-wise)). **Chain ERPs** such as Ginesys and LOGIC ERP offer native style-size-colour models, sell-through analytics and loyalty, on undisclosed, dealer-quoted subscriptions ([Ginesys](https://ginesys.in/blog/ginesys-vs-gofrugal-fashion-retail-erp); [LOGIC ERP](https://www.logicerp.com/blog/top-apparel-retail-store-brands-streamlining-garment-billing-with-logic-erp-garment-software/)).

One pattern runs across all three tiers. Size-ratio purchasing, sale-or-return consignment from brands, alteration tickets, salesperson commission and buy-X-get-Y schemes are **rarely documented publicly by any Indian SMB product**. Even LOGIC ERP's garment page does not mention them. Keep in mind that most of this evidence comes from vendor marketing, much of it written by Accountune about its rivals, so these features may exist in demos without being advertised. Even so, for an owner choosing an app on a phone, a feature nobody shows is a feature the owner does not know about.

## Build ten features, led by the variant matrix and the smart bill

Grocery Khata already has strengths that garment rivals lack or charge extra for. It has an offline outbox (recent commits queue order actions and supplier bills while offline), per-line GST snapshots with CGST/SGST/IGST, credit notes numbered CN/FY/nnnn with restocking, supplier purchases and returns, a stocktake screen, and a salon StylistPay module whose logic could serve salesperson commission (code audit). The plan below builds on those strengths, in rough order of value.

| # | Feature | What it fixes | Effort (audit estimate) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Style -> variant model: size x colour grid, per-variant SKU, barcode, MRP, stock; bulk grid create | A shirt in 5 sizes x 3 colours today means 15 hand-made products | Large |
| 2 | Clothes master list (300-600 items, with dept/category/HSN/construction and size-scale defaults) | Only 8 starter items today | Medium |
| 3 | Per-piece GST rule (5% up to Rs 2,500 net of discount and tax, 18% above) applied at billing | Fixed product rate gives wrong tax | Small-medium |
| 4 | Barcode/camera scan into the bill, plus price-tag and label printing (MRP, size, code) | Billing today is by text search only | Small-medium + medium |
| 5 | Line and bill discounts (% or Rs), with a ceiling for each role | No bill discounts exist | Medium |
| 6 | One-screen exchange: return lines + new lines, net payable/refund | Today it is a credit note plus a separate bill, done by hand | Medium |
| 7 | Salesperson on each line, with a commission report (reuse StylistPay ideas) | No staff field on bills | Medium |
| 8 | Discreet "bill name" flag for innerwear and bridal/honeymoon nightwear | Privacy for sensitive items | Small |
| 9 | Size-ratio purchase entry (e.g. S:M:L:XL = 1:2:2:1) and size-wise stock and ageing reports | Purchases cannot record sizes today | Large (needs #1) |
| 10 | Season tags, stock-age alerts, bulk markdown; later consignment and alteration tickets | Winter dead stock | Medium |

Two of these features deserve more explanation. Size-ratio purchasing matches how the trade already places orders. An order of 100/200/200/100 units of S/M/L/XL is described as a 1:2:2:1 ratio ([Online Clothing Study](https://onlineclothingstudy.com/2018/05/what-is-size-ratio-in-fashion-industry.html)). Letting the owner type one ratio and a total, instead of filling in fifteen cells, would cut purchase entry from minutes to seconds. The discreet flag responds to how Indian lingerie is actually bought. Zivame grew largely because women preferred not to buy innerwear in male-staffed shops ([Business Standard/Reuters](https://www.business-standard.com/article/reuters/for-india-s-largest-lingerie-e-tailer-privacy-pays-115091700191_1.html)), and it advertises discreet packaging ([CashKaro](https://cashkaro.com/blog/zivame-grand-lingerie-festival/191755)). With the flag set, the bill prints a neutral name such as "Innerwear" or "Nightwear set" instead of "Honeymoon babydoll". The flag also hides item photos in the customer app and in WhatsApp shares, and marks delivery orders for opaque packing. The HSN code stays correct underneath. Whether a generic description that matches the HSN satisfies the invoice rules has not been checked against an authority, so a CA should confirm it.

The variant model is the critical path. Barcode billing, size-wise purchase, ageing reports and accurate exchanges all depend on it. It touches the schema, API, billing, stock, purchase, reports and the offline outbox. A practical order is to ship features 2, 3, 5 and 8 first on the current product model, since each is small or medium and fixes real accuracy problems now, while the variant model is built underneath.

## Make the bill compute tax per piece and leave an audit trail

Accuracy starts with the GST rule, because applying one rate to the whole bill is the most common error. The slab test uses each piece's **value before GST, after any discount shown on the invoice**. A Rs 2,600 tag that includes 5% tax is about Rs 2,476 before tax, so 5% applies. A Rs 3,000 piece with Rs 500 off is taxed at 5%, but with Rs 499 off it stays at 18% ([Accountune](https://accountune.com/gst-on-garments-above-2500); [Taxo.online](https://taxo.online/latest-articles/understanding-the-₹2500-slab-under-gst-2-0/)). In one worked example, a mixed Rs 9,600 bill should carry **Rs 1,104 of GST; charging 18% on the whole bill gives Rs 1,728, an overcharge of Rs 624** ([Accountune](https://accountune.com/gst-on-garments-above-2500)). The app should therefore follow a fixed sequence for every line. First, apply the line discount. Then share out any bill-level discount across lines in proportion to value. Then back out tax, because Grocery Khata already prices GST-inclusive and computes taxable value as Amount / (1 + rate). Finally, compare the result with Rs 2,500 and pick the rate. Note that the rate depends on the taxable value and the taxable value depends on the rate, so the check must be done carefully at the boundary. One older page (Busy.in) still describes the previous Rs 1,000 / 12% regime and should be ignored ([Busy.in](https://busy.in/gst-rates/garments)). Three points remain unsettled and need a CA: whether a bill-level discount spread across lines may legally move a piece into the lower slab, whether a pair or set counts as one piece, and the exact text of Notification 9/2025-CT(Rate), which the research saw only through secondary sources.

Buy-X-get-Y schemes need care, because the sources disagree. One says GST applies only to the paid item ([TaxTMI](https://www.taxtmi.com/article/detailed?id=8419)). Another says there is no GST on the free item ([Bajaj Finserv](https://bajajfinserv.in/gst-on-clothes)). A third treats the deal as a composite or mixed supply ([Bilimoria Mehta](https://www.bilimoriamehta.in/blogs/valuation-under-gst)). The most defensible design records the scheme as an invoice discount and spreads the price across all pieces, including the "free" one, in proportion to MRP. That leaves no zero-value lines and gives each piece a rate based on its share. Because spreading can pull pieces under Rs 2,500, the app should store the scheme rule with the bill.

MRP rules set a hard ceiling. MRP must be declared and includes all taxes. Garments sold loose need about four declarations, including MRP and size, from 1 January 2023 ([Lexplosion](https://lexplosion.in/garments-hosiery-products-sold-in-loose-or-open-form-are-exempted-from-compliances-under-legal-metrology-packaged-commodities-rules-subject-to-declaring-manufacturer-address-mrp-size-country-of/)). Selling above MRP is punishable under Section 36 of the Legal Metrology Act ([Lawx](https://lawx.in/acts/section/47/480/4622)). One unconfirmed source says the penalties changed to an improvement notice from May 2026 ([righttoinformation.wiki](https://righttoinformation.wiki/legal-metrology-2026-amendment-improvement-notice-ecommerce-penalty)). Today a line's unit price can be edited freely (`BillScreen.tsx`), so the bill should **block any price above MRP**, require a reason for any price cut, and require a manager PIN above a limit for each role. It should also keep a history of MRP changes, since many shops restickered prices after the GST cut.

Returns must link back to the original invoice line. A Section 34 credit note carries the **original invoice's rate and HSN** and must be reported by 30 November after the financial year of the sale. For FY 2025-26 sales, that deadline is 30 November 2026 ([TaxGuru](https://taxguru.in/goods-and-service-tax/section-34-understanding-credit-notes-gst.html)). After that date, only a commercial credit note is possible, which does not reduce output tax ([Tally](https://tallysolutions.com/gst/how-to-handle-gst-on-goods-returned-after-6-months/)). An exchange that crosses the Rs 2,500 line becomes a credit note at the old rate plus a new invoice at the new rate. A piece sold before 22 September 2025 at 12% must be credited at 12%. The one-screen exchange should handle all of this automatically. Grocery Khata's existing `CreditNoteLine.BillLineNo` already provides the link it needs.

The rest of the accountability layer follows standard retail practice. No statute requires these controls, and none of the research sources is a regulator. Sell by barcode so that MRP, HSN and rate come from the master record. Keep invoice numbers sequential per financial year, and make sure bills are cancelled, never deleted, with user, time and reason kept on record. Tag a salesperson on every line, both for commission and for reviewing returns. Flag discounts that land a piece just under Rs 2,500, because that pattern signals slab-gaming. Close each day with a Z report that compares expected and counted cash, UPI and card for each cashier. On the printed bill, show for each line the item, size, colour, HSN, quantity, MRP, discount, net amount and GST %. Below the lines, print a rate-wise tax table, the total savings, the payment split, the salesperson, the exchange policy (for example "exchange within 7 days with tag and bill") and a bill QR code for returns. Never add GST on top of an MRP-inclusive total. HSN is optional on B2C bills for shops with turnover up to Rs 5 crore, and must have 6 digits above that ([IRIS](https://irisgst.com/for-taxpayer-with-turnover-upto-rs-5-cr-mention-4-digit-hsn-code-and-for-turnover-above-rs-5-cr-mention-6-digit-hsn-code/)).

## Conclusion

For clothing, the hard part is not billing itself. It is that **a piece's tax rate depends on the price it actually sells at**, and in clothing that price moves constantly through discounts, schemes, exchanges and season markdowns. Accurate tax and an accurate catalogue (size, colour, MRP for each piece) are the same problem: once each physical piece is a scannable SKU and every price change goes through a rule, correct GST, clean returns and staff accountability largely follow. That makes the variant model and the per-piece GST engine one investment, not two.

Grocery Khata is well placed for this market. Its offline outbox, its GST-inclusive line snapshots, its credit notes linked to bill lines, and its staff-pay logic from salons address weaknesses competitors admit to: no offline mode in the case of Accountune, and lost data and clumsy counter flows in Vyapar's reviews. If Grocery Khata pairs those strengths with things rivals rarely show, such as discreet bills, ratio purchasing and season clearance, it can win shops that today choose between a cheap app and a dealer-sold ERP. Before launch, a CA should settle four open legal points: the notification text, discount spreading across slabs, sets and pairs, and saree classification.
