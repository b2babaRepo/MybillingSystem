# Make the exchange slip a counter-agnostic tender

The shopkeeper's idea is right, and it is how the better Indian retail POS products already work. When the return and the new purchase happen at the same counter, Grocery Khata should run **one exchange screen with one net settlement**. Behind that screen it should still save **two linked GST documents**: a credit note (CN) against the old bill and a fresh tax invoice for the new goods. When the return is taken at a returns desk and the shopping is billed somewhere else, the CN should print as a numbered **exchange slip**. Any counter then redeems that slip as a **payment mode**, after a server-side check that it is valid, unexpired, unused and tied to the right mobile number. Today the app keeps the credit only in one browser tab and applies it to the next bill on the same device. That breaks as soon as a second counter exists, and it gives no protection against a slip being used twice. The fix is to keep the CN balance on the server, give it a status, a mobile number, an expiry and a counter of issue, and redeem it in one atomic step. GST does not allow one "net" bill to stand in for both the return and the sale. Returned and new goods can also be at different rates, for example apparel at 5% against 18%. So the combined flow can only be combined on screen and on the printout. It cannot be one document.

## Big chains and Indian POS vendors already use "credit note as tender"

Two design families exist. Global cloud POS products run a single mixed cart. In Lightspeed, the old sale's items come back as negative lines, the replacement is added as a positive line and the cashier presses Exchange ([Lightspeed](https://x-series-support.lightspeedhq.com/hc/articles/25534278666267)). Square nets both sides and calls an equal-value swap an "even exchange" ([Square](https://squareup.com/help/us/en/article/6350-process-a-return-or-exchange-with-square)). Shopify POS shows Refund, Collect or Exchange depending on the balance ([Shopify](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/sell-in-person/shopify-pos/order-management/exchange-refund)). Indian accounting tools take the other route and keep two vouchers. TallyPrime records a credit note and adjusts it against a new sales invoice ([Tally](https://help.tallysolutions.com/tally-prime/sales-process/sales-return-tally/)). Zoho Inventory chains a sales return, a credit note and a new invoice, then applies the credit ([Zoho](https://www.zoho.com/inventory/kb/sales-returns/product-replacement.html)).

Indian retail POS vendors combine the two approaches, and they are the closest model for Grocery Khata. Ginesys supports "Return against Bill" inside the POS, and it also lets the cashier pick a credit note as the **mode of payment** on any new bill. That credit note has its own numbering series and **mandatory customer tagging** ([Ginesys](https://ginesys.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/GPOS/pages/3840017716/How+To+Take+payment+using+a+Credit+Note)). It lists only credit notes that are valid, unexpired and not voided. It requires an **OTP sent to the mobile number recorded on the note** ([Ginesys OTP](https://www.ginesys.in/blog/credit-note-becomes-more-secured-with-ginesys-otp)), and it can centralise notes so they work at any store ([Ginesys centralised CN](https://www.ginesys.in/blog/ginesys-retail-erp-now-attributed-with-centralized-credit-note)). GoFrugal creates credit notes automatically on return, posts them to accounts, puts the stock back at once, and issues "Return Redemption Notes" that can be adjusted against future bills ([GoFrugal](https://www.gofrugal.com/retail/billing-software/customer-return-process-retail.html)). The same GoFrugal page argues that returns slow down billing queues, and that is the operational reason a separate returns counter exists. (One research note reads it as recommending dedicated return counters, another only as a warning about queues. Either way, the cross-counter slip follows from it.)

Big Indian apparel chains give **store credit, not cash**, and print the validity on the note. Max Fashion's note works at any Max store for **90 days** ([Max](https://helpin.maxfashion.com/support/solutions/articles/9000209608-how-can-i-exchange-a-product-)). Westside's works only at Westside stores for **6 months** ([Westside](https://westside.com/pages/return-policy)). Reliance Trends complaints describe notes refused at other stores, or lapsing after about three months ([consumercomplaints.in](https://www.consumercomplaints.in/reliance-trends-credit-note-received-from-reliance-trends-not-being-used-c2933831)). No chain publishes its inside-the-store SOP, so the exact desk-to-counter handoff is inferred rather than documented. Shopify's own community shows what goes wrong when leftover credit is handled loosely: a leftover balance reset to zero and had to be re-entered by hand ([Shopify Community](https://community.shopify.com/t/shopify-pos-exchange-issue-remaining-store-credit-balance-resets-to-zero-after-discount/621900)).

## GST requires a credit note plus a full new invoice, settled net

Section 34 allows a credit note when goods are returned, and it must quote the original invoice ([CBIC s.34](https://taxinformation.cbic.gov.in/content/html/tax_repository/gst/acts/2017_CGST_act/active/chapter7/section34_v1.00.html); [TaxGuru](https://taxguru.in/?p=733586)). The new goods need their own tax invoice under the general invoicing rules. The research found **no provision or CBIC clarification that allows a single netted exchange bill**. Tally, Zoho, Vyapar and Busy all model the exchange as two documents ([Zoho Books](https://www.zoho.com/in/books/gst/credit-note.html); [Busy](https://busy.in/gst/guide-to-a-credit-note-under-gst.md)). Rates make this unavoidable. Since 22 Sep 2025 apparel is **5% up to Rs 2,500 per piece and 18% above** ([Accountune](https://accountune.com/gst-on-garments-above-2500)). A credit note keeps the rate of the original invoice even after a rate change ([Tally](https://tallysolutions.com/smart-moves/expert-insights/gst-credit-debit-notes-rate-change-scenarios/)). So a Rs 2,000 shirt returned for a Rs 3,000 jacket gives a 5% CN and an 18% invoice, and no single "difference" line can be taxed correctly.

Timing and reporting add three constraints. A GST credit note can reduce output tax only if it is issued by **30 November after the end of the financial year**, or by the annual return if that is earlier. For FY 2025-26 that date is 30 Nov 2026 ([Pice](https://piceapp.com/blogs/time-limit-for-amendment-in-gstr-1/); [TaxGuru](https://taxguru.in/?p=1047226)). After that date any credit is only a commercial "financial" credit note ([Busy](https://busy.in/gst/guide-to-a-credit-note-under-gst.md)). B2C credit notes are netted into GSTR-1 Table 7 by rate and place of supply ([WB FAQ](https://comtax.wb.gov.in/GST/GST_FAQ/Credit_Note_in_GST.pdf)), so every CN line must keep its rate, HSN and place of supply. The slip itself should not be taxed. Since Circular 243/37/2024 vouchers are actionable claims, and GST arises only on the goods supplied when the voucher is used ([GST Council](https://gstcouncil.gov.in/sites/default/files/2025-01/circular-no-243-2024.pdf)). The cleanest reading treats the CN as the GST document and the slip as a customer-facing claim on it. Spending the slip is just a tender against a full-GST invoice. That reading is an inference: no source rules directly on retail exchange slips. Composition and unregistered shops cannot issue tax credit notes and should get a plain "return note" instead. Whether a same-item, same-size swap is a non-taxable replacement is unconfirmed.

## Recommended design: one flow at the same counter, a slip across counters

**Same counter, combined flow.** The cashier finds the old bill (the existing "Find a bill" screen), ticks the items being returned with a reason, and adds new items in the same screen. The app shows one summary: returned value, new bill value, and **net to collect, refund or keep as slip**. On Save, the server writes the CN against the old bill, writes the new invoice, applies the CN to it as a tender, puts the returned stock back, and prints a single slip with both sections and one net line. There is no separate "exchange step" and no second entry of customer details. If the new goods cost less, the shop's setting decides whether the leftover is refunded or issued as a new slip (credit-only is the safer default, following Max and Westside). The leftover must never silently vanish.

**Different counter, exchange slip.** At the returns desk the same screen ends at "Issue exchange slip" and no new items are added. The server creates the CN with a **status** (Open, Used, Expired, Void, Refunded), a **balance**, the **mobile number**, the **counter and user who issued it**, the original bill number and an **expiry date**. The printout shows a short slip number and a QR or barcode. At any billing counter, the cashier adds "Exchange slip" as a payment mode, scans or types the number, and the server checks it and locks it in one atomic transaction. This is the step that prevents the classic failure where "any store credit number can be entered" ([SAP KB](https://userapps.support.sap.com/sap/support/knowledge/en/1882033)), and it stops two counters spending the same slip at the same time. Optionally, for slips above a threshold, an OTP or a match on the last four digits of the mobile number confirms it, as Ginesys does.

**One-time use.** Spending a slip closes it. If the new bill uses less than the slip's value, the server closes the old slip and issues a fresh slip for the remainder with the same expiry. This keeps each paper slip single-use, so an old printout can never be presented again, and it avoids the confusion of a running balance printed on paper.

**Validity.** The default should be **90 days**, editable per shop, matching Max ([Max](https://helpin.maxfashion.com/support/solutions/articles/9000209608-how-can-i-exchange-a-product-)). The app should warn when a return falls after the GST credit-note cutoff. Lost slips are not reissued: a manager can void the old slip and issue a new one after the mobile number matches, which is the BFL control ([BFL KB](https://knowledge-bfleu.kapturecrm.com/article-detail.html?cont_id=97)). Expiry should post the leftover to "unclaimed exchange credit" in the books, with no GST effect.

**Controls.** Only roles the owner allows can issue slips or refund a CN to cash. Above a shop-set amount, a manager PIN is required. A daily register should list open, used and expired slips by counter and by user. Flag staff with unusually many returns, since that pattern is a known sign of insider fraud ([Red Stag](https://redstagfulfillment.com/return-fraud/)).

**What the bill screen asks.** Mobile number is **mandatory** on any CN or slip, because it is the binding key for lookup, OTP and lost-slip recovery, and Ginesys makes customer tagging mandatory for the same reason. When a slip is used, or the combined flow runs, the new bill should **pre-fill name and mobile from the CN and not ask again**. The cashier is asked only if the slip came from a different customer, which should show a warning. For B2B or GSTIN customers the GSTIN carries over as well.

## Conclusion

The real change is not adding screens. It is moving the credit out of the browser tab and into a server record that any counter can look up and close. Once the CN has a status, a balance, a mobile number and an expiry, both of the shopkeeper's flows come from the same mechanism. The same-counter exchange is a CN applied as a tender within one save. The cross-counter exchange is the same CN, printed and redeemed later. Refunds, multi-device shops and fraud controls then follow without special cases.

Two points need a CA's confirmation before launch: whether an even same-item swap can skip the new invoice, and the exact Rule 53 fields to print on the CN. Until then, always issuing two documents is the conservative default that the major Indian tools already follow.
