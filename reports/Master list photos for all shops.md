# Shoot the hard items, generate the easy ones

Grocery Khata should put one photo on each master-list item, owned or properly licensed by the platform. Every shop item picked from the master list should show that photo through a **live link**, and the shop should be able to replace it with a private override. The cheapest legal route for 3,000–4,000 items is a mix of sources. AI generation with human review covers generic loose and commodity goods (chana, rice, sugar, screws, hinges, bricks, a sand heap) for roughly **₹40,000 in generation fees** plus review time. An in-house or commissioned shoot covers spec-sensitive trade items where a wrong picture misleads the buyer: 10/20/40 mm aggregate, TMT sariya, binding wire gauge, pipe sizes and paint shades. Free stock (Unsplash, Pexels, Pixabay, Commons CC0/CC BY) fills gaps at zero cost if the app never offers the images for download. Paid stock is a poor fit. Standard licences are single-seat, and Extended licences at roughly $80 per image would cost **$160k–$400k** for the catalogue. Branded packs should never be AI-generated or shown against a generic item; they come from the shop's own photo, Open Food Facts (credited) or the brand. Every number and clause below is a starting point. The research found no measured AI acceptance rates for Indian trade items, no bulk shoot rates, and no lawyer-checked terms, and points marked as legal questions need an Indian IP lawyer.

## Five sources, but only two scale cleanly to 3,500 items

**Free stock is legal but thin on Indian trade goods.** Unsplash grants an "irrevocable, nonexclusive, worldwide copyright license" for commercial use with no attribution required. It forbids selling images without significant modification and compiling them "to replicate a similar or competing service" ([Unsplash License](https://unsplash.com/license)). Pixabay is similar, and it also bans commercial use of images showing recognisable trademarks or brands ([Pixabay](https://pixabay.com/service/license-summary/)). Pexels bans standalone distribution and competing services ([Pexels help](https://help.pexels.com/hc/de-de/articles/900005880463)). A thumbnail inside billing software, with no raw-download button and no browsable public image library, is normal product use and low risk. Whether a catalogue built mostly from Unsplash photos could count as a "competing service" is still a question for a lawyer. Wikimedia Commons works with visible credit. Prefer CC0 or CC BY, because CC BY-SA puts share-alike duties on edited images, and avoid NC licences entirely ([Commons reuse](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Commons:Reusing_content_outside_Wikimedia)). The free sites give no indemnity, and some uploaders do not own what they upload, so log the source URL, author, licence and date for every image.

**Paid stock is built for one buyer, not thousands of tenants.** iStock bars letting others "download, extract, or redistribute content as a standalone file". It requires an extended licence for multi-user server access and for templates distributed to others, and it forbids sublicensing ([iStock licence](https://www.istockphoto.com/legal/license-agreement)). Shutterstock Standard is a single-seat licence. Apps that embed its images for their own end users go through its API Platform licence ([Shutterstock comparison](https://www.shutterstock.com/license-comparison)). Adobe Stock Extended runs about $79.99 per asset ([Photutorial](https://photutorial.com/adobe-stock-licenses-explained)). Freepik is effectively ruled out: it bans putting content "in a database" and authorising "any third parties to use" it ([Freepik/Magnific terms](https://www.magnific.com/legal/terms-of-use)). Indian agencies price at Getty-like levels; one royalty-free Indian image lists at Rs 14,000 ([Getty India](https://www.gettyimages.in/detail/photo/indian-street-vendor-preparing-food-chapatti-flat-royalty-free-image/499781514)). Use paid stock only for a handful of hero images, and only with a written platform licence.

**Own shoot or a commissioned photographer gives the cleanest title.** A 40 cm LED light box costs Rs 2,299 ([Boxtudio](https://boxtudio.in/products/boxtudio®-lightbox-with-front-cover-tabletop-portable-photostudio-product-photography-light-tent-40x40x40cms)), and a 24-inch Neewer tent about ₹20,380 ([Tanotis](https://www.tanotis.com/products/neewer-tabletop-photo-studio-light-tent-24-x-24)). Indian studios quote **₹200–350 per product** for white-background shots ([Kenit Patel Photography](https://kenitpatelphotography.pages.dev/product-photography-ahmedabad); [IndiaMART](https://dir.indiamart.com/mumbai/product-photography.html)). Outsourcing all 3,500 items at those list rates comes to about ₹7–12 lakh, before the bulk discount that no source quantified. Background removal costs very little: Photoroom is about $0.02 per image ([Activepieces](https://www.activepieces.com/blog/photoroom-pricing-all-plans-and-costs-2026-guide)), and remove.bg falls to about $0.21 per credit in bulk ([remove.bg](https://remove.bg/zh/pricing)). A commissioned photographer must sign a written copyright assignment. Without one, the photographer owns the copyright by default.

**AI generation is cheapest, and its ownership is weakest.** List prices run about $0.02–$0.19 per image for OpenAI's gpt-image-1 ([PetaPixel](https://petapixel.com/2025/04/24/chatgpts-popular-new-image-generator-coming-to-adobe-figma-canva-and-more)) and $0.02–$0.06 for Imagen 4 on Vertex ([Wireflow](https://www.wireflow.ai/blog/imagen-4-pricing)). Check current model availability, because a snapshot noted a June 2026 discontinuation date. Adobe offers IP indemnity for select Firefly outputs to eligible enterprise customers ([Adobe](https://helpx.adobe.com/legal/product-descriptions/adobe-firefly.html)). Indian law assumes a human author, so purely AI images may not be protected by copyright ([S.S. Rana](https://ssrana.in/articles/who-owns-what-the-machine-makes-authorship-and-ownership-of-ai-generated-works-under-indian-law/)). Reform is under review, and the December 2025 DPIIT working paper is not law ([K&S Partners](https://ksandk.com/intellectual-property-rights/ai-generated-content-copyright-ownership-india/)). The practical result is that Grocery Khata can use AI images but may not be able to stop a rival copying them, which matters little for a photo of loose sugar.

|---|---|---|---|

## Which method suits which item comes down to whether size is the product

The dividing line is whether the picture carries a specification the buyer pays for. A heap of chana or a pile of bricks looks the same whatever the source. Generic loose kirana goods, plain hardware, bricks, a sand heap, brushes, rollers and salon tools are common in model training data. They should render well with AI, at 3–4 generations per kept image, and any image showing text or a logo should be rejected. No hands-on test confirmed this for Indian items, so run a 50-item pilot first.

Spec-sensitive items are different. In a 10 mm, 20 mm or 40 mm aggregate photo, the size grade is the whole product. A generated "20 mm" stone that is really 40 mm misleads the buyer. The same goes for TMT rib patterns, binding wire gauge, screw head and thread types, pipe diameters and paint shades. Photograph these. One afternoon at a building-materials yard covers aggregates, sand grades, bricks, sariya bundles and wire coils. Shoot a handful of each aggregate beside a ruler or coin, in a tray rather than a light tent, because bulky dusty goods do not fit tabletop kit. Add a size caption such as "20 mm" in the item name, not in the image.

Branded and regulated goods follow a third rule: never generate them. A made-up UltraTech bag or Asian Paints tin is misleading and a trademark risk. Pixabay explicitly bans commercial use of recognisable brands, and iStock marks many such images editorial-only ([iStock](https://www.istockphoto.com/legal/license-agreement)). A generic "Cement bag 50 kg" gets a plain unbranded bag photo. A branded SKU gets the shop's own photo, a brand or distributor image, or an Open Food Facts image for food. Medicines, FSSAI-labelled food and anything with an MRP on the label get a neutral category icon unless a real photo exists. Showing a brand's genuine pack shot to sell genuine goods is probably low risk, but no Indian case settles it, so get a lawyer's view.

## One standard image spec makes mixed sources look like one catalogue

Amazon's main-image rules are the de facto standard. They require a pure white background (RGB 255,255,255), and a non-compliant main image can get a listing suppressed ([Amazon](https://sellercentral.amazon.com/gp/help/external/G75PWC4THA8J269P)). Third-party guides add that the product should fill about 85% of the frame, with no text, logos or props, and at least 1000 px on the long side ([Photoroom guide](https://www.photoroom.com/blog/amazon-product-images)). Amazon's own pages do not confirm those figures. Indian ONDC buyer apps set a floor of 300×300 px and reject placeholders and logos ([MartPe](https://www.martpe.in/product-sorting-parameters)).

Grocery Khata's master standard should be a square 1:1 image on pure white, with the product centred and filling about 80–85% of the frame, a soft shadow at most, and no text, watermark, price sticker, hand or brand. Loose goods sit in a plain white or steel bowl, or as a heap. Keep a master source of 1000 px or more. Serve the 512 px and 320 px WebP renditions the app already makes, at about 15–30 KB and 6–15 KB for white-background shots. Every image, from any source, goes through the same background-removal and normalising step, which is what makes AI, stock and shoot photos look like one set. Keep one photo per pack size when packs look different, because a wrong pack size is the most likely wrong-photo complaint.

## Inheritance: live link, private override, content-addressed files

No Indian billing app documents how its item library shares photos. myBillBook advertises more than 1 lakh library items ([myBillBook](https://mybillbook.featurebase.app/en/help/articles/4381436-how-to-add-and-manage-inventory-in-mybillbook)), and Vyapar has an Items Library alongside per-shop store images ([Vyapar](https://vyaparapp.in/blog/vyapar-app-features/)), but neither is confirmed to include photos. Amazon shows both the upside and the trap of a shared image. One detail page means a fix reaches every seller at once. But when contributors compete, Amazon's system picks the image, and sellers routinely complain their images were overwritten ([Amazon detail page rules](https://sellercentral.amazon.com/help/hub/reference/external/G202024200); [hustlegotreal](https://hustlegotreal.com/faq/amazon/amazon-detail-page-changed-why-were-my-title-images-or-bullets-overwritten)). The lesson is curation. Only the admin sets the master photo, and shops can suggest but never overwrite.

The recommended design, which is a design inference rather than a documented precedent, works like this. The `MasterItem` gets a `master_photo_id`. A shop item created from the master list keeps `master_item_id` and an optional `shop_photo_id`. The image is resolved in this order: the shop override, then the master photo, then the category placeholder. No files are copied per shop, so 3,500 master photos serve any number of shops. Store image bytes once, keyed by content hash (for example `/img/{sha256}/{320|512}.webp`), and serve them with immutable one-year cache headers. When the admin replaces a master photo, the row points at a new hash, so caches never go stale. This follows Shopify's versioned `?v=` image URL idea ([Shopify image_url](https://shopify.dev/docs/api/liquid/filters/image_url)) and fits today's Postgres WebP storage. Keep a history row for each master-photo change (old hash, new hash, who, when, why) so a bad update can be rolled back in one click. Printed thermal bills carry no images. If PDF bills ever do, snapshot the hash at bill time. Each photo row also stores provenance: source type (shoot, commission, AI, Unsplash, OFF, shop), author or model, prompt, licence, source URL, reviewer and an internal `is_ai` flag.

Shop overrides stay private by default. A per-photo "Share with other shops" tick, which is off by default, lets a good shop photo go into an admin review queue as a candidate master photo. The admin, not the crowd, decides. This turns the user base into a slow, legal pipeline for regional items the platform never shot.

Wrong-photo reports already exist per shop item. Extend them with a reason (wrong product, wrong size or grade, wrong pack size, poor quality, brand or copyright complaint) and with routing. A report against a shop override goes to that shop. A report against an inherited master photo hides it at once for the reporting shop only, which falls back to the placeholder, and joins an admin queue ranked by how many distinct shops reported it. Admin review, or crossing a threshold, replaces or removes the photo for everyone. Run copyright and trademark complaints through a separate takedown address with a defined turnaround: remove the photo from the master list first, investigate afterwards, and log the action. The exact deadlines under India's IT Rules 2021 were not verified and need a lawyer.

## Terms of use need three clauses and a caption

Marketplaces take a broad, royalty-free, worldwide, non-exclusive licence over seller materials. Amazon's Business Solutions Agreement is the template ([BSA](https://abilityone.gov/laws,_regulations_and_policy/documents/Amazon%20Services%20Business%20Solutions%20Agreement.pdf)), and the licence runs to Amazon, not to other sellers ([Third Chair](https://usethirdchair.com/blog/amazon-copyright-rules-for-sellers-authors-and-brands)). Open Food Facts photos are CC BY-SA and require crediting contributors ([OFF licence tutorial](https://openfoodfacts.github.io/openfoodfacts-server/api/tutorials/license-be-on-the-legal-side)). The following draft wording has not been reviewed by a lawyer.

*Platform images:* "Grocery Khata grants you a non-exclusive, non-transferable, revocable licence to display master-list images only within your use of the Service, including your bills, catalogue, online store and WhatsApp shares, while your account is active. You may not extract, resell or redistribute the image files. Images are representative; the actual product may vary in size, colour, grade or packaging. Product names, logos and packaging remain the property of their owners. Some images are supplied under third-party licences (for example CC BY-SA from Open Food Facts) and carry their attribution."

*Shop uploads:* "You confirm you took the photo or have the right to upload it, and that it does not infringe anyone's rights. You keep ownership. You grant Grocery Khata a worldwide, royalty-free, non-exclusive licence to store, resize and display it for your shop."

*Opt-in sharing:* "If you tick 'Share this photo with other shops', you also grant Grocery Khata a perpetual, worldwide, royalty-free, sub-licensable licence to use, edit and display it in the master list for all shops. This licence survives deletion of your account, but you may ask us to remove the photo from the master list."

On screen, show "Representative image" under every master photo, show credits where a licence requires them (a detail-view line plus an Image credits page), and keep the AI flag internal unless rules require disclosure. Whether CCPA's 2022 misleading-advertisement guidelines or MeitY's draft synthetic-content labelling rules apply to AI product thumbnails was not verified.

## Conclusion

The cost problem turns out to be a sorting problem. Once items are split by whether the picture carries a specification, about 80% of the list can be generated or sourced free for well under ₹1 lakh. Shoot money then goes only to the few hundred items where a wrong photo actually misleads, and one yard visit plus one kirana-market day probably covers most of them. The live-link-with-override model means each photo is made once and improves for every shop at the same moment. The wrong-photo reports and opt-in sharing already in reach turn shopkeepers into the quality-control and long-tail supply that no stock library offers for Indian trade goods.

Do three things before scaling: a 50-item AI pilot across kirana, hardware and building items to measure real acceptance rates, a bulk quote from one or two commissioned studios, and one lawyer review covering the terms clauses, the "competing service" question for free stock, photographer assignments and AI labelling. Until those come back, treat the cost table as an order-of-magnitude guide, not a budget.
