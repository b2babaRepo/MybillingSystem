# Build the pharmacy shop on batches, not chatbots

Grocery Khata should launch a medical shop type whose core is deterministic and compliance-led: a batch-and-expiry stock layer (FEFO picking, strip/loose units, revised MRP per batch), a schedule flag on every item that blocks H/H1/X sales until prescriber and patient are filled, auto-built H1 and Schedule X registers kept at least 3 years with no hard deletes, GST per item (nil, 5%, 18%) computed back from MRP-inclusive prices, scheme-aware landed cost for true margin, and a distributor ledger that tracks expiry returns and credit notes. AI belongs on top of that layer, not inside it. The best-value, lowest-risk AI job is turning a photo or PDF of a distributor invoice into a draft purchase entry with batch, expiry, MRP, PTR and free quantity, which mainstream rivals mostly have not shipped well and which never touches a patient. Next come expiry-risk and dead-stock alerts, reorder drafts sent to distributors on WhatsApp after approval, Hindi voice questions over the shop's own numbers, and GST and H1 drafting. Handwritten prescription reading and drug-interaction warnings are the riskiest uses: the best published model reads Indian handwritten prescriptions at about 82% accuracy, and ChatGPT scored 30% on interaction questions, so these must stay draft-only, verified by the registered pharmacist, with interactions taken from a licensed database. The market opening is real: no competitor found combines pharmacy depth, udhaar with WhatsApp reminders and bill import for a single-counter chemist at under about ₹2,000 a year, mobile-first and in Hindi.

## Desktop ERPs are too heavy and mobile apps too shallow, leaving a single-counter gap

The chemist software market splits four ways. Desktop ERPs like Marg are deep but sold through dealers at roughly **₹8,100–₹25,200 one-time plus about ₹3,500/year AMC** ([Accountune](https://accountune.com/best-billing-software-medical-store-india)), and draw steady complaints: customer support rated **2.9 on Capterra** ([GetApp](https://www.getapp.com/sales-software/a/marg-erp-9/)), and one chemist called it "very very hard and slow to operate" ([Trustpilot](https://au.trustpilot.com/review/margbooks.com)). GoFrugal and Medeil target chains and hospitals. Cloud pharmacy SaaS such as eVitalRx claims **20,000+ pharmacists**, suggests generics by salt, integrates Tally and sells on ONDC and WhatsApp ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/id1637429769)). Generic mobile apps advertise batch and expiry and "Schedule H / H1 Record Ready" ([Vyapar](https://vyaparapp.in/free/billing-software-for-medical-shop)), but a competitor says Vyapar has no drug database and HSN must be typed by hand ([Accountune](https://accountune.com/best-billing-software-medical-store-india)). Ledger apps own udhaar: OkCredit names medical stores among its main users and pitches repeat-medicine credit with automatic reminders ([OkCredit](https://okcredit.in/khata-app-for-medical-store)).

Grocery Khata already holds the pieces those apps split apart: khata, GST billing, suppliers, Tally export and WhatsApp reminders, in Hindi. Adding pharmacy depth and self-serve onboarding with no dealer answers both the "too heavy" and "too shallow" complaints. Two outside forces shape the plan. Quick commerce and e-pharmacy are pressuring chemists, with a nationwide AIOCD strike on 20 May 2026 ([ETV Bharat](https://www.etvbharat.com/en/health/pharmacies-across-india-shut-may-20-epharmacy-concerns-enn26051305103)), yet platforms like Flipkart Minutes say they will fulfil through licensed local pharmacies ([Third Eyesight](https://www.thirdeyesight.in/?p=6611)), which creates demand for software that can accept those orders later. And as of 2026 India still has no notified e-pharmacy rules ([LSHTM](https://www.lshtm.ac.uk/media/102426)), so the app should record home deliveries with prescription image and pharmacist sign-off but never market itself as an e-pharmacy. One caution: vendor release notes for 2025–26 were not checked, so "no AI shipped" among incumbents is likely but unproven.

## Compliance and batches decide the data model before any feature

The pharmacy type cannot reuse the batch-less grocery stock model. Every stock line needs a batch with its own expiry, MRP and cost, and billing should pick the earliest-expiring batch first ([Accountune](https://www.accountune.com/pharmacy-billing-batch-expiry-guide)). Packs are bought by box or strip and sold by strip or tablet, so stock should be held in base units with pack conversion, as Vyapar and GoFrugal already do ([Vyapar](https://vyaparapp.in/free/billing-software-for-retail-shop/pharmacy)). Loose-tablet price is strip MRP divided by units per strip, and the app must block any price above batch MRP, because overcharging above MRP or the NPPA ceiling is recoverable from retailers with interest ([Vikaspedia](https://vikaspedia.in/health/nrhm/national-health-policies/faqs-on-drug-pricing)).

The item master needs a schedule flag (OTC, G, H, H1, X, C, NDPS), salt/composition, manufacturer, 8-digit HSN, GST rate, pack size, cold-chain flag and rack location. Schedule H, H1 and X drugs sell only on a registered practitioner's prescription; every H1 sale goes into a separate register with prescriber name and address, patient, drug and quantity, **kept for 3 years** and open to inspectors ([NHSRC](https://www.nhsrcindia.org/sites/default/files/2021-07/Attachment%20A%2C%20Schedule%20H1%20register%20and%20NDPS%20Forms.pdf)). Schedule X needs a duplicate prescription with one copy kept 2 years ([OkCredit blog](https://okcredit.in/blog/dos-and-donts-for-medical-store-owners/)). The schedule lists must be updatable from the server, because drugs move: **pregabalin entered H1 in May 2026** ([InsightsIAS](https://www.insightsonindia.com/2026/05/23/health-ministry-brings-pregabalin-under-schedule-h1/)). Enforcement is real; one Bhopal drive acted against 32 stores and suspended 3 licences ([Free Press Journal](https://www.freepressjournal.in/bhopal/action-against-32-medical-stores-licences-of-three-suspended-eight-registrations-cancelled)).

The shop profile stores the Form 20/21 licence numbers (printed on every bill) and the pharmacist's registration. Since 2017 retail licences do not expire but need a **retention fee every five years** ([calcguru](https://calcguru.in/?p=4879)); in Delhi, late payment adds 2% of the fee per month ([Delhi Drugs Control](https://drugs.delhi.gov.in/sites/default/files/2026-03/forms_and_fees_sale_licences.pdf)), so a reminder pays for itself. A later win is scanning the QR code that the top 300 brands must carry since 1 Aug 2023 under Schedule H2, which encodes batch, expiry and manufacturer ([CDSCO FAQ](https://cdsco.gov.in/opencms/resources/UploadCDSCOWeb/2018/UploadPublic_NoticesFiles/Final%20FAQs%20on%20QR%20code%2021.07.2023.pdf)); reports that this widened to vaccines, antimicrobials and cancer drugs in June 2026 are not yet checked against the gazette ([Daily Pioneer](https://www.dailypioneer.com/news/govt-expands-qr-code-tracking-to-key-drug-categories-to-curb-counterfeits)). Note that this section's legal points rest mostly on secondary sources; check each clause against the Drugs Rules, 1945 text before building, and the 2026 CCTV draft rule ([gktoday](https://www.gktoday.in/health-ministry-proposes-amendments-to-drugs-rules-1945-for-schedule-h-h1-and-x-drugs/)) remains unconfirmed.

| Service | What the app tracks | Key rule or report |
|---|---|---|
| Stock | Batch, expiry, MRP, revised MRP, cost, base units | FEFO pick; near-expiry at 30/60/90/180 days; breakage and count variance |
| Sales | Patient, doctor, batch per line, schedule | Block H/H1/X without Rx fields; Rx photo; no price above MRP |
| Registers | H1, Schedule X from sale lines | 3-year retention, no hard delete, daily sign-off |
| Purchases | PTR, free qty, discount, GST, distributor licence | Landed cost = (PTR × qty − discount) ÷ (qty + free) |
| Vendors | Ledger, due dates, expiry returns, credit notes | Credit-note ageing against the 60-day norm |
| Customers | Regular medicines, refill dates, udhaar | WhatsApp reminders with consent |
| Licences | Form 20/21, pharmacist, retention due | Reminder before 5-year retention date |

## Margins of 16–20% vanish fast, so profit must be scheme-aware and GST-exclusive

GST 2.0 changed the rate table on **22 Sep 2025**: most medicines went from 12% to **5%**, a notified list of life-saving drugs went to nil, and devices, kits, glucometers, thermometers, diapers and most toiletries also moved to 5% ([TaxGuru](https://taxguru.in/goods-and-service-tax/gst-2-0-impact-pharmaceutical-sector.html)). "HSN 3004 equals 5%" is not a safe rule, since specific drugs are nil and nicotine gum is reportedly 18% ([Busy](https://busy.in/gst-rates/medicines/)), so the rate must be stored per item and seeded from a curated list. Sources disagree on whether 33, 36 or 37 drugs are exempt, and post-2.0 rates for cosmetics and supplements (HSN 2106) are unverified, so a CA should sign off the seed list. Since prices are MRP-inclusive, taxable value is the selling price after discount divided by (1 + rate), using the rate on the sale date, not the purchase. The NPPA required the cut to be passed on even on old-MRP stock ([A2Z Taxcorp](https://a2ztaxcorp.net/gst-rate-cut-on-medicines-from-sept-22-no-need-for-re-labelling-prices-to-be-updated-via-revised-lists/)), which is why each batch needs a revised-MRP field. HSN rules matter too: up to ₹5 crore turnover, 4 digits are mandatory on B2B invoices; above it, 6 digits on all ([GST Council](https://www.gstcouncil.gov.in/sites/default/files/2024-05/notfctn-78-central-tax-english-2020.pdf)).

Margins are thin. Retailers earn about **20% of printed price** on brands ([Moneylife](https://moneylife.in/article/indian-pharma-largely-unaffected-by-proposed-caps-on-trade-margin-says-fitch-ratings/58822.html)), with 16% built into NPPA prices for scheduled drugs ([Lok Sabha](https://eparlib.nic.in/bitstream/123456789/597172/1/100323.pdf)); even listed chain MedPlus runs **26.1% gross but 4.7% EBITDA** ([AlphaStreet](https://alphastreet.com/india/medplus-health-q1-fy26-earnings-results)). Worked through at 5% GST, a strip with MRP 100 and PTR 76.19 ex-GST earns 9.52 at a 10% discount, but **zero at 20% off**; a 10+1 scheme lifts profit to 16.45. So profit must use effective landed cost after free goods, GST-exclusive, and the bill screen should warn when a discount eats the margin. Free scheme quantity is not a separate supply and its ITC is not blocked ([CBIC Circular 92](https://gstcouncil.gov.in/sites/default/files/2024-06/circular-cgst-92.pdf)): book free units into the batch and spread the cost.

Expiry handling closes the loop between stock, vendors and tax. Under CBIC Circular 72/46/2018 a chemist can return expired drugs either as a fresh supply or against the distributor's credit note ([TaxHeal](https://taxheal.com/circular-no-72-46-2018-gst-procedure-for-return-of-time-expired-drugs-medicines.html)); AIOCD has pushed for settlement within 60 days ([AIOCD letter](https://medicaldialogues.in/pdf_upload/issues-causing-concerns-to-trade-members-146429.pdf)), and retail returns commonly start about 6 months before expiry ([Terra Insight](https://www.terra-insight.com/insights/pharma-expiry-returns-saleable-non-saleable-india/)). The app should support both modes and show pending credit notes by age. Write-offs of destroyed stock are a P&L loss, with ITC reversal a CA question. The P&L should carry pharmacy heads: expiry and breakage, scheme income, pharmacist salary, cold-chain electricity, licence and retention fees, and MDR charges.

For filing, the export set is a GSTR-1 file (B2B invoice-wise for clinics with GSTIN, B2CS by rate, credit notes, HSN summary, nil-rated Table 8) and a 3B summary, plus Tally vouchers with ledgers split by rate, building on the existing Tally export. Composition (1%, ₹1.5 crore, no ITC) rarely beats regular registration at 5% ([Zoho](https://www.zoho.com/in/books/gst/faq/composition-scheme.html)) but should be supported with a bill of supply. For income tax, a digital-versus-cash receipts split drives the 44AD 6%/8% deemed profit and the ₹2 crore versus ₹3 crore limit ([ClearTax](https://cleartax.in/s/section-44ad-presumptive-scheme)), which cash-heavy chemists will hit. GST due dates were not verified from primary sources in this research.

## AI earns its keep on paperwork and stock, and stays out of dispensing

The ranking of AI uses follows one test: how much typing or loss it removes, against how close an error gets to a patient. **Distributor invoice to purchase entry** wins both ways. Invoices are printed and structured, but generic OCR drops exactly the pharma columns that matter, batch, expiry and pack ([invoicedataextraction.com](https://invoicedataextraction.com/blog/india-pharma-distributor-invoice-batch-expiry-extraction)). The design is a vision model that fills a strict schema (item, pack, batch, expiry, MRP, PTR, qty, free qty, discount, GST, HSN), then deterministic checks: lines sum to the total, MRP is at least PTR, expiry is in the future, GST matches HSN, plus fuzzy matching to the item master and per-distributor name mappings that learn. A review screen comes before posting. Marg and eVitalRx advertise bill reading, but no independent accuracy benchmark exists, so Grocery Khata should measure its own.

**Expiry-risk, dead-stock and reorder** come next. Flag a batch when days to expiry are fewer than the days needed to sell it, and suggest a return before the window closes; reorder points come from moving average times lead time plus safety stock. These are rules, not ML. The LLM explains the numbers and drafts each distributor's WhatsApp order, which the owner approves before sending. The funded example is Plazza, which raised **$15M** and claims fill rates **above 95% against a 50–60% norm** using AI inventory ([AI Market Watch](https://www.ai-market-watch.com/news/ai-pharmacy-startup-plazza-raises-15m-to-solve-indias-medicine-fill-rate-crisis-p1vb1z)), a company claim not independently verified. **Hindi voice and chat** ("aaj ka profit kitna") uses read-only tools over shop data, answering only from tool results, the same pattern Grocery Khata already uses. **GST and H1 drafting** generates returns and registers deterministically, while the LLM explains anomalies such as an HSN-rate mismatch or a purchase missing from 2B; the pharmacist signs the H1 register daily. Refill reminders and a WhatsApp order bot work too, but the bot must hand off to a human before confirming any prescription drug.

**Handwritten prescription reading** is where AI is most tempting and least safe. The MIRAGE study, using annotations from 1,133 doctors, reached **82%** when both drug name and dose had to be right, against 64–70% for earlier systems ([arXiv 2410.09729](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.09729v2)); that is about one line in five wrong, with sound-alike brands the main danger. Vendor claims of 99%+ have no published method ([Veryfi](https://www.veryfi.com/technology/turn-unstructured-medical-prescriptions-into-structured-data/)), and no independent benchmark of 2025–26 frontier models on Indian prescriptions was found. Ship it only as a draft beside the image, with per-line confidence and a mandatory pharmacist tick on every line. **Interaction warnings** need a licensed curated database: ChatGPT scored **30%** on community-pharmacist interaction questions ([Pharmacia 2024](https://doaj.org/article/40281e027505426fa2a54f73a5fd78a2)), and all tested models missed metoprolol–verapamil ([ERCSP 2024](https://doaj.org/article/e4f6c91c40cc4cddb8cf5736ea04cab5)). India has no official open drug API, and Kaggle salt datasets of 195k–250k rows have unclear licences ([Baselight/Kaggle](https://baselight.app/u/kaggle/dataset/shudhanshusingh_az_medicine_dataset_of_india)). Salt-based substitutes are feasible for OTC items or on request, but Rule 65(11A) bars swapping a prescribed scheduled drug for another preparation ([Medical Dialogues](https://business.medicaldialogues.in/coming-soon-pharmacists-may-be-allowed-to-substitute-branded-drugs-with-generics)), so every suggestion is labelled "for pharmacist decision" and logged.

The guardrails are architectural. Money, tax, interactions and expiry run in deterministic code; the model extracts, explains and drafts. The model gets narrow tools (read stock, create drafts) and no tool that sends a message or posts a bill without a UI confirmation. Every AI action logs the model output, human edits, approver, time and image hash, which doubles as evidence at an inspector visit. Privacy is binding: the DPDP Rules were notified on **13 Nov 2025** ([TheLeaflet](https://theleaflet.in/amp/story/explainer/indias-new-digital-personal-data-protection-rules-2025-a-detailed-reading)), and prescriptions fall under general personal data needing explicit, specific consent that is as easy to withdraw as to give ([AMLegals](https://amlegals.com/health-data-and-the-dpdp-act-a-practical-guide/)). Record consent per purpose (billing, reminders, marketing), minimise patient data sent to the model and use zero-retention terms. Meta's policy on prescription-drug messages over WhatsApp Business was not checked and must be before launching reminders. Cost stays low by caching the item master and prompt, using a small model for voice and drafts and reserving the large vision model for handwriting.

## Conclusion

The pharmacy type is less an AI product than a compliance product that AI makes fast. Its moat is the dull layer, batch-level stock, schedule gates, retention-safe registers and per-item GST, because that is what inspectors check and what generic mobile apps get wrong; AI then removes the hour of invoice typing and the silent loss of expired stock, which is where a 16–20% margin actually leaks. The sensible order is to build the deterministic core and invoice import first, add expiry and reorder agents second, and treat prescription reading and interactions as later, draft-only, licensed-data features.

Three unknowns should be settled before build: a CA-signed GST seed list (nil drugs, cosmetics, supplements), a reading of the Drugs Rules text for bill fields and NDPS records, and WhatsApp's policy on medicine messages. Grocery Khata should also run its own accuracy test on real distributor invoices from a few pilot chemists, since no public benchmark exists and that number will decide how much review the import screen needs.
