# Cut round trips, not features, to speed Grocery Khata

Grocery Khata's counter billing is already fast: a 5-item cash bill takes about **6-7 taps** and saving waits on **no network call**, because bills go into an offline outbox ([data.ts:193-215](../frontend/src/lib/data.ts), [outbox.ts:68-71](../frontend/src/lib/outbox.ts)). The slow parts are everywhere else. Online orders need about **4 taps and 3 blocking server round trips** and fail offline. New orders are noticed only by **30-second polling**. Supplier purchases are online-only. On the server, `GET /v1/catalogue` loads two whole tables and string-matches them on every request, roughly **O(items x photos)**. The master-item picker draws every row with no virtualization. The highest-value changes are an in-memory or precomputed catalogue, order actions moved into the outbox, a combined "pack and deliver" step, "repeat last order" for customers and shopkeepers, and a WhatsApp end-of-day summary on top of a daybook and a stock report. Competitors teach a consistent lesson. Ledger apps win on one-tap UPI reminders and "no app needed" customers (OkCredit). Billing apps win on barcode billing and bill-photo purchase entry (myBillBook, Vyapar, Swipe). Quick-commerce apps win on reorder-first home screens and editing an order after it is placed (Blinkit, BigBasket). The best model for Grocery Khata is a hybrid: keep the shop-first, khata-anchored relationship and add the reorder and edit-after-placing conveniences, rather than imitating marketplaces. Caveat: tap counts come from reading the code, not from timing the running app, and no runtime latency was measured.

## Selling at the counter is fast; selling online is three blocking round trips

The bill screen puts a search box above a 3-column tile grid. One tap adds an item at qty 1, with +/- per line, payment-mode chips and a save button ([BillScreen.tsx:122-141, 212-214, 272-279](../frontend/src/screens/BillScreen.tsx)). A **5-item cash bill is about 6 taps** (5 tiles plus save), plus one more if the payment mode must be changed. Each extra unit costs one "+" tap. Loose items and medicine strips open a prompt dialog, adding about **2 steps per line** ([BillScreen.tsx:90, 208](../frontend/src/screens/BillScreen.tsx)). Udhaar adds about 2-3 taps to pick the customer and set the paid amount, and the ledger delta travels in the same outbox op ([data.ts:210-211](../frontend/src/lib/data.ts)). This is competitive. Swipe markets bills "in under 10 seconds" ([Capterra Swipe](https://www.capterra.com/p/10008988/Swipe/)), but no rival publishes measured tap counts, so speed claims on all sides are marketing.

Order handling is where the friction sits. Opening an order does a GET. "Pack" POSTs `/pack`, the shopkeeper then picks Paid or On khata, and "Deliver" POSTs `/deliver` ([OrderScreen.tsx:31, 83, 94, 192-209](../frontend/src/screens/OrderScreen.tsx)). That is **about 4 taps and 3 server round trips, each one blocking**, called directly through `api()` rather than the outbox. So orders fail offline even though bills keep working, and `OutboxKind` already reserves an `'order'` type ([outbox.ts:4](../frontend/src/lib/outbox.ts)). New orders arrive by polling every 30 s in three separate places ([orderAlerts.ts:36](../frontend/src/lib/orderAlerts.ts), [InboxScreen.tsx:16](../frontend/src/screens/InboxScreen.tsx), [StoreApp.tsx:320](../frontend/src/store/StoreApp.tsx)). The average delay before an order is noticed is about 15 s. Each open device makes about 120 requests an hour, possibly doubled when the inbox and the alert loop run together.

Four changes remove most of this. First, add a single **"Pack and deliver (paid / khata)"** button for pickup and walk-in orders, saving about 2 taps and 1 round trip. Second, route pack, deliver and cancel through the outbox, applied optimistically, so the shopkeeper never waits on the network. Third, replace the triplicated polling with one shared query or a server push such as SSE or web push. Fourth, add **"repeat last bill"** and a long-press qty keypad to the bill screen. Rivals also suggest two counter features: barcode scanning, now standard in the billing tier (myBillBook, Vyapar, Swipe), and sharing the bill on WhatsApp with a UPI QR as the default end of checkout ([myBillBook](https://mybillbook.in/s/?p=29974), [Capterra Swipe](https://www.capterra.com/p/10008988/Swipe/)). PhonePe's SmartSpeaker shows the value of an audible payment confirmation, so the shopkeeper does not have to look at the phone ([PhonePe blog](https://www.phonepe.com/blog/merchant-solution/why-more-merchants-are-choosing-phonepe-a-smarter-way-to-do-business/)). An in-app spoken "Rs 240 received" would serve the same purpose.

## Buying and stock work, but purchases go offline-blind and the picker janks

Items enter the shop by bulk-picking from the master list: trade chips, then categories, then multi-select rows, with pack sizes chosen per item. Adding N items costs about **3 + N taps** ([PickItemsScreen.tsx:125, 190-220](../frontend/src/screens/PickItemsScreen.tsx)). The picker renders rows with a plain `.map` and recomputes several derived sets with `filter`/`find` scans on every render, one of them O(categories x items) ([PickItemsScreen.tsx:52, 75, 83, 220](../frontend/src/screens/PickItemsScreen.tsx)). No virtualization library is installed ([package.json](../frontend/package.json)). On a 1,000-row list on a low-end Android phone, the audit estimates **100-300 ms of jank per keystroke**. That figure is inferred, not measured. Memoising the derived sets, plus `useDeferredValue` or a windowed list, fixes it cheaply.

Supplier purchases have a strong feature that rivals mostly lack: AI reading of a photo of the invoice. The photo is shrunk to 1600 px, POSTed, and returns a draft to review and save ([SuppliersScreen.tsx:95, 134, 189-196](../frontend/src/screens/SuppliersScreen.tsx)). myBillBook offers the same pattern, OCR with review-before-save, on mobile only ([myBillBook Scan and Record](https://mybillbook.in/s/features/scan-and-record-bills/)). Grocery Khata's weakness is that purchase save and supplier payment are direct API calls that fail offline. Stock writes, by contrast, already use the outbox ([outbox.ts:4](../frontend/src/lib/outbox.ts)). Moving purchase save and supplier payment into the outbox closes the gap. Photo reading has to stay online, but the photo can be queued for upload.

The bigger opportunity is closing the loop from low stock to reorder. Vyapar and OkCredit both alert on low stock ([Techjockey Vyapar](https://www.techjockey.com/detail/vyapar), [okcredit.in/llms.txt](https://okcredit.in/llms.txt)). No billing app was found offering auto-reorder suggestions. Distributor apps hold the actual ordering: HUL Shikhar generates over a third of HUL sales ([Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/companies/news/hul-kirana-distribution-samadhan-strategy-124123100260_1.html)), and Jumbotail reports near-100% unassisted app orders from kiranas ([YourStory](https://yourstory.com/2024/03/jumbotail-marketplace-kirana-retail-artal-asia-funding)). Shikhar also suffered high dormancy, because retailers kept their monthly, face-to-face salesman rhythm ([WARC](https://www.warc.com/content/article/emvies/hul-shikhar-app-ai-prescribes-how-to-conquer-shikhar/en-gb/157382)). So the right move is a modest one: a low-stock list grouped by supplier that turns into a **one-tap WhatsApp order to that supplier**, fitting the habit shopkeepers already have rather than replacing it.

## Customers should start from their last order, not an empty aisle

Today a customer picks a shop, opens an aisle, taps items, adjusts quantities and sends. A 5-item list takes **about 9 taps** ([ListScreen.tsx:82-94, 124-228](../frontend/src/screens/planner/ListScreen.tsx)). Order status refreshes only when the screen refetches, since MyOrdersScreen has no refetch interval; only substitute approvals get an explicit OK / Leave out prompt ([MyOrdersScreen.tsx:82-83](../frontend/src/screens/planner/MyOrdersScreen.tsx)).

The large apps cut steps in three ways. They choose the store automatically from location. They lead with one-tap reorder: BigBasket's Smart Basket promises "quick reorder... in one tap" ([BigBasket](https://www.bigbasket.com/about-us)). And they let customers add items after paying, while the order is being packed, with no extra fee, as Blinkit has done since Dec 2025 ([CIOL](https://www.ciol.com/news/blinkit-enables-last-minute-order-additions-at-no-extra-fee-10872275)). Their weak spot is out-of-stock handling. Items are cancelled after payment and refunded later, which draws user complaints and even a consumer-commission ruling against Blinkit ([LiveLaw](https://livelaw.in/consumer-cases/south-mumbai-district-commission-holds-blinkit-liable-for-failure-to-refund-price-for-undelivered-grocery-item-244061), [Voxya](https://voxya.com/consumer-complaints/wrong-product-delivered-and-refund-denied/252729)). Grocery Khata's substitute-approval step is already better than this and should be promoted as a feature. Regular kirana customers on WhatsApp send typed lists, photos of handwritten lists or voice notes, and often just say "same as last time" ([chotu](https://owner.chotu.com/?p=3301)). That evidence comes from a vendor, but it matches the JioMart WhatsApp-to-kirana model ([The News Minute](https://www.thenewsminute.com/article/reliance-s-e-grocery-offering-jiomart-goes-live-whatsapp-here-s-how-it-works-123417)).

The best approach combines these. Pre-select the customer's last shop. Make the home screen **"Order again"** (last order and saved monthly list), so a repeat order takes 2-3 taps. Accept a photo or voice note as an order attachment for the shopkeeper to transcribe. Allow edits until the shopkeeper taps Pack. Send status updates ("ready", "out for delivery") by push or WhatsApp instead of waiting for a manual refresh. Avoid discount memberships as a retention tool. Zepto Pass lifted spend by over 30% ([YourStory](https://yourstory.com/2024/03/zepto-pass-loyalty-grocery-quick-commerce-user-experience)), but Zepto shut Zepto Daily in Feb 2026 because "subscriptions fail to anchor loyalty" ([YourStory](https://yourstory.com/2026/02/zepto-discontinues-zepto-daily-as-subscriptions-fail-to-anchor-loyalty-in-quick-commerce)). The khata relationship is the anchor, and a reorder reminder timed to each customer's usual gap is the cheap lever, though no public A/B evidence on such reminders was found. Standalone kirana marketplaces keep failing: PhonePe's Pincode closed its consumer business in Dec 2025 to build tools for shopkeepers instead ([YourStory](https://yourstory.com/2025/12/phonepes-pincode-winds-down-b2c-quick-commerce-operations)). That supports the current shop-first design over a discovery marketplace.

## The catalogue endpoint and serial sync are the latency hotspots

On every call, `GET /v1/catalogue` loads all CatalogueItems and all MasterItems with photos. It then runs up to three `FirstOrDefault` scans per item, ending in a case-insensitive `Name.Contains`. At 1,000 x 1,000 rows that is up to about **3 million string comparisons per request**, with no server cache, OutputCache or response compression ([CatalogueEndpoints.cs:35-47](../backend/src/GroceryKhata.Api/Catalogue/CatalogueEndpoints.cs)). There is also no index on `MasterItems.CatalogueItemId` ([AppDbContext.cs:322](../backend/src/GroceryKhata.Api/Persistence/AppDbContext.cs)). The fix is to precompute `PhotoMasterId` when a master photo is saved, or cache the DTO in memory and invalidate it on photo save, then add an ETag and response compression. That turns the endpoint into a near-constant read, and clients can skip the download entirely. The client already caches the catalogue for an hour ([planner.ts:211](../frontend/src/lib/planner.ts)).

The second hotspot is offline catch-up. The outbox flushes one op at a time with no batching ([outbox.ts:106-153](../frontend/src/lib/outbox.ts)), so after a day offline N queued ops mean **N sequential round trips**. A batch endpoint such as `POST /v1/sync/batch`, applied idempotently by op id, cuts catch-up time roughly N-fold. It also makes moving orders and purchases into the outbox cheap. The rest of the stack is in good shape: about 45 screens are lazy-loaded, a bundle budget script exists, and indexes cover bills, ledger entries, orders and products ([App.tsx:16-60](../frontend/src/App.tsx), [AppDbContext.cs:396, 1154, 1300](../backend/src/GroceryKhata.Api/Persistence/AppDbContext.cs)). The existing "SLOW" request log ([Program.cs:85-91](../backend/src/GroceryKhata.Api/Program.cs)) should be read before and after these changes. No runtime numbers were collected for this review.

## Add reporting as a daily push first, deep reports second

Grocery Khata already has more reporting than ledger apps. There is a monthly summary (sales, cash/UPI split, udhaar given and collected, profit after returns, stock loss and expenses, stock value, supplier due, a daily chart, top items), plus P&L, GST export, cash book, dues and trade registers ([ReportsScreen.tsx:47-129](../frontend/src/screens/ReportsScreen.tsx), [App.tsx:30-58](../frontend/src/App.tsx)). Several reports that rivals treat as core are missing: a **daybook**, a **stock summary and low-stock report**, **item-wise margin**, **customer dues ageing**, **supplier ageing**, and order fulfilment time. Vyapar's standard set includes Daybook, Bill-Wise Profit, Cash Flow, Sale Aging, Item-wise P&L, Stock Summary and Low Stock Summary ([Vyapar guide](https://vyaparapp.in/guides/how-to-check-transaction-reports-in-vyapar-app), [Vyapar P&L](https://vyaparapp.in/use-cases/profit-and-loss-reports)). myBillBook adds party statements shared as PDF on WhatsApp and GSTR reports auto-emailed to the CA ([myBillBook help](https://knowledge.mybillbook.in/articles/1989927-how-to-view-and-share-party-statements-on-mybillbook), [myBillBook CA](https://mybillbook.in/ca-icai)).

How reports are delivered matters as much as which ones exist. Swipe pushes a **daily summary on WhatsApp and email** ([Swipe community](https://community.getswipe.in/t/how-to-turn-on-daily-business-report/4395)). Payment apps centre on "what reached my bank", with PhonePe's settlement report arriving by 8 a.m. the next day ([PhonePe help](https://cms.phonepe.com/en/mx/merchant-help/issues-customer-payments/what-if-i-havent-received-money-successful-customer-payment/daily-settlement)). **97% of retail MSMEs** use WhatsApp ([Entrepreneur India](https://entrepreneur.com/en-in/news-and-trends/whatsapp-and-whatsapp-business-dominate-msme-landscape-with/476232)), and **Rs 74 of every Rs 100** of top-city retail spend is digital ([Deccan Herald](https://www.deccanherald.com/business/digital-payments-now-74-of-retail-spend-in-top-indian-cities-3744178)). So the cash-versus-UPI split is the number shopkeepers most need to check. Charts are secondary across rivals; Vyapar graphs only sales and purchases.

| Priority | Report | Delivery |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Today: sales, cash vs UPI, udhaar given/collected, cash in hand, pending orders | Home card plus optional end-of-day WhatsApp |
| 2 | Daybook (every bill, payment, expense for a date) | Screen, PDF share |
| 3 | Udhaar ageing (0-30/31-60/60+ days) with statement and one-tap UPI reminder | Screen, WhatsApp PDF |
| 4 | Low stock grouped by supplier, then stock value | Screen, "order on WhatsApp" button |
| 5 | Item-wise sales and margin, fast/slow movers | Monthly screen |
| 6 | Supplier ageing, expenses, Excel/PDF export for the accountant | Export |
| 7 | GSTR-1/3B-ready views | Paid or GST-registered shops only |

Build this on one server-side daily aggregate per shop (sales by mode, ledger deltas, stock movements), computed from existing indexed tables. Every report and the WhatsApp summary can then read the same numbers, and the client does not re-sum raw bills. No survey ranks which reports kirana owners actually open, so the ordering above follows the overlap across rivals plus the payment-app evidence. Usage logging should confirm it after launch.

## Conclusion

The main finding is that Grocery Khata's speed problem is architectural, not a matter of layout. Bills feel instant because they are offline-first. Orders, purchases and catalogue loading feel slow because they are not. Extending that one pattern — outbox for every write, batched sync, server-precomputed read models — fixes more latency than any screen redesign, and it also makes the app trustworthy on patchy rural networks, the place rivals like Vyapar draw sync complaints.

The competitive opening is also sharper than "copy the leaders". Marketplaces handle out-of-stock items badly and cannot hold customer credit. Ledger apps do not take orders. Billing apps do not serve the customer. A shop-first app that combines "order again", edits until packing, substitute approval and khata settlement in one place fills that gap. A daily WhatsApp summary then turns the data the app already collects into the one report every shopkeeper reads.
