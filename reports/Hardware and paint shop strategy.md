# Win hardware and paint counters with depth

Grocery Khata is already closer to a hardware and paint shop app than any app-first rival. Estimates, delivery challans billed together into one invoice, site-wise totals per contractor, credit limits and credit days, a painter/electrician/carpenter commission report, a tinted-line bill sheet, purchase orders, inbound e-way bill numbers and credit notes all exist in the code today, along with 337 paint and 319 hardware master rows ([Trades.cs](../backend/src/GroceryKhata.Api/Ledger/Trades.cs); [MasterItems.cs](../backend/src/GroceryKhata.Api/Catalogue/MasterItems.cs)). Vyapar and myBillBook publish only the general basics (multi-unit, batch, barcode, estimates, challans) and nothing specific to paint, while BUSY is the real benchmark, with tinting formulas that deduct base and colourant, shade-code stock, painter incentives, party-wise price lists and dealer schemes ([BUSY](https://busy.in/accounting-software/paint-shop/)). Gofrugal leads on hardware unit handling: it sells weight-bought goods by piece or metre and keeps retail and wholesale price levels ([Gofrugal](https://www.gofrugal.com/retail/specialized-retail-pos/hardware-shop-software.html)). Our gaps sit in exactly those places. We have no price tiers per customer type, no brand-wide percentage rate change, no selling of one item in two units on one bill, no shade history, and no commission payout ledger. All of these extend data structures we already have, so most are weeks of work, not quarters. If we close them at a subscription near the market's Rs 3,000-5,000 a year, we get BUSY-level trade depth at app-first price and speed.

## Project sales break grocery-style billing, and we already handle them

The defining hardware workflow is not a counter sale. A typical job starts with a quotation, then material goes out in parts against challans, unused or wrong items come back, and the account is settled at project end ([BluePrimes](https://blueprimes.com/?p=206)). One bill often mixes cement, pipes, paint, fasteners and electricals ([BillClap](https://www.billclap.com/billing-software-for-hardware-stores)), and shops carry **1,000+ SKUs** across those trades ([Accountune](https://accountune.com/hardware-store-billing-software)). Contractors, plumbers and electricians buy repeatedly, usually on credit, often split by site ([Munim](https://themunim.com/retail/gst-billing-software-for-hardware-shop/)).

Our code covers most of this chain. Estimates work even for walk-ins without a khata ([Estimates.cs](../backend/src/GroceryKhata.Api/Ledger/Estimates.cs)). Challans take goods out of stock immediately and stay off the khata until they are merged into one invoice, and each challan records site, vehicle number and place of supply. Customers get site-wise totals, a credit limit and credit days ([Trades.cs](../backend/src/GroceryKhata.Api/Ledger/Trades.cs)), and the bill warns when this bill would push the balance over the limit ([TradeBill.tsx](../frontend/src/screens/TradeBill.tsx)). Two links are missing. There is no path from estimate to challan, only from estimate to bill, so a quotation cannot show "balance pending to deliver". And only the UI warning was seen for the credit limit, with server-side enforcement unverified. Site returns go through numbered credit notes ([CreditNotes.cs](../backend/src/GroceryKhata.Api/Ledger/CreditNotes.cs)), but nothing restricts a return to items actually supplied to that site, and no rule prices cut pieces or opened boxes differently.

Units are the second structural test. Wire is sold by coil of fixed length, for example 90 m, priced per coil by size ([Havells price list](https://havells.com/content/dam/havells/brouchers/Consumer-cables/Havells_ConsumerCables.pdf)). Screws come in boxes but sell loose, and pipe is cut. Our `Unit` enum already covers Metre, RunningFt, Box, Dozen, Pair, Bag, Kg and Tonne, and products carry `PackSize`, `BagSize` and `PurchaseUnit` for buying in cartons and stocking in the base unit ([Product.cs](../backend/src/GroceryKhata.Api/Catalogue/Product.cs)). What the audit could not confirm is selling the same item in two units on one bill, such as "1 box or 7 loose". myBillBook advertises exactly that, with auto-detection of the selling unit ([myBillBook](https://mybillbook.in/s/billing-software-for-hardware-store/)). The cut-length case also matters: a 90 m coil cut to 23 m should leave 67 m in stock. No rival documents this cleanly, so it is an opening.

The competitive field has two layers, and that is what makes our position interesting.

The pattern is that **depth costs desktop licences and slowness, and speed costs depth**. Vyapar reviewers call it "terribly slow for fast-paced retail counters" and say item fields are limited ([G2](https://g2.com/products/vyapar/reviews?page=2)). Others report mobile-desktop sync problems ([Capterra](https://www.capterra.com/p/180579/Vyapar/reviews/)). One Marg user said complaints went unanswered for a year ([Capterra](https://www.capterra.com/p/151832/Erp-Software-9/reviews/?page=2)). Stock that does not match the software, and slow GST billing in peak hours, are the most-cited hardware pains ([TallyAtCloud](https://www.tallyatcloud.com/articles/gst-billing-software-for-hardware-traders-in-bathinda-focal-point-industrial-area-mangaluru-kiadb-industrial-area-2026-guide)). Niche paint software sells on IndiaMART for Rs 8,000-25,000 ([IndiaMART](https://m.indiamart.com/uniprotech/billing-software.html)), which shows dealers will pay for trade depth. Our caveat: these are vendor marketing pages, not tested products. BUSY's claims in particular may be thinner in practice than on its page, and no hardware-specific Play Store reviews were found.

## Paint shops need shade memory, not a tinting machine

The tinting machine and its formula software belong to the brand and form a closed system. Asian Paints dealers add custom shades inside the machine's own software ([Asian Paints](https://www.asianpaints.com/my-profile.html)), and no source shows any brand exporting tinting data to outside billing software. A new Berger dealer pays about **Rs 1.5-2 lakh** for the machine ([Berger](https://www.bergerpaints.com/blogs/how-to-become-a-paint-dealer)). We should not try to drive it. The shop's real problem sits next to it: the same shade name can carry different codes across product lines ([aapkapainter](https://aapkapainter.com/blog/explore-the-latest-asian-paint-shade-cards-for-your-home-aapkapainter)), and touch-ups years later need the exact match. BluePrimes sells a mixing log of date, shade, pack, customer or project and formula reference "for audit and repeat orders" ([BluePrimes](https://blueprimes.com/?p=210)).

Our tinted-line sheet already does half of this. The owner picks the base tin, which comes off stock, then types the machine's shade code and a tinting charge ([TradeBill.tsx](../frontend/src/screens/TradeBill.tsx)). The missing half is retrieval. We need a saved shade per customer, phone or site, a photo of the machine slip, and one tap to "repeat last shade". Optional colourant stock deduction would match BUSY's headline feature, but it needs a recipe per shade, and most dealers will never type one. A searchable shade history is the higher-value build.

Painters are the other lever. Every brand pays them directly: Asian Paints Masterstrokes, where dealers upload points ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/in/app/masterstrokes/id6445817469)), Birla Opus ID with QR coupons ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/in/app/birla-opus-id/id6476536889)), Nerolac RRK ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id1462264410)) and Berger Value Club ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id1533057353)). Hardware has the same structure: Havells EPLUS for electricians, Finolex and Astral plumber programmes. Astral values points on the **pre-GST purchase value from authorised dealers** ([Astral](https://www.astraladhesives.com/blog/know-how-to-turn-bondtite-purchases-into-big-rewards)). The dealer's own "painter cut" sits outside all these apps. We already store a `Painter` with a commission percentage, tag bills with `PainterId`, and send a monthly commission report on WhatsApp ([PaintersScreen.tsx](../frontend/src/screens/PaintersScreen.tsx)). What is missing is a payable ledger (earned, paid, balance), commission per brand or per litre rather than on the whole bill, and a bill format that prints brand, item and pre-tax value per line, tagged to the tradesperson, so the bill doubles as proof for a brand claim.

Brand schemes are the third paint-specific item. Asian Paints' auditors treat dealer discounts and incentives as a **key audit matter** ([Asian Paints FY25](https://www.asianpaints.com/content/dam/asianpaints/website/secondary-navigation/investors/annual-reports/2024-2025/Standalone-Financial_Statements.pdf)), which shows how much money flows through them. Only BUSY mentions dealer schemes, and no app reconciles the rebate a dealer expects against the credit note that arrives. Ordering already lives in brand apps such as MyAwaaz ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/in/app/myawaaz/id1077292745)), so we should log purchases and scheme credits, not rebuild ordering.

## Price lists move fortnightly, and our rates screen cannot keep up

Copper rose over **60% on MCX in 2025**, and wire makers pass hikes on fortnightly or monthly ([Upstox](https://upstox.com/news/market-news/stocks/copper-prices-soar-60-in-2025-four-key-sectors-that-could-face-the-heat-of-price-rise-check-details/article-187353)). Havells issues dated lists, each superseding the last ([Havells PDF](https://havellspowerplus.havells.com/NotificationUpload/285_flashAttachment.pdf)). A further 5-7% hike is reported for Q3 FY27 ([News Karnataka](https://newskarnataka.com/business/havells-plans-5-7-price-hikes-in-q3-fy27/23102026/)). Our "today's rates" endpoint accepts explicit new prices per item and logs every change to the audit trail ([ProductEndpoints.cs](../backend/src/GroceryKhata.Api/Catalogue/ProductEndpoints.cs)). That is good history, but it means typing 200 wire prices by hand. Shops need "Havells wires +6% from 1 Dec" by brand and category, and in time a CSV import of a brand list. Customer price tiers follow from the same logic: contractor and retail rates are discounts off the list. BUSY and Gofrugal both offer party or level pricing, and we have only a quantity-triggered `WholesalePrice` ([StockService.cs](../backend/src/GroceryKhata.Api/Catalogue/StockService.cs)). The best model stores the list price plus a discount percentage per customer group, so one list update reprices everything.

Tax is simpler than it looks. Paint (3208/3209) is 18% ([ClearTax](https://cleartax.in/s/paint-hsn-code-and-gst-rate)), cement fell from 28% to 18% on 22 September 2025 ([Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/industry/news/gst-rate-cut-on-cement-to-boost-infrastructure-125090401466_1.html)), and plastic pipe (3917) is 18% ([Tally](https://tallysolutions.com/gst/hsn-code-3917-product-classification-gst-rate-business-filing-guide/)). Agricultural hand tools are 5% while workshop tools stay at 18% ([Busy](https://busy.in/gst-rates/construction-materials)). Our master rows already carry GST and HSN with "check with CA" comments. E-way bills apply above **Rs 50,000** consignment value, challans and returns included ([ClearTax](https://cleartax.in/s/eway-bill-gst-rules-compliance)). Truckloads of cement, steel and pipe cross that line routinely. We only show a reminder at that limit and record inbound EWB numbers ([GoodsTracking.cs](../backend/src/GroceryKhata.Api/Ledger/GoodsTracking.cs)), so an e-way bill JSON export from a bill or challan is a natural next step.

Several things remain uncertain. We have no shop-owner interviews or reviews specific to hardware, no data on how much of a hardware shop's turnover is quotation-based, no public dealer discount structures, and no confirmation that brand tinting machines can export data. Items 1-5 are worth shipping regardless, because each one completes a feature we have already half-built.

## Conclusion

The surprise in this research is that our weakness is not missing modules but unfinished ones. Rivals either lack the project-sale chain entirely (Vyapar, myBillBook) or bury it in desktop ERPs priced in the tens of thousands (BUSY, Marg). We already own the chain, so our fastest path to leadership is to close the loops: settling commission, retrieving shades, repricing whole brands, and blocking unsafe credit. Our goal is not BUSY's feature count but its depth, delivered at counter speed on a phone.

The second insight is strategic. Brands spend heavily to own painters and plumbers directly, but none of their apps helps the dealer keep those tradespeople loyal to the shop. A shop app that makes the bill a claim document, runs the shop's own commission ledger, and remembers which painter's site used which shade turns Grocery Khata from a billing tool into the dealer's tool for keeping tradespeople. That is a position the brand apps and the generic billers have both left open.
