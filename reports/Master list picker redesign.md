# Turn the master list into a shopping aisle

Grocery Khata should rebuild the master-list picker so it works like a quick-commerce aisle rather than a settings form. Replace today's big yellow GST/HSN box with a small "2 GST सुधार" pill. Put a shop-type starter bundle ("★ लोकप्रिय 100", with every item pre-ticked) above everything else. Swap the single row of Hindi chips for 6 to 8 bilingual chips plus an "All ▾" sheet of icon tiles. Turn each grey checkbox line into a 56 to 64 px row with a thumbnail, a bold bilingual name, pack-size chips and a "+ जोड़ें" pill that becomes "✓ 2". Replace the pale "Add 0 items" button with a dark tray, "3 चुने · दुकान में जोड़ें →", that slides in on the first pick. The global "Choose all 1028 shown" control goes away, replaced by bundles, a per-category "Add all (45)" and a select-all that only appears once a filter has narrowed the list to about 50 items. This matters competitively. Mainstream Indian billing apps (Vyapar, myBillBook, Dukaan, GoFrugal) still make shopkeepers add items one form at a time or through Excel templates, and a real built-in library shows up mainly in niche apps such as Kirana Fast, which offers English only ([Vyapar](https://vyaparapp.in/guides/how-to-manage-stock-inventory-in-vyapar); [myBillBook](https://knowledge.mybillbook.in/articles/0441947-how-to-add-items-in-bulk-using-mybillbook); [Kirana Fast listing](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/-/id1567324958)). A fast, Hindi-first picker can therefore be the moment that makes a first impression. It can also stay light. Mukta at 2 to 3 weights, Lucide SVG icons, CSS-only motion, 320 px WebP thumbnails and `content-visibility` (or TanStack Virtual) keep the screen within a budget that a ₹7,000 Android phone can handle. One caveat applies throughout: most of the evidence is practitioner consensus and descriptions of Blinkit and Zepto, not controlled studies, so the numbers below are starting values to test with real shopkeepers.

## Competitors leave the "pick from a library" moment unclaimed

Indian billing apps mostly assume the shopkeeper brings their own catalogue. Vyapar adds items one at a time or through an Excel import, while still telling kirana owners to add items "along with the product images" ([Vyapar general store guide](https://vyaparapp.in/blog/general-store-inventory-management/)). myBillBook allows up to 5,000 items per uploaded template. It can also read a purchase bill from a photo, and it tags each line **"New" or "Matched"** against existing items ([myBillBook bulk](https://knowledge.mybillbook.in/articles/0441947-how-to-add-items-in-bulk-using-mybillbook); [myBillBook photo bills](https://knowledge.mybillbook.in/articles/2564722-how-to-auto-enter-purchase-bills-and-update-stock-from)). Dukaan asks for the business category at signup but keeps bulk upload **desktop-only** ([Dukaan start](https://dukaan.help.center/article/5056-how-do-i-start-a-store-on-dukaan); [Dukaan bulk](https://dukaan.help.center/article/5010-how-to-add-products-in-bulk)). Square's "Quick Create" needs only name and price ([Square](https://squareup.com/help/au/article/5115)). The closest analogues to Grocery Khata's master list are Kirana Fast, which claims **2 million preloaded products**, a business-category dialog on first login and auto image suggestions, and Mystore's ONDC enterprise "master catalogue" ([Kirana Fast](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/-/id1567324958); [Mystore](https://www.mystore.in/en/blog/5-powerful-features-of-mystore-seller-app-for-enterprises-to-unlock-ondc-networks-potential)). Kirana Fast has about 58.8K installs and a near-empty rating record ([AppGoblin](https://appgoblin.info/apps/com.apnidukan.my_app)), so nobody has yet made this pattern feel good.

Three ideas are worth borrowing. Take the shop type from signup to choose the starter bundle. Use myBillBook's "Matched" idea to mark master items the shop already has ("दुकान में है") rather than letting them be added twice. Use Square's minimum to keep price optional at pick time. No source documented the exact picker UI of any Indian app, and OkCredit, Marg, Zoho and Petpooja were not covered, so a screenshot audit is still worth doing.

The visual language shopkeepers already know comes from quick commerce. Blinkit and Zepto product cards pair an image, a bold name, a subdued pack and price line, and a strong "add" button that turns into a stepper, and the cart updates in place without changing page ([vp0](https://vp0.com/blogs/in-blinkit-zepto-10-minute-grocery-delivery-ui-template); [Aadhavan M Zepto redesign](https://aadhavanm.pages.dev/skills/visual-design)). Zepto rebuilt its search to understand typos and Hindi phrasing ([Storyboard18](https://www.storyboard18.com/brand-marketing/from-typos-to-hindi-phrases-zepto-says-its-search-now-understands-everyday-language-inputs-94148.htm)). The mockup's "+ जोड़ें" pill and dark tray deliberately copy this idiom, and that is the right call.

## Six of today's elements work against the shopkeeper

**The yellow updates box.** It is the first and largest thing on screen, and it is the least urgent. The NHS design system warns that "people often miss banners", and that overusing them makes this worse ([NHS](https://service-manual.nhs.uk/design-system/components/notification-banners)). A permanent box trains users to ignore it. Shrink it to the header pill "2 GST सुधार" (amber fill, dark text). Tapping it opens a bottom sheet that lists each GST/HSN change with old → new, per-item "लागू करें" and "सब लागू करें". Show it as a one-line expanded banner only when there are unseen updates, and only once.

**"Choose all 1028 shown".** NN/g endorses Select All, but only alongside clear feedback and undo ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/videos/bulk-actions-design-guidelines/)). The stakes grow with the count, so bulk actions should state their count and offer an undo toast rather than a modal ([AppMaster](https://appmaster.io/blog/bulk-actions-done-safely-preview-permissions-undo-patterns)). One tap that dumps 1,028 items into a shop leaves a catalogue that is hard to clean. Replace it with bundles, per-category "Add all" and a filtered select-all, each followed by a single "↶ वापस" snackbar placed above the tray.

**The long checkbox list.** A long list of checkboxes "looks intimidating" and invites abandonment ([UX Planet](https://uxplanet.org/checkbox-and-toggle-in-forms-f0de6086ac41)), and the native checkbox is a small target. Multi-select is still correct, because checkboxes, not toggles, fit a batch submitted together ([DMC Info](https://www.dmcinfo.com/blog/22434/user-interface-design-tips-checkboxes-vs-toggle-switches/)). The fix is presentation, not semantics: the whole row becomes the target, and the selected state is loud.

**The grey detail lines.** Category and GST repeated on every row add clutter without helping the decision. The section header already names the category, and GST belongs in the review sheet.

**The single Hindi chip row.** Only a few tabs fit across a phone screen, and whatever sits off-screen is easily overlooked ([Digital Doughnut](https://www.digitaldoughnut.com/articles/2021/august-2021/top-6-basic-patterns-for-mobile-navigation)). With 20 to 30 categories, most of them are invisible.

**The pale "Add 0 items".** "Disabled elements are rarely the right solution" ([Staffbase](https://spark.staffbase.com/patterns/disabling-elements)). Users cannot tell why a greyed button is dead ([UX Movement](https://uxmovement.com/buttons/why-you-shouldnt-gray-out-disabled-buttons/)). A typical pale grey such as #d1d5db on white is only **1.47:1**, far below the 3:1 that WCAG 1.4.11 asks of UI components ([Stark](https://getstark.co/wcag-explained/perceivable/distinguishable/non-text-contrast)).

## The recommended screen, top to bottom

The table gives the target layout at 360 px width. It matches the mockup already shown, with the gaps filled in.

| Zone | Content | Behaviour |
|---|---|---|
| Header (56 px, teal #0f6b5c) | "← मास्टर लिस्ट से चुनें", small "2 GST सुधार" pill on the right | Pill opens the updates sheet; hidden when there are no updates |
| Search (sticky) | "आटा, dal, sabun… खोजें" with a clear button | Instant on-device match across Hindi, English and Hinglish |
| Category chips (sticky) | ★ लोकप्रिय · आटा-दाल · तेल-घी · मसाले · … · "सभी ▾" | Active chip follows scroll position; "सभी" opens the icon-tile sheet |
| Starter card | "★ लोकप्रिय 100 · आपकी किराना दुकान के लिए" + "सब 100 जोड़ें" + "देखें" | Preview sheet with everything pre-ticked; shopkeeper unticks |
| Sectioned list | Sticky header "आटा व दाल · Atta & Dal (45) · सब जोड़ें"; rows inside one white card per group | Row tap toggles; pack chips expand inline |
| Bottom | No selection: slim hint "आइटम पर टैप करें"; with a selection: dark tray "3 चुने · दुकान में जोड़ें →" | Tray count opens a review sheet with removable items |

### Selection: a quick-commerce "+ जोड़ें" pill, not a checkbox

Each row is **56 to 64 px min-height**, never a fixed height, because a fixed height clips matras. It holds a 44 × 44 thumbnail (radius 12), a bold name, a subdued meta line, and a trailing pill. The pill reads "+ जोड़ें" in teal outline. When selected it fills teal and reads "✓" or "✓ 2" (the number of pack sizes chosen). The row gets the tint #e3f1ee and a 2 px left bar, so selection does not depend on colour alone. The whole row is the tap target, far above WCAG 2.2's 24 px minimum ([WAI](https://deploy-preview-343--wai-website.netlify.app/standards-guidelines/wcag/new-in-22)), and the pill's hit area pads to about 48 × 48 ([UXcel](https://uxcel.com/lessons/mobile-selection-controls-191)). An item the shop already has shows a muted "दुकान में है" tag instead of the pill. If an item has several pack sizes, selecting it expands choice chips inline (250 g | 500 g | 1 kg, multi-select). Price stays optional: show MRP as a hint where it is known, and keep a "बाद में भाव डालें" default so bulk picking never stalls on a number field.

A list, not a photo grid, is right for this screen. A 2-column tile grid shows about 6 items per screen against about 10 to 12 list rows, and the job here is covering hundreds of items quickly. The earlier image-grid research's 2-column, 164 px card remains correct for the store and billing views, where browsing the photos is the point.

### Categories: chips for the common ones, a tile sheet for all

NN/g lists category landing pages and section menus among the standard mobile subnavigation patterns ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/topic/navigation/?page=3)). Quick-commerce apps use category grids so that everything is visible at a glance ([vp0](https://vp0.com/blogs/in-blinkit-zepto-10-minute-grocery-delivery-ui-template)). Keep 6 to 8 chips in Hindi with small English text, and end the row with "सभी ▾". That opens a bottom sheet with a 3-column grid of icon tiles, each showing an icon, the Hindi name, the English name and a progress count such as "3/45". The list below is sectioned with sticky headers that carry "सब जोड़ें (45)". Keep the sticky stack short (search plus chips at about 104 px), because sticky elements must not eat the screen and can hide section headings ([Framer](https://www.framer.com/dictionary/sticky-navigation); [Brizy](https://support.brizy.io/hc/en-us/community/posts/13157679353105-Mobile-User-Experience-of-Tabs-Accordions)). Collapse the search into the header when the user scrolls down.

### Search: assume Hinglish

About **21% of non-English queries on an Indian e-commerce platform were Hinglish**, and literal translation of them did badly ([arXiv 2208.03713](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2208.03713v1.pdf)). Myntra built vernacular search for queries such as "Kala Kurta" ([Business Standard](https://business-standard.com/companies/news/myntra-unveils-vernacular-search-for-users-to-shop-in-local-languages-123042500789_1.html)). With about 1,000 to 1,500 master items, run search entirely in the browser. Precompute one key per item from the English name, the Devanagari name, a romanised Hindi name and an alias list (atta/aata, besan/baisan, dal/daal). Normalise by lowercasing, collapsing repeated vowels and folding ph→f, v→w and sh→s. Match on prefix first, then contains, with a debounce of about 120 ms. When the box is empty, show "हाल में" and "किराना में लोकप्रिय". When nothing matches, offer "यह आइटम जोड़ने का अनुरोध करें" and a quick custom-item add. Once a search narrows the list to 50 items or fewer, show "सब 12 चुनें".

### Starter bundles: untick, don't tick

Dukaan and Kirana Fast already ask for the shop type at signup ([Dukaan](https://dukaan.help.center/article/5056-how-do-i-start-a-store-on-dukaan); [Kirana Fast](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/-/id1567324958)). Grocery Khata should use that answer. For a kirana, show "★ लोकप्रिय 100". A beauty shop gets its own top list drawn from its 952-item master. "सब 100 जोड़ें" adds the bundle in one tap with an undo snackbar. "देखें" opens a sheet where everything is pre-ticked and the owner removes what they don't stock. Hick's law says fewer choices mean faster decisions ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/topic/navigation/?page=3)), and turning 1,028 decisions into roughly 15 unticks is the biggest single win available. Curate the first bundles by hand, then rebuild them from "most-added among shops of this trade" once usage data exists. No public data gives the ideal bundle size, so test 60, 100 and 150.

## Bold and light: type, colour, icons, motion

**Typography.** Keep Mukta. It is an OFL family with seven static weights from 200 to 800 that covers both Devanagari and Latin ([Fontsource](https://fontsource.org/fonts/mukta/about)). Self-host only 400, 600 and 800 as woff2, using Devanagari and Latin `unicode-range` subsets with `font-display: swap` ([unicodefyi](https://unicodefyi.com/series/modern-web/fonts-and-rendering)), and preload only the 600 Devanagari file. Suggested scale: screen title 20 to 22 px/800; row name 16 to 17 px/600 at line-height 1.45 to 1.5; meta line 13 to 14 px/400 in slate (#64748b passes at 4.76:1); section headers 13 px/700. Devanagari clips at tight line-heights. Tests showed clipping at 1.1 that was fixed at 1.6 or more, and fixed-height containers clip matras whatever the font ([SuttaCentral](https://discourse.suttacentral.net/t/testing-for-line-height-with-tall-scripts/6196); [Articulate](https://community.articulate.com/discussions/discuss/hindi-text-clipping-in-player-ui---looking-for-a-devanagari-safe-font/1242062)). Never letter-space Devanagari. Set `lang="hi"` on Hindi spans, and calibrate `size-adjust` and the ascent/descent overrides on the fallback face to remove font-swap layout shift ([web.dev](https://web.dev/articles/css-size-adjust)). Lead with Hindi and set English beside it in a lighter weight.

**Colour.** Teal #0f6b5c passes AA on white (**6.41:1**) and on the #eef3f2 background (5.72:1). White cards on #eef3f2, however, are only 1.12:1, so separate groups with a hairline shadow (`0 1px 2px rgb(15 40 35/.06), 0 0 0 1px rgb(15 40 35/.05)`) rather than relying on fill. Use teal for the primary colour and selection, #0a5247 for pressed, and #e3f1ee for the selected tint. A dark slate tray (#0f1f1c) with an amber count badge gives the bold, CRED-like confidence at no cost. Amber #f59e0b is 2.15:1 on white, so use it only as a fill under dark text, never as text. For warning text use #b45309 (5.02:1) and for success #15803d (5.02:1) ([getwcag](https://getwcag.com/blog/wcag-2-2-checklist)). The yellow flood goes away entirely.

**Icons and images.** Use lucide-react, which tree-shakes to about 0.5 to 1 KB per icon ([PkgPulse](https://www.pkgpulse.com/guides/lucide-vs-heroicons-vs-phosphor-react-icon-libraries-2026)). Draw category tiles as two-tone inline SVGs under 2 KB each. Avoid emoji as the primary glyph, because rendering varies by Android version. Pairing an icon with text helps semi-literate users ([BillCut](https://www.billcut.com/blogs/fintech-in-bharat-designing-for-digital-literacy), an agency blog). Thumbnails use the master photos that already exist, served as the 320 px WebP rendition from the image-grid work (about 6 to 15 KB) with explicit `width`/`height`, `loading="lazy"` and `decoding="async"`. Where there is no photo, show a tinted square with the first letter in Mukta 700. It costs no bytes, looks intentional, and will be the normal state for many loose staples.

**Motion.** Animate transform and opacity only, for 120 to 200 ms. Rows press with `:active{transform:scale(.98)}` and the tap highlight removed. The check draws with a 180 ms stroke-dashoffset. The tray slides up on the first pick, and its count pops 1 → 1.15 → 1. Add an optional `navigator.vibrate(10)` behind feature detection and a user toggle ([OpenReplay](https://blog.openreplay.com/haptic-feedback-for-web-apps-with-the-vibration-api/)), applied consistently to the same action ([Android haptics](https://developer.android.com/develop/ui/views/haptics/haptics-principles)). Wrap all of it in `prefers-reduced-motion: no-preference`. Don't use Lottie or Framer Motion on this screen.

## The web equivalent of FlashList is content-visibility, then windowing

The React Native advice translates directly. FlashList's lessons are to recycle rows, memoise them, keep separate pools for headers and rows, and measure on a low-end device in release builds ([FlashList performance](https://shopify.github.io/flash-list/docs/fundamentals/performance)). On the web, start with `content-visibility: auto` plus `contain-intrinsic-size: auto 64px × rows` on each category group's card. In web.dev's demo this cut rendering from **232 ms to 30 ms**, it keeps content in the accessibility tree, and it is supported in Chrome 85+ ([web.dev](https://web.dev/articles/content-visibility)). It also keeps in-page find and sticky headers simple. If search or filter interaction (INP) goes above 200 ms on a test phone, move to `@tanstack/react-virtual`, with headers and rows as distinct item types, so that only about 30 rows, and therefore about 30 decoded thumbnails, exist at once. Store the selection as a `Set` of ids in a store outside the row props, and pass each row only `selected: boolean`, so that one tap re-renders one memoised row, not 1,000. Run search filtering inside `startTransition` so typing stays responsive.

Budgets, measured against Alex Russell's P75 device budget of about 150 KiB for HTML, CSS and fonts and 300 to 350 KiB of JS for a whole app ([Infrequently Noted summary](https://conffab.com/elsewhere/the-performance-inequality-gap-2024-infrequently-noted/)): CSS 25 KB gz or less, fonts 60 KB or less, picker route JS 100 KB gz or less, thumbnails about 6 to 15 KB each, CLS as close to 0 as possible, INP under 200 ms. Cache the master list JSON and thumbnails with a Workbox cache-first route, so the second visit opens instantly offline.

## Prioritised build plan

| # | Step | Effort | Why first |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Header pill + updates sheet replace the yellow box; hint bar + dark tray replace the disabled button; remove "Choose all 1028" | ~1 day | Biggest visual change for the least code; removes the two worst first impressions |
| 2 | New row: whole-row tap, "+ जोड़ें" → "✓" pill, tint + left bar, bold bilingual name, one meta line, thumbnail/letter tile, "दुकान में है" tag | 1–2 days | Core of the "bold and attractive" ask |
| 3 | Sectioned list with sticky headers + "सब जोड़ें (n)", per-group `content-visibility`, Set-based selection, undo snackbar | 1–2 days | Makes 1,000 rows fast and safe |
| 4 | Starter bundle card + preview sheet per shop type (curated lists) | 1–2 days plus curation | Turns about 1,000 decisions into about 15 |
| 5 | Hinglish search index (aliases, normalisation), empty/no-result states, filtered select-all ≤ 50 | 1–2 days | Covers the shopkeeper who knows exactly what they want |
| 6 | Chip row synced to scroll + "सभी ▾" icon tile sheet with progress counts; SVG category icons | 2 days plus icon art | Navigation across 20 to 30 categories |
| 7 | Inline pack-size chips, optional MRP/price, review sheet with GST shown | 1–2 days | Detail without row clutter |
| 8 | Polish and measure: Mukta subsetting and preload, fallback metrics, motion and haptics, Lighthouse on a ₹7–8k Android, TanStack Virtual if INP > 200 ms | 1–2 days | Proves "lightweight" on real hardware |

Steps 1 to 3 alone deliver most of the first impression in about a week. Instrument from day one: time from opening the picker to the first add, number of items added per shop, bundle untick rate, and search no-result terms (which feed the alias list and the master list itself).

## Conclusion

The redesign is really about moving the shopkeeper's effort from deciding to correcting. A pre-ticked starter bundle, a "दुकान में है" match and an undo snackbar all let the owner edit a good guess rather than build a catalogue from nothing. That is also what separates Grocery Khata from competitors who still hand over an Excel template. The second insight is that "bold" and "lightweight" do not pull against each other here. The boldness comes from type weight, one deep teal, a dark tray and confident spacing, all of which cost nothing to download, while the heavy things (big banners, animation libraries, full-size photos, 1,000 live checkboxes) are exactly what the redesign removes. The open questions are empirical: the best bundle size, whether shopkeepers want GST at pick time, and how Blinkit-style rows feel to a first-time Hindi-only owner. A five-shop hallway test of steps 1 to 4 should settle them before step 6's icon artwork is commissioned.
