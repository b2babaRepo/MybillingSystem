# Split items into options, show photos on tints

Grocery Khata should keep its shared catalogue but add one layer it is missing: a **generic common item ("Atta") that owns several options (brand + pack size, or "Loose, per kg"), each priced per shop**. That single change makes both ordering flows work. A household can pick an exact option from an "N options" bottom sheet, or order the generic item ("Atta, 5 kg, any brand") with a substitution preference, and the shopkeeper chooses the option at packing time. For browsing, use a **preset two-level tree of about 15 kirana aisles**, shown as 4-per-row rounded pastel tiles under bold section headers. Then use 3-column product cards with cut-out photos, falling back to today's emoji-on-gradient when no photo exists. Images should be resized on the server into 160/320 px WebP thumbnails of about 15–20 KB, lazy-loaded with native attributes, and kept in a capped Workbox cache so offline use and the 130 KB JS budget both hold. The evidence is uneven, and it should be read that way. The data model, substitution policies, licensing and caching findings come from fetched primary sources. **Almost everything about how Blinkit, Zepto and Flipkart Minutes lay out cards and tiles is unverified**: their pages returned HTTP 403 or rendered empty, and no citable teardown exists. Those layout details come from the researchers' recollection and need a screenshot audit before you copy any measurement.

## About 15 compound-named aisles, two levels deep, fits a kirana

The big apps converge on compound aisle names joined by "&", with item types one level below. Zepto's "Atta, Rice, Oil & Dals" contains Oil, Atta, Besan/Sooji/Maida, Healthy Dal and Rice & More ([Zepto](https://www.zepto.com/category/atta-rice-oil-dals)). Zepto's "Dairy, Bread & Eggs" sits beside separate "Cold Drinks & Juices" and "Snacks" aisles ([Zepto](https://www.zepto.com/category/dairy-bread-eggs)). Blinkit uses names like "Munchies" and "Dairy, Bread & Eggs" ([Shiprocket](https://www.shiprocket.in/blog/most-selling-products-on-blinkit/); [MetricsCart](https://metricscart.com/quick-commerce-analytics/reports/blinkit/munchies-on-blinkit/)). BigBasket's URLs show a third level (Foodgrains, Oil & Masala → Masalas & Spices → Powdered Spices) ([BigBasket](https://www.bigbasket.com/all-offers/d/10-15/foodgrains-oil-masala/masalas-spices/)). JioMart, by contrast, used only **8 broad categories** (Staples, Dairy & Bakery, Snacks & Branded Foods, Beverages, Personal/Home/Baby Care, Fruits & Vegetables) ([Entrepreneur India](https://india.entrepreneur.com/en-in/lifestyle/jiomart-is-serving-200-cities-and-heres-how-you-can-order/351083)). Kirana billing tools go flatter still. Vyapar has a single optional user-defined "Item Category", used mainly for reports ([Vyapar](https://vyaparapp.in/blog/general-store-inventory-management/)).

A 53-item catalogue that will grow into the hundreds sits between these extremes. The recommendation, which is a synthesis and not taken from any one app, is a preset bilingual tree of **15 top-level aisles**: Atta, Rice & Grains; Dal & Pulses; Oil & Ghee; Masala & Spices; Sugar, Jaggery & Dry Fruits; Dairy, Bread & Eggs; Fruits & Vegetables; Snacks & Biscuits; Instant & Packaged Food; Beverages; Personal Care; Cleaning & Detergents; Household; Pooja Items; Baby Care. Each aisle has one sub-level of item types (Toor, Moong, Chana under Dal). Brand and size belong on options, not in the tree. Group the aisles under four or five home-screen headers ("Kitchen staples", "Dairy & fresh", "Snacks & drinks", "Personal & home care", "Pooja & baby"), as the reference beauty app does. Let shops add custom sub-categories without changing the shared top level. Some placements will be disputed, such as ghee under Dairy (JioMart) or under Oil (others). Pick one and make search match both. ONDC's RET10 grocery enum would be a natural standard to align with, but it **was not retrieved**, so treat any ONDC mapping as future work.

## A common item, shared options and shop prices make both flows possible

The standard commerce model is a parent product whose variants are separate rows, each with its own price, SKU and barcode ([Shopify schema](https://shopify.dev/docs/apps/data-warehouse/reference/schema/product_variants)). Shopify now allows 2,048 variants per product but still only **3 option axes** ([Shopify changelog](https://shopify.dev/changelog/the-product-variant-limit-is-now-2048-for-all-merchants); [Matrixify](https://matrixify.app/documentation/shopify-limits/)), which suggests that groceries rarely need more than brand + size (+ flavour/packaging). Indian SMB tools work the same way at a smaller scale. Dukaan gives each size variant its own name, MRP and selling price ([Dukaan](https://dukaan.help.center/article/5006-how-to-add-size-and-color-options-to-products)), and Vyapar stores a manufacturer EAN as the item code ([Vyapar](https://vyaparapp.in/guides/how-to-generate-a-barcode-in-vyapar)). For loose goods, retail standards treat the item as variable-measure: the price or quantity is computed from weight ([GS1](https://support.gs1.org/support/solutions/articles/43000734396-what-is-a-variable-measure-trade-item-)), or the item gets a PLU code when it has no barcode ([ecosire](https://ecosire.com/zh/apps/erpnext/erpnext-supermarket-grocery-pos)).

For Grocery Khata this becomes three tables. **CommonItem** is the existing 53 rows. **ItemOption** is shared across shops and holds brand, size value + base unit normalised to g/ml/piece, packaging, `isLoose`, optional EAN, and MRP. **ShopOption** holds shop price, in-stock flag and the shop's default. The existing per-shop product price moves down to ShopOption. A "Loose" option has unit = kg, takes a decimal quantity, and charges qty × rate. Normalised sizes let the app show ₹/kg and rank "similar size" substitutes. They also match Legal Metrology. Since 2022 standard pack sizes are no longer mandated, so any size is valid, but packages must declare MRP, and since 2021 a unit sale price ([KS&K](https://ksandk.com/legal-metrology/packaged-commodities-rules-2022/); [S.S. Rana](https://ssrana.in/articles/legal-metrology-packaged-commodities-amendment-rules-2022-2/)). The shop should be warned when its price is above MRP. That MRP ceiling is well known but was not cited in the notes. Shop onboarding is "tick the options you stock and type a price" from the shared master, and scanning an EAN can match an option. GS1 India's DataKart (**14M+ products**, API and GTIN validation) is the paid national master that could seed this ([GS1 India](https://gs1india.org/datakart/)).

### Customer picks exactly, or delegates and states a substitution rule

Western grocery delivery has a mature substitution pattern. Instacart lets the customer set, for each item, **Best Match (shopper picks), Specific Item, or Refund**. It defaults to finding a replacement and lets the customer approve or decline live ([Instacart](https://company.instacart.com/shopper-community/providing-a-more-straightforward-replacements-experience)). Pricing rules differ. Walmart charges the substitute's price and nothing for a declined one ([Walmart CA](https://www.walmart.ca/en/help/article/substitutions/cff56618f0d744ed87046ade05792161)). Woolworths NZ upgrades at the original price ([Woolworths NZ](https://www.woolworths.co.nz/help/policies/store-policies)). Deliveroo caps the allowed price difference in either direction ([Deliveroo](https://help.deliveroo.com/en/articles/6404744-faqs-item-substitutions-grocery-partners-only)). No equivalent Blinkit, Zepto, BigBasket or JioMart documentation was found.

Applied to this app, an order line stores either a specific `optionId` or a generic `commonItemId + quantity`, plus a preference: **"Shop chooses any brand", "Ask me", or "Don't replace"**. A single order-level default saves taps. At packing, each generic line shows that shop's in-stock options, ranked by size closeness and then by the household's past purchases. The shopkeeper taps one, or scans it. When a specifically chosen option is out of stock, the shop proposes a substitute. The packed option's shop price is billed. A delegated choice within a threshold (for example ±10% or ₹20) is auto-accepted. Anything else needs the customer's accept or reject, with WhatsApp or call as fallback. **Nothing posts to the khata until it is accepted**, and later complaints flow into the existing item-level dispute screen. That keeps the ledger's transparency promise intact. The thresholds are design proposals, not sourced numbers.

## Cards copy the quick-commerce anatomy, with layout details unverified

The general e-commerce pattern is sourced. A product with variants replaces quick-add with an option picker ([Foxecom](https://docs.foxecom.com/zest-theme/theme-settings/product-card); [Maropost](https://galaxy.maropost.com/kb/articles/2187-variant-selector-for-add-to-cart)). Merchants dislike the extra pop-up step ([Cornerstone feedback](https://cornerstone-theme.nolt.io/87)), so the default option should stay one tap away. Commentary on Zepto warns against covering key CTAs with modals ([Tear Them Down](https://tearthemdown.substack.com/p/zepto-prop)). Write-ups describe a tap-friendly category grid and product cards with a clear price and ADD button ([vp0](https://vp0.com/blogs/in-blinkit-zepto-10-minute-grocery-delivery-ui-template), a vendor source).

The table below separates what is verified from what is recalled.

| Variant products show an option picker instead of instant add | **Verified** as a general e-commerce pattern; not verified for Blinkit/Zepto specifically |
| Card order: cut-out image → name (2 lines) → pack size → price + struck MRP → ADD that becomes a −/qty/+ stepper | Unverified recollection |
| "N options" under ADD opens a bottom sheet with each pack's size, price, MRP and its own stepper (Blinkit/Zepto); BigBasket uses a dropdown | Unverified recollection |
| Category tiles: 4 per row, ~16 px radius, pale tint, collage of 2–3 cut-out packs, 2-line label, section headers like "Grocery & Kitchen" | Unverified, though it matches the owner's beauty-app reference |
| Category page: left rail of subcategory thumbnails + 2-column grid; % OFF ribbon; veg/non-veg mark; greyed out-of-stock card | Unverified |

Even unverified, the pattern is consistent enough to design against. Grocery Khata's **category tile** should be a rounded square (about 16 px radius) tinted with the category's existing colour, softened to a pastel. It holds one cut-out photo or illustration filling about 70% of the tile, with a centred label of at most 2 lines / ~18 characters below. Use 4 per row at 360 dp and 3 below 340 dp. The **product card** in the household list and shop views should sit in a 3-column grid. It has a square image on the light tint, the name in 2 lines, the default option's size in grey, the shop price with struck MRP only when the shop sets MRP above price, an ADD button that becomes a stepper, and an "N options" chip that opens the bottom sheet. The first row of that sheet is "Let shop choose". Drop the delivery-minutes chip, because it means nothing at a kirana. Use the slot for "Udhaar ok" or a stock state. The out-of-stock state is a desaturated image and no ADD. The billing and packing screens should stay dense list rows with 40 px thumbnails, not cards.

## Shop photos and server-side thumbnails keep images legal and light

Copying images from Blinkit, Zepto, Flipkart or BigBasket is not an option. Scraper services sell them ([Apify](https://apify.com/123webdata/blinkit-scraper.md)), but that confers no rights. The usable sources are these. Open Food Facts photos are **CC BY-SA**, with attribution, a link and share-alike, and that licence does not clear package trademarks ([OFF terms](https://world.openfoodfacts.org/terms-of-use)). GS1 DataKart images are brand-verified and need a Premium subscription ([DataKart Premium](https://gs1india.org/datakart/datakart-premium)). Brand-supplied packshots need written permission. Photos the shop takes itself are the most practical route, and come out best with background removal into cut-outs. Generic illustrations or free stock images suit category tiles. Indian coverage in OFF and the legal standing of showing pack artwork (likely nominative use) are inferences that warrant legal advice. The practical priority is: shop "tap to add photo" for real options, illustrations for the 15 category tiles, and OFF or DataKart as optional seeds with a credits screen.

For performance, attach images to ItemOption, falling back to CommonItem. Store a 512×512 master and generate **160 px and 320 px WebP thumbnails at upload on the server**, which adds no client JS. Render them with `loading="lazy"`, explicit width/height and a CSS tint background as the placeholder. When no image exists, show the current emoji-on-gradient, never a broken-image icon. Offline, use a separate cache-first runtime image cache with Workbox `maxEntries` (about 300–500, roughly 5–10 MB), `maxAgeSeconds` (about 30 days) and `purgeOnQuotaError` ([Workbox](https://developer.chrome.com/docs/workbox/modules/workbox-expiration)). Call `navigator.storage.persist()`. Chrome storage is evictable "best effort" unless it is persisted, even though an origin may use up to 60% of disk ([web.dev](https://web.dev/articles/storage-for-the-web)). Serve images same-origin or with CORS to avoid opaque-response quota padding. That padding is known behaviour but was not confirmed by a fetched source. On Wi-Fi, optionally pre-cache the category tiles and the shop's top ~100 options.

## Conclusion

The biggest point is structural, not visual. The app's current model of one item with one shop price cannot express "Aashirvaad 5 kg" versus "loose atta per kg". It also cannot express "any brand, you choose", and without those, both customer choice and shopkeeper substitution stay informal and fall outside the khata. An option layer with explicit substitution states turns packing decisions into recorded, approvable ledger events that feed the dispute flow the app already has. This gives Grocery Khata a transparency edge that the quick-commerce apps do not need and the kirana billing tools do not offer.

The visual work is cheaper than it looks. The tinted-tile look is CSS plus photos, which costs nothing against the JS budget, and the emoji fallback means the catalogue can fill in gradually as shops photograph their stock. The open risk is copying layouts from memory. A one-hour screenshot audit of Blinkit, Zepto and Flipkart Minutes is still needed for the options sheet and tile sizes. Separately, someone should retrieve the ONDC RET10 category list before the tree is frozen.
