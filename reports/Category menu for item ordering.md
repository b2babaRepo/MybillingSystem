# Build one aisle menu for every order screen

Apna Kaarobar should build **one shared "सब विभाग / All categories" menu component** and open it from every screen where items are picked for an order. That means the shop's bill screen, supplier orders, the customer online store, the household list and the master-list picker. All of them should read from **one bilingual kirana tree with three levels**: about 7 departments in the left rail, 3–5 category tiles under each (Tea, Dal, Dry fruits, Noodles, Fast food and Masala each get their own tile), and a short row of sub-category chips inside each category. The biggest gap is on the shop side. The bill screen has **no category navigation at all**: it shows only 12 tiles on a phone (30 on wide screens), so most of the 958-item kirana list can only be found by search or barcode. Supplier orders use a type-ahead that "only an exact name picks" ([BillScreen.tsx](frontend/src/screens/BillScreen.tsx); [SupplierOrders.tsx](frontend/src/screens/SupplierOrders.tsx)). The screenshot's pattern (department rail, grouped sub-category tiles) is a good skeleton, but it is a marketplace pattern. A kirana version can beat it in six ways. It can show only what this shop stocks, with counts. It can pin the shop's fast movers first. It can label everything in Hindi first with English beside it. It can let the user add items without leaving the menu. It can keep sibling sub-categories one tap apart. And it can carry filters that fit the job, such as "low stock" when ordering from a supplier. The hard part is not the layout. Today the app runs **three separate category vocabularies** (16 aisles, 18 Hindi-only master categories, and each shop's own category table), and none of them covers the whole kirana list. Unifying them is the first build step. The full plan comes to roughly **10–14 developer days**. One caution: competitor layouts here come from search snippets, URL structures and third-party teardowns, not verified screenshots, so the measurements are starting values to test.

## Seven screens pick items, and the shop-side ones are search-only

The code audit found seven places where items are picked. Only three browse by category, and none has the full rail, tiles and listing layout from the screenshot. The table shows what each has today and what the menu should do there.

| Screen | Today | What the category menu adds | Priority |
|---|---|---|---|
| Bill screen (`BillScreen.tsx`, 1,543 lines) | Most-sold tiles capped at 12 on a phone and 30 on wide screens; search, barcode, `2*aata` quantity syntax; **no categories or filters** | "☰ सब विभाग" button beside search on phones; a persistent left rail on desktop; tap-to-add from the listing straight into the bill | **P1** |
| Supplier orders and reorder (`SupplierOrders.tsx`, `ItemPicker.tsx`) | One row per line, type-ahead with 8 name matches, no photos or categories | Browse in multi-add mode with "Low stock" and "This supplier" chips; picks become order rows | **P1** |

The bill screen comes first because it is used all day and fails silently. A shopkeeper who forgets whether the product is "Tata Tea Gold" or "Tata Gold Tea" can only guess letters, and an item outside the top 12 never shows up as a tile. Supplier orders share first place for a related reason: reordering means walking the shelf in your head, aisle by aisle, and an exact-name type-ahead is the worst tool for that. Search stays the main path for anyone who knows what they want. One Blinkit case study cites an Adobe figure that **73% of online grocery shoppers use search as their primary way to find products** ([UXM / Medium](https://medium.com/uxm-community/aim-to-improve-the-search-flow-of-blinkit-app-da8e8338a243)). The menu is the path for everyone else, and for the moments when the user is scanning a whole aisle. A student teardown of Instamart likewise found that most users spend their time browsing categories ([Nextleap](https://assets.nextleap.app/submissions/NLInstamart-b491df67-a706-4695-ae28-957845ec46ce.pdf)).

## Three category vocabularies must collapse into one tree first

Today's data cannot drive a single menu. The global `Aisles` tree has 4 sections and 16 aisles, with Hindi and English names on the server and Gujarati names in `aisleNames.ts`, but it covers only the roughly 70-item common catalogue ([CommonCatalogue.cs:31-58](backend/src/GroceryKhata.Api/Catalogue/CommonCatalogue.cs); [aisleNames.ts](frontend/src/lib/aisleNames.ts)). The kirana master list has **958 items in 18 categories, stored as Hindi-only strings, with no sub-category or sort order** ([kirana-master.csv](backend/src/GroceryKhata.Api/Catalogue/Data/kirana-master.csv); [MasterItems.cs:14-54](backend/src/GroceryKhata.Api/Catalogue/MasterItems.cs)). Each shop also has its own `Category` table, which has a one-level `ParentId`, but no picker uses it and it has no icon or translated names ([Categories.cs:15-45](backend/src/GroceryKhata.Api/Catalogue/Categories.cs)). The 18 master groups and the 16 aisles do not line up. Dry fruits, pickles, chocolates, baby care and oral care have no aisle, and "आटा, चावल, दाल" spans two aisles. So the store probably shows "Atta & rice" (an aisle) next to "आटा, चावल, दाल" (a shop category), and that duplication would carry into any new menu built on top.

The fix is small because the link already exists. Shop products carry `MasterItemId`, set when an item is picked from the master list and back-filled by name matching ([Product.cs:168](backend/src/GroceryKhata.Api/Catalogue/Product.cs); [MasterItems.cs:495-515](backend/src/GroceryKhata.Api/Catalogue/MasterItems.cs)). Add a `node` key column to the kirana CSV and to `MasterItem`, and every master-linked product lands in the right sub-category automatically. Products the shop typed by hand fall back in order: the shop category's new `TreeKey` (set once on the existing Categories screen), then a keyword rule on the name ("dal", "चाय", "noodles"), then "बाकी सामान / Other". Brand and pack size stay off the tree, as the earlier catalogue research recommended. They become filters.

### The kirana tree: 7 departments, 20 categories, sub-chips only where a category is large

All the Indian apps converge on the same groups: atta, rice and dal; masala and dry fruits; tea and drinks; packaged food and noodles; snacks. Their labels join two or three nouns with "&" ([Zepto Atta, Rice, Oil & Dals](https://www.zepto.com/category/atta-rice-oil-dals); [BigBasket Foodgrains, Oil & Masala](https://www.bigbasket.com/cl/foodgrains-oil-masala/?nc=nb)). Blinkit's URLs encode a strict category → sub-category pair. For example, `atta-rice-dal/toor-urad-chana` is ID 16/1010, and dry fruits are 1557/1160 under a masala parent ([Blinkit Toor, Urad & Chana](https://blinkit.com/cn/atta-rice-dal/toor-urad-chana/cid/16/1010); [Blinkit Dry Fruits](https://blinkit.com/cn/masala-oil-more/dry-fruits/cid/1557/1160)). Zepto's sub-category counts show how deep a real assortment goes: 11 under "Tea, Coffee & More" and 8 under "Masala, Dry Fruits & More" ([Zepto Tea](https://www.zepto.com/category/tea-coffee-more); [Zepto Masala](https://www.zepto.com/category/masala-dry-fruits-more)). Baymard finds users start to feel overwhelmed beyond about **10 options at one level**. It recommends subdividing at that point and aiming for at least 10 products in each deepest category ([Baymard](https://baymard.com/blog/ecommerce-navigation-best-practice)). Those two thresholds set the shape below. The rail has about 7 departments plus one pinned entry. Each department has 2–5 tiles. Sub-chips appear only in categories big enough to need them.

## The screen layout: the screenshot's skeleton, rebuilt for a counter

The screenshot puts departments in the left rail and grouped sub-category tiles on the right. That resembles the Amazon/Flipkart "All categories" screen more than Blinkit's or Zepto's categories tab. Those apps put grouped tiles on the categories screen and move the left rail down a level, onto the sub-categories of the product listing ([v0 Blinkit-style brief](https://v0.app/deliverylads23-4818s-projects/chat/duplicate-of-new-version-15-aug-TuPMmBwbQCw); [GitHub Blinkit clone](https://github.com/Mehul721/-BlinkitAssignment)). The recommended design keeps the screenshot's top screen, because a vertical rail holds 7–10 departments without hiding any. That matters because horizontal tab strips leave some tabs off-screen at phone widths ([Baymard](https://baymard.com/research-articles/avoid-horizontal-tabs)). For the listing, it borrows the quick-commerce rule that changing sub-category swaps the content in place rather than opening a new screen.

On a 360 px phone the menu opens as a full-screen sheet over the bill or order, never as a route change, so the cart is never lost. The left rail is about **80 px wide**. Each department shows a two-tone SVG icon over a two-line Hindi label with small English underneath, and the active department has a tinted background and a left bar. The right pane shows the department's category tiles, **3 per row**, under a bold heading. Each tile has a pastel tint, a photo (the shop's own best-selling item in that category, else the master photo at 320 px, else the SVG), the Hindi name, the English name in lighter type, and a count of items in stock. Three columns suit compact tiles that carry no long text, as the image-grid research found ([UX Movement](https://uxmovement.com/?p=8647)). The pinned "★ रोज़ के" entry sits at the top of the rail, in the slot every quick-commerce app gives to "Top Picks" or "Hot Deals" ([Blinkit Hot Deals](https://blinkit.com/cn/atta-rice-dal/hot-deals/cid/16/16); [Zepto Tea](https://www.zepto.com/category/tea-coffee-more)).

Tapping a tile opens the listing inside the same sheet. A sticky header holds "‹ दाल · Dal" and a search box scoped to it ("दाल में खोजें"). Below it are a row of sub-category chips (सब · तूर · मूंग · चना…) and a row of filter chips. The items follow, shown per screen. The bill and supplier screens use dense 56–64 px rows with a 40 px thumbnail, a −/qty/+ stepper and stock shown, because the job is speed across many items. The earlier picker research made the same call for the same reason ([Master list picker redesign](reports/Master%20list%20picker%20redesign.md)). The store uses the 2-column, 164 px photo card, because browsing photos is the point there ([Product image grid for mobile](reports/Product%20image%20grid%20for%20mobile.md)). A dark tray at the bottom ("5 आइटम · ₹640 · बिल पर लौटें →") counts every add and closes the sheet. On desktop the bill screen keeps a persistent 180–220 px rail on the left of its tile grid, the same pattern already shipped in `PickItemsScreen` and the kirana store, and F2 still focuses search ([PickItemsScreen.tsx:166-171](frontend/src/screens/PickItemsScreen.tsx)).

### Six ways this beats the screenshot

| Screenshot ("All categories") | Apna Kaarobar menu | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Shows every department the platform sells (Nutrition & Healthcare, Household Supplies…) | Shows only departments and tiles where **this shop has stock**, each with a count | No dead ends. Blinkit's CMS also hides empty sub-categories ([Blinkit CMS](https://www.prakhyakant.com/blinkit-cms)) |
| Generic, platform-ranked tiles | "★ रोज़ के" fast movers pinned first; within each tile, most-sold first | Re-ordering is the main grocery behaviour; BigBasket's Smart Basket auto-collects frequent buys ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/in/app/id660683603)) |
| English labels | Hindi first, English beside it, Gujarati from the same key | Users who don't know a product's English name can't place it in an English-labelled category ([Zepto heuristic evaluation](https://assets.nextleap.app/submissions/HeuristicEvaluationofZepto_UXCaseStudyofanOnlineGroceryApp_byAkshhatJaiin_Apr2025_Medium-d31f9cf7-6cf9-4938-9723-540fdb8bda05.pdf)) |
| Tap a sub-category tile, then land on a separate product page | Stepper on every row, and the cart tray never leaves | Quick-commerce ADD-to-stepper cards update the cart in place ([Recreating Blinkit PLP](https://medium.com/@srirammanogar07/understanding-ui-design-by-recreating-the-product-listing-page-of-blinkit-a-case-study-66fff1e951b)) |
| Changing from Protein to Vitamins means going back | Sibling sub-categories are chips at the top of the listing | Teardowns flag "too many taps" to reach grocery sub-categories ([Zepto heuristic evaluation](https://assets.nextleap.app/submissions/HeuristicEvaluationofZepto_UXCaseStudyofanOnlineGroceryApp_byAkshhatJaiin_Apr2025_Medium-d31f9cf7-6cf9-4938-9723-540fdb8bda05.pdf)) |
| Shopping filters only | Job-specific filters: "Low stock" when ordering from a supplier, "In stock" when billing, "Offer" and "Veg" in the store | A shopkeeper ordering stock needs reorder level, not ratings |

Tiles need clear words more than pictures. One Zepto tester scanned category titles and ignored the images ([Zepto heuristic evaluation](https://assets.nextleap.app/submissions/HeuristicEvaluationofZepto_UXCaseStudyofanOnlineGroceryApp_byAkshhatJaiin_Apr2025_Medium-d31f9cf7-6cf9-4938-9723-540fdb8bda05.pdf)). NN/g found that simple, flat outline icons helped low-English Indian participants find categories ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/international-shoppers-ecommerce-sites/)). Hence SVG icons on the rail, real photos on the tiles, and a label that always carries the meaning. Item counts confused some Zepto testers. Show them as a small grey number, and drop them if the first shopkeeper tests show the same confusion.

## Search and filters: one engine, facets from the tree, chips per job

The app has three search implementations of different quality. The store uses `rank` from `lib/search.ts`, which already holds a hand-made Hindi, Gujarati and Hinglish synonym table (doodh→milk, haldi→turmeric, jeera→cumin) plus edit-distance tolerance ([search.ts](frontend/src/lib/search.ts)). The bill screen uses `nameMatches`, which works across scripts ("aata" finds "आटा"). The picker uses word-prefix Hinglish matching. `ItemPicker` needs an exact name ([BillScreen.tsx:58-65](frontend/src/screens/BillScreen.tsx); [PickItemsScreen.tsx:22-36](frontend/src/screens/PickItemsScreen.tsx)). The menu should run on one engine, `rank`, extended in three ways. First, index each item's tree path, so typing "dal" ranks dal items and also shows a "दाल विभाग देखें →" suggestion at the top. Second, show the matching sub-categories as facet chips with counts above the results. NN/g notes that facets turn search back into navigation and that counts prevent zero-result combinations ([NN/g Filters vs Facets](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/filters-vs-facets/)). Third, inside a category, search that category by default and offer a one-tap "सब जगह खोजें" to widen it.

The evidence backs this cheap, dictionary-first route over machine learning. Zepto's LLM spellchecker exists because phonetic and vernacular spellings ("kothimbir") break normal search. It reportedly lifted conversion 7.5% on the affected queries, and it learns from quick rewrites such as "banan chips" → "banana chips" ([Analytics India Magazine](https://analyticsindiamag.com/ai-features/zeptos-spellchecker-fixes-errors-with-a-little-help-from-llms/)). Flipkart translates Hinglish queries but leaves brand words untouched, so "veer brand oil" keeps its brand ([arXiv 2208.03713](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2208.03713v1.pdf)). A local alias table plus edit distance gets most of that benefit offline. Two cheap additions copy the Zepto idea: log zero-result queries and quick rewrites, so the owner or admin can grow `SYNONYMS`; and keep brand names out of synonym expansion. No query should dead-end. The bill screen should offer "did you mean" plus "add as a typed item", and the store already offers "Ask the shop on WhatsApp" ([StoreApp.tsx:402-407](frontend/src/screens/store/StoreApp.tsx)). With about 1,000 items, keep the whole index in memory, debounce typing by about 120–150 ms, and run filtering in `startTransition`.

Filters follow Baymard's mobile rules. Promote the important ones as one-tap chips, and keep them in their original place in the full sheet as well. Put the rest in a bottom sheet with an explicit "Show N items" button. Show applied filters as removable chips above the list, and provide "Clear all" ([Baymard promoting filters](https://baymard.com/blog/promoting-product-filters); [Baymard guideline 1125](https://baymard.com/guidelines/1125-apply-buttons-in-mobile-filtering-interfaces); [Baymard applied filters](https://baymard.com/blog/how-to-design-applied-filters)). Allow several values within one filter, such as two brands ([Baymard](https://baymard.com/research-articles/allow-applying-of-multiple-filter-values)). The chip set should change with the job, because a one-size filter bar would bury the one filter each screen needs.

| Screen | Quick chips | Bottom sheet | Default sort |
|---|---|---|---|
| Bill | In stock · Offer · top 4–6 brands in this category | Pack size buckets, price range | Most sold (existing `usePopularOrder`) |
| Supplier order | **Low stock / below reorder** · This supplier · Last ordered | Brand, pack size | Lowest days-of-cover first |
| Store | In stock · Offer · Veg · Bought before | Brand, pack size, max ₹ | Relevance, then price; add ₹/kg |
| Household list / picker | In shop · Not yet added | — | Category order |

The store needs a deliberate reversal. Its filter drawer is hidden for kirana today (`{!kirana && …}`) on the assumption that the rail is enough ([StoreApp.tsx:387, 472-485](frontend/src/screens/store/StoreApp.tsx)). With sub-category chips taking over from the rail, the sheet should come back for kirana too. Brand and pack-size facets are where the 958 branded items differ from one another. The veg mark is a cheap filter only if it is stored. The master CSV has no veg column today, so treat Veg as later work.

## Hindi and English names: one key, three scripts, everyday words

Every node in the tree should carry a stable key (`dal`, `dal.toor`, `masala.powder`) and three names. The server holds Hindi and English, as `Aisles` already does. Gujarati sits in the frontend map, following the `aisleName()` and `sectionName()` pattern that switches by app language ([aisleNames.ts](frontend/src/lib/aisleNames.ts)). That is the whole i18n design: shop and master strings stop being display labels, so a Gujarati or English user no longer sees Hindi-only kirana category names ([PickItemsScreen.tsx:78-79, 207](frontend/src/screens/PickItemsScreen.tsx)). On tiles and the rail, show the app language's name in bold, with English underneath in lighter type when the app language is not English. In English mode, show the Hindi name underneath instead, because many staples are best known by their Hindi names.

Use the words shopkeepers say, not dictionary Hindi. That means दाल, मसाले, सूखे मेवे, चाय, नूडल्स and फ़ास्ट फ़ूड, with loanwords simply written in Devanagari. Avoid formal coinages such as "त्वरित भोजन" for fast food. One practitioner blog warns that machine-translated Hindi reads stiff and recommends conversational Hinglish. It is an opinion piece, not tested research ([Induji Technologies](https://www.indujitechnologies.com/blog/building-bharat-first-apps-low-bandwidth)). The commercial signal is stronger: about **95% of Flipkart users who switched to its Hindi interface kept using it** ([YourStory](https://yourstory.com/2019/09/flipkart-hindi-platform-bharat-india-ecommerce)). Keep labels to two lines of about 14 characters at rail width. Use line-height 1.45 or more and never a fixed-height container, so matras do not clip, as the picker research specified. Every name also goes into the search index, so typing a category's name in any of the three scripts finds that category.

| 8 | **Art and measurement.** About 8 rail SVGs and 25 tile fallbacks, under 2 KB each; photo tiles from best sellers at 320 px; test on a ₹7–8k Android, with INP under 200 ms as the bar | assets, `itemArt.ts` | 1–2 days plus art | Replaces emoji, which render differently across Android versions |

Steps 1–4 (about **7 days**) deliver a working category menu on the bill screen, which is the change shopkeepers will feel. Steps 5–6 bring supplier ordering and search into line in about 2–3 more days. Step 7 is where duplicate aisle and category chips disappear for customers. Lists stay fast if each tile pane and listing uses `content-visibility: auto` and thumbnails stay at 320 px. TanStack Virtual is the fallback if a listing passes a few hundred rows on a slow phone. That follows Swiggy's and Jumbotail's experience that image memory and jank decide low-end performance ([Swiggy Bytes](https://bytes.swiggy.com/media-on-swiggys-mobile-apps-e97452fceb3f); [freeCodeCamp / Jumbotail](https://www.freecodecamp.org/news/how-we-reduced-memory-footprint-by-50-in-our-android-app-49efa5c93ad8)). Instrument from day one: menu opens per bill, taps from open to first add, searches that end in a menu open, and zero-result terms.

Several things remain unknown. The plan is read from code, not a running app. No shop's real data was checked, so how many products lack a master link, and will therefore fall to name rules, is unknown. Nobody has counted master-photo coverage either, and that coverage decides how many tiles show photos rather than SVGs. Competitor tile sizes and rail widths are unverified. The user's screenshot is described here, not measured.

## Conclusion

The request sounds like a new screen, but it is really a data decision with a screen on top. Once every product resolves to one keyed node in one bilingual tree, the menu is a single component that each ordering screen opens with its own filter chips. The same tree then also fixes the store's duplicate chips, the picker's Hindi-only labels and the household list's 70-item ceiling. That is why this plan beats the screenshot rather than copying it. A marketplace's "All categories" screen is a map of everything a platform sells. A kirana menu should be a map of one shop's shelf, ordered by what moves, labelled in the owner's language, with filters that change with the job.

The plan carries one strategic risk and one way to check it. The risk is over-building browse at the cost of speed. At a busy counter, search, barcode and the most-sold tiles will still carry most bills, and the menu has to stay one tap away without getting in their way. The check is cheap: put steps 1–4 in front of five kirana owners for a week. If menu opens per bill stay low but searches that end in "not found" fall, the menu is doing its real job, which is catching the items people cannot name.
