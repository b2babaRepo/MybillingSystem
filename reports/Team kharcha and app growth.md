# Turn Every Kharcha Entry Into an Invite

Grocery Khata should build "team kharcha" as **one shared book that several people write into**, not as a split-and-settle tool. Each entry carries the name of whoever added it. The book shows daily and monthly totals by person and by category, and the existing month report (PDF + WhatsApp) gains a name-wise page. The strongest early users are **shop owners with partners and helpers**, who need petty-cash advances, approvals and receipt photos, and **joint/family households**, who are the largest group and log ghar kharcha every day. Both groups already fit the app's khata habit, and neither is well served today. Splitwise caps free users at about 3–4 expenses a day, and Vyapar's multi-user mode is a paid staff-roles feature for billing, not a shared spending log. The growth case rests on a pattern Indian khata apps have already proven. OkCredit and Khatabook grew because every reminder went to a non-user as a WhatsApp/SMS link that **opened without an install**, and some of those recipients installed the app. Team kharcha should work the same way: an invite or daily summary goes out over WhatsApp, a read-only web page shows the team book, and the "install to add your own entries" prompt comes after that. Invited members stay free, the team owner pays for extra members, history and exports, and daily entries are never capped. Consent to share entries must be recorded at join time, because most DPDP Rules 2025 duties apply from about **mid-May 2027**. The research behind this was a set of short passes using mostly secondary sources and no user interviews, so the figures are indicative and the group priorities are inferences that need a pilot.

## Shop teams and families need a shared book, not a bill splitter

Shared-expense apps fall into two models. "Split and settle" apps (Splitwise, Tricount, Google Pay's bill split) compute who owes whom. Google Pay groups hold up to 20 people and are tied to payment requests ([Google Pay Help](https://support.google.com/pay/india/answer/11420982?hl=EN); [Smartprix](https://us.smartprix.com/bytes/how-to-use-google-pay-to-split-bills-other-expenses)), and reviewers found Splitwise better than Google Pay for tracking ongoing expenses ([XDA](https://www.xda-developers.com/google-pay-india-split-bill/)). "Staff logging into one business book" is Vyapar's model: **Sync & Share** invites staff by phone or email with fixed roles, and the owner gets a per-user activity log and reports by user ([Vyapar blog](https://vyaparapp.in/blog/user-roles-permissions-vyapar/)). That feature is paid ([Vyapar guide](https://vyaparapp.in/guides/how-to-use-vyapar-app-on-multiple-devices)), and users complain that the roles are too rigid ([Trustpilot](https://pt.trustpilot.com/review/vyaparapp.in)). Khatabook keeps staff in a separate app, Pagarkhata ([G2](https://www.g2.com/products/khatabook/discuss)). The research found no product that does a name-wise, daily/monthly shared spending log for small shops and households. Team kharcha fits that gap: it uses Vyapar's single-book model and borrows Splitwise's per-person view.

The main opening in this category is a resented paywall. Splitwise limits free users to **3 or 4 expenses a day** (sources disagree on the number), and Pro reportedly costs about **₹149/month or ₹999/year** in India ([ITVoice](https://www.itvoice.in/splitwise-has-introduced-restrictions-on-the-number-of-free-expenses-users-can-add); [Finny](https://getfinny.app/blog/splitwise-pricing-2026)). A household logging milk, vegetables and auto fares hits that cap before lunch. Grocery Khata's daily notebook already lets people log many small entries, and it should never limit them.

The user groups rank as follows. The ranking is inferred, because no India survey measures shared-expense tracking by group.

| Group | Frequency | What they need | Value to Grocery Khata |
|---|---|---|---|
| Shop owner + partners/helpers | Daily | Petty-cash advance per helper, running balance per person, owner approval, receipt photo, categories (transport, chai, packing, repairs) | Most likely to pay; owner/helper logins already exist |
| Family / joint household | Daily | Monthly ghar kharcha by person and category, Hindi voice entry, budgets, private entries | Largest number of users; feeds the household grocery side |
| Contractor / site / field team | Daily | Advance-and-settle, photo proof, offline entry, per-site tag | Same shape as shop petty cash; reuses the site khata idea from cement shops |
| Roommates / PG / hostel | Weekly | Who-owes-whom, UPI settle-up, fixed rent split | Splitwise's turf; worth winning only through its cap |
| Trips, weddings, festivals | Short bursts | Temporary team, many invitees, pool, final PDF | Good for virality, poor for revenue |
| SHG / RWA / temple committee | Monthly | Treasurer approval, receipts, contributions vs spends | Very large but already served by NABARD EShakti and society apps |

The committee row deserves caution, not dismissal. SHGs support the livelihoods of **14.2 crore households** and held more than **₹47,240 crore** in savings as of FY2022 ([NABARD](https://www.nabard.org/pdf/fostering-grassroots-institutions-eng.pdf)), and NABARD's EShakti is digitising their books ([TERI](https://www.teriin.org/news/nabard-commences-digitisation-shgs)). Society apps charge per flat, with ApnaComplex starting at ₹499 a month, and one comparison calls MyGate's accounting "basic" ([codingclave](https://codingclave.com/blog/best-society-management-app-india-2026)). Both groups have their own channels and formal audit needs. A simple contributions-vs-spends export is enough for them until shops and families are working.

## Build one team book, a WhatsApp invite and a no-install viewer

The core object is a **Team** that owns a kharcha book. Members have a role (Owner, Admin, Member, Viewer), and every entry stores the author, timestamp, amount, mode (cash/UPI), category, an optional photo and an optional "for whom" tag. The existing Today's kharcha screen gains a team switcher (My kharcha / Dukaan team / Ghar). The day view groups lines by name with subtotals, and the month report adds a name × category table. Entry stays the one-line voice/text format from the earlier expense notebook work ("chai 40, auto 120, thaili 250 upi"), now tagged with the speaker's name. A few features matter to every group. Sync must work offline first. An **edit/delete history visible to all members** is essential, because Tricount shows what happens without it: anyone holding its open link can delete expenses ([Tricount](https://tricount.com/en/who-can-access-my-tricount)). Each team also needs a daily and month summary that can be shared to WhatsApp.

Shop teams then get three extra modules. **Advance and settle** lets the owner give a helper ₹2,000 in the morning, the helper logs spends against it, and the balance due back shows at evening cash count. **Approval** puts helper entries above an owner-set amount in a pending state. **Per-helper monthly limits** warn before a helper overspends. Households get per-category budgets, pocket money for children, and a "private" flag so a member's entry counts in the total without showing its details. A roommate/trip mode (owes vs tracking, UPI settle-up link) can come later as a switch on the same Team, without becoming a separate product.

The invite flow follows the least-friction pattern in the category. Tricount needs no account at all ([Tricount FAQ](https://www.tricount.com/en/do-i-need-a-login-or-account)), Settle Up lets non-users see balances in a browser ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/id737534985)), and Olik Split uses an 8-character code in a QR that expires after 48 hours ([Olik Split](https://oliksplit.app/features/group-sharing)). For Grocery Khata the flow is:

1. The owner taps **Invite**. A Hindi/English WhatsApp share intent opens with a deep link and a 6–8 character code that expires in 48 hours. A QR on screen covers helpers standing in the shop.
2. The link opens a lightweight web page that shows the team name, who invited the person, and a **read-only view** of the team summary. No install is needed to look.
3. To add entries, the joiner installs the app, signs in with phone OTP, and sees the consent notice described below before joining.
4. The owner approves the joiner and sets the role. An invite link alone never grants write access.

The owner can also add people who will never install the app, such as an elderly parent or a part-time helper, as **non-login named members**. The owner enters on their behalf. This keeps name-wise reports complete and gives a simple answer for minors.

## Consent at join time keeps team sharing on the right side of DPDP

The DPDP Rules were notified on **14 Nov 2025**. The rules on notice, security, breach, retention/erasure and children's data (Rules 3 and 5–16) take effect after 18 months, around mid-May 2027 ([SCC Online](https://www.scconline.com/blog/post/2025/12/26/digital-personal-data-protection-rules-2025-key-highlights/); [KPMG](https://assets.kpmg.com/content/dam/kpmgsites/in/pdf/2025/11/dpdp-rules-2025-guidance-to-dpdp-act-implementation.pdf)). Building to them now is cheaper than retrofitting. On withdrawal of consent the fiduciary must erase the data, including at its processors, unless the law requires keeping it (Section 8(7)), and Section 12 gives rights to correction and erasure ([dpdpa.com Section 8](https://www.dpdpa.com/dpdpa2023/chapter-2/section8.html); [chapter 3](https://dpdpa.com/dpdpa2023/chapter-3/chapter3.html)). Processing data of anyone **under 18** needs verifiable parental consent, and tracking or targeted ads aimed at children are barred, with penalties up to ₹200 crore ([dpdpa.com Section 9](https://www.dpdpa.com/dpdpa2023/chapter-2/section9.html); [Schedule](https://dpdpa.com/theschedule.html)).

In the product this means the following. The join screen shows a short bilingual notice: "आपकी entries, नाम और amount [team] के सदस्यों को दिखेंगे / Your entries, name and amounts will be visible to members of [team]". The member taps agree, and the app stores that consent with a timestamp and notice version. One tap in Settings withdraws it, which is as easy as giving it. Leaving a team stops sync at once, and the leaver chooses between deleting their entries and keeping them in the team's books as "Former member". For shop teams, the notice says that business records may be kept for accounts and tax. Self-declared minors cannot join as login members, and a parent adds them as non-login names instead, which avoids building age verification. Receipt photos stay visible to the team only and are deleted with their entry. Team expense data is never used for offers or lending signals without a separate, explicit opt-in. Two points are still open. Whether a family team falls under the "personal or domestic purpose" exemption is unclear, and that exemption covers individuals, not the app operator. The gazette text was not checked directly, so a lawyer should confirm the dates.

## Charge the owner, keep invitees free and never cap entries

Indian users pay roughly **₹100–150/month or ₹600–1,000/year** for consumer expense tools (the Splitwise India figures above). Shop software costs more: Vyapar Desktop Gold is listed at ₹3,399/year plus GST, and older mobile plans were about ₹599–942/year ([Capterra](https://www.capterra.com/p/180579/Vyapar/pricing/); [itforsme](https://www.itforsme.in/pricing/vyapar-india)). Khatabook's core ledger is free ([Techjockey](https://techjockey.com/compare/khatabook-vs-vyapar)). Every invitee is a potential install, so invitees should never pay. The suggested tiers below are inferences, because no Indian willingness-to-pay data was found for team plans.

| Tier | Who pays | Includes |
|---|---|---|
| Free (all plans, as today) | Nobody | Personal kharcha + **1 team, up to 3–4 members**, unlimited daily entries, 2 months of team history, name-wise day/month view, WhatsApp summary |
| Team Pro (~₹49–99/month or ₹499–999/year) | Team owner | More members and teams, unlimited history, PDF/Excel name-wise report, receipt photos, approvals, advances, per-helper limits |
| Bundled in existing shop plans | Shop owner | Team Pro for the shop's helper team, which strengthens the owners-and-helpers plan already shipped |
| Event pass (~₹99 one-time) | Organiser | Wedding/trip team with many members for 60 days and a final PDF |

The paid features (receipts/OCR, full history, charts, exports) are the same ones Splitwise Pro charges for ([Finny](https://getfinny.app/blog/splitwise-pricing-2026)), so users already understand them. Basic entry stays free. Revenue beyond subscriptions, such as distributor offers to shops or cash-flow-based credit, is possible, but it needs its own DPDP consent and should come only after the core product has earned trust.

## Growth comes from links that work before the install

The best-documented Indian SMB loop is the reminder loop. OkCredit's investors report that **3 out of every 10 customers** a merchant reminds is another merchant, and that **2 organic merchants join for every paid one** ([Lightspeed](https://lsvp.com/stories/empowering-indias-60m-offline-merchants-2/)). OkCredit's reminders carry a UPI pay link, and customers do not need the app ([OkCredit](https://okcredit.in/payment-reminder-app)). An unverified analyst estimate says Khatabook's 2M merchants sent about 10M SMSes with web links, which together with influencer campaigns added more than 3M merchants in 9–10 months ([Flyy](https://www.theflyy.com/blog/growth-hacking-the-rise-of-bookkeeping-fintech-apps)). ShareChat attached a Play Store link to every WhatsApp share and got about **25% of early installs from APK shares** ([Social Samosa](https://www.socialsamosa.com/2018/03/social-media-platform-feature-sharechat)). Meesho made every reseller a distributor to their own WhatsApp network ([upGrowth](https://upgrowth.in/meesho-built-social-commerce-india-gtm-strategy-teardown/)).

Sending through the user's own WhatsApp share intent is free. Through the WhatsApp Business API, marketing templates cost about **₹0.86** each and utility templates about **₹0.115** each (Jan 2026), plus 18% GST and a provider fee ([ecorpit](https://ecorpit.com/whatsapp-business-api-cost-india-d2c-2026/); [Meta](https://developers.facebook.com/documentation/business-messaging/whatsapp/pricing)). Every loop below therefore uses share intents by default and the API only for opt-in utility alerts.

The loops for Grocery Khata are as follows. In the **team invite loop**, each team brings 2–4 new people through a link they can view before installing. In the **daily summary loop**, an evening Hindi image card ("आज दुकान का खर्च ₹1,240 — Ramesh ₹600, Sonu ₹640") goes to the team's WhatsApp group with a "view full book" link, so members who haven't installed see the brand every day. In the **month report loop**, the existing kharcha PDF carries a name-wise page and a footer link. People forward it to an accountant, a partner or a parent, and each forward reaches a new audience. In the **shop-to-household loop**, the kirana's customers are households. A dues reminder or bill link from the shop can invite the customer to "keep your ghar kharcha and grocery list free here", and a household that joins strengthens its tie to that shop. The **Splitwise switcher loop** markets "no daily limit, Hindi voice" to roommates and families who hit the cap.

Beyond team kharcha, several ideas give non-users a reason to open a link. A **shared household grocery list** lets family members add items and send the list straight to their kirana on Grocery Khata, which turns household users into orders for shops. A **festival/wedding kharcha book** with a printable final summary brings many invitees at once. A **helper salary and advance page** builds on Pagarkhata's opening and the existing helper logins. A **digital shop card/QR** in the Khatabook style ([Flyy](https://www.theflyy.com/blog/growth-hacking-the-rise-of-bookkeeping-fintech-apps)) gives the app something to share every week. Referral rewards should be non-cash, such as Team Pro months, paid only after the referee has logged entries on several days and limited per device and phone number. Tez paid about **₹102 per user** through its ₹51+₹51 model ([Flyy](https://theflyy.com/blog/viral-referral-program-planning-and-development)), NPCI had to stop people farming cashback with self-transfers ([Entrackr](https://entrackr.com/?p=20751)), and incentivised referrals rarely create lasting virality ([eChai](https://echai.ventures/glossary/viral-coefficient)).

The phased plan is as follows. **Phase 1 (4–6 weeks):** Team model, roles, author on every entry, a team switcher on Today's kharcha, a name-wise day/month view and a name-wise page in the PDF, the WhatsApp invite with code/QR and owner approval, the consent notice with timestamped records, a leave/erase flow and the audit history, all on Free with the 3–4 member limit. **Phase 2 (next 6 weeks):** the read-only web viewer, the evening Hindi summary card, shop modules (advance/settle, approval, helper limits, receipt photos), Team Pro billing and bundling into shop plans. **Phase 3:** the shared grocery list sent to the kirana, household budgets and private entries, the event pass, roommate settle-up through UPI links, non-cash referral rewards and a committee export. Measure each phase by invites sent per team, the share of invite links opened, the share of viewers who install, the share of teams with 2+ active writers in week 4, and Team Pro conversion among owners. No benchmark for these exists in the research, so the Phase 1 pilot sets the baseline.

## Conclusion

Team kharcha matters for growth more than as a feature, because it turns Grocery Khata's single-user notebook into a multi-person habit. The success test is whether non-users get something useful before they install. That is why the viewer and the daily card in Phase 2 matter more for growth than any paid module. The shop-to-household link is the idea no competitor has. Splitwise has no shops, and Khatabook and Vyapar have no households, so a single shared book that connects a family's kharcha to its kirana's khata is the strongest reason for many more people to install the app.

The biggest unknown is behavioural, not technical: will helpers and family members really log entries themselves, or will one person keep writing for everyone? Non-login named members handle the second case without blocking the first. A month-long pilot with about 20 kirana teams and 20 families should answer that question before pricing and Phase 3 are finalised.
