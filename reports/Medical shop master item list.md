# Seed the chemist list, flag every rate

Grocery Khata's medical shop master list should hold about **700 seed rows in four groups**: roughly 260 allopathic medicines led by high-volume acute brands (Dolo, Pan, Augmentin, Azithral, Montair), about 220 Ayurvedic, wellness, sexual-health and baby-care items (Dabur, Himalaya, Patanjali, Baidyanath, Zandu, Durex, Manforce), and about 250 devices and surgical items laid out by body part, from cervical collars to heel cushions, plus a water and heat therapy group. The tax picture is simpler than it was a year ago: since **22 September 2025, retail-packed medicines under HSN 3004 carry 5% GST, down from 12%**, Ayurvedic, Unani and homoeopathic medicaments sit in the same 5% slab, and most Chapter 90 medical devices (9018, 9019, 9021) also moved to 5%. That lets the app default most rows to 5% with confidence. The hard part is the edges: condoms and sanitary pads are believed to be nil-rated, health drinks, glucose powders, antiseptic liquids, sanitisers, hot-water bags, steam vaporisers and weighing scales sit under other headings with unconfirmed rates, and the drug schedule (H, H1, NDPS) must drive a prescription prompt at billing. Every brand, pack size and schedule in the notes comes from market knowledge, not page-by-page checks, so the list is a seed for shopkeepers to confirm, not a final catalogue. The right design is a generic item plus brand and pack variants, with a visible "verify" flag on every row whose GST or HSN is not settled.

## Sales rankings say lead with acute brands, not the costliest ones

India's biggest brands by annual value are **Augmentin (GSK) at Rs 816 crore and Glycomet GP (USV) at Rs 803 crore in FY25**, in a market that grew 8.4% mostly through price increases ([Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/industry/news/robust-chronic-performance-drives-8-4-growth-for-indian-pharma-mkt-in-fy25-125040801060_1.html)). By December 2025 their moving annual totals had reached Rs 889 crore and Rs 871 crore, while Eli Lilly's obesity drug **Mounjaro led the month at Rs 105 crore** ([BioSpectrum / Pharmarack](https://biospectrumindia.com/news/16/27158/mounjaro-eli-lilly-leads-ipm-for-dec-2025-as-no-1-brand-with-sales-of-rs-105-cr-pharmarack-.html)). That headline misleads a shop app: in October 2025 Mounjaro sold about **85,000 units against about 5.8 million for Augmentin** ([Angel One](https://www.angelone.in/news/market-updates/eli-lilly-s-obesity-drug-mounjaro-tops-india-s-drug-sales-in-october)). A neighbourhood chemist bills Augmentin, Dolo and Pan dozens of times a day and Mounjaro rarely, so search order and starter lists should rank by unit volume, not rupee value. The full IQVIA and AWACS top-300 lists are paywalled and were not obtained.

The schedule column needs care. India has no formal OTC schedule yet, so "OTC" in the notes means sold without prescription in practice, and every schedule value is the usual classification for the salt rather than a check of the pack label. Benzodiazepines such as alprazolam and clonazepam are H1 and NDPS-controlled, which means the app should demand a prescription record and keep a register, not just show a warning.

## A 5% default holds for medicines, Ayurveda and Chapter 90 devices

The rate cut came out of the **56th GST Council meeting**, which moved most medicines from 12% to 5% and exempted specified cancer and rare-disease drugs ([Business Today](https://www.businesstoday.in/industry/pharma/story/cancer-rare-disease-medicines-exempted-from-gst-other-drugs-at-5-check-faqs-492416-2025-09-03)). HSN 3004 covers retail-packed formulations and 3003 covers bulk ([Tally](https://tallysolutions.com/gst/hsn-code-3004-product-classification-gst-rate-business-filing-guide/)). Medicaments of the Ayurvedic, Unani, Siddha and homoeopathic systems are also in the 5% slab ([Cleartax](https://cleartax.in/s/gst-rates)), so Dabur Chyawanprash, Honitus, Liv.52, Cystone, Ashokarishta and Hamdard Safi can share the medicine default. One caution applies: at least one accounting source warns against treating "3004 equals 5%" as a blanket rule ([Accountune](https://accountune.com/medicine-gst-rate-list)), because about 36 to 37 life-saving drugs are nil-rated and that exact list was not verified.

Devices follow the same pattern. Blood glucose monitoring systems and test strips are listed at 5% under Notification 9/2025-Central Tax (Rate) ([Patron Accounting](https://www.patronaccounting.com/hsn-code/90189019)), HSN 9018 medical and surgical instruments is summarised at 5% ([Mark IT Solutions](https://markitsolutions-website.pages.dev/hsn/9018)), and 9019 mechano-therapy apparatus and 9021 orthopaedic devices are listed at 5% ([Razorpay](https://razorpay.com/learn/gst-on-medicines/)). One site lists oxygen therapy apparatus (9019 20) at 0% ([Disytax](https://disytax.com/hsn-chapter-90-optical-medical-instruments-gst/)), which conflicts with the wider 5% listing and remains unverified. Personal-care goods a chemist also stocks, including **toothpaste, soap, shampoo, talc and hair oil, fell from 18% to 5%** ([Cleartax comparison](https://cleartax.in/s/new-vs-old-gst-rates-comparison)), and feeding bottles and baby napkins went from 12% to 5% ([Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/economy/news/new-gst-rate-cut-what-gets-cheaper-expensive-5-18-percent-125092200215_1.html)).

## Wellness and sexual health split into three tax buckets

The Ayurvedic and wellness group falls into three buckets, and the app should treat them differently. The first is **5% medicaments under 3004**: Dabur's Chyawanprash (500 g and 1 kg jars), Honitus syrup, Pudin Hara pearls, Lal Tail, Shilajit Gold, Triphala and Ashwagandha churna; Himalaya's Liv.52, Septilin, Cystone, Speman, Evecare and Bonnisan; Patanjali and Divya's Giloy Ghanvati and Madhunashini Vati; Baidyanath's arishtas; Zandu Balm; Charak, Hamdard and Kerala Ayurveda oils and tonics. These are listed as "5?" because each product's classification was not confirmed, though the slab itself is cited. The second is **5% personal care under 3304, 3305, 3306 and 3401**: Dabur Meswak and Red Paste, Amla hair oil, Himalaya face washes, baby soaps, shampoos and powders. The third is the **unresolved food and drink group**: Hajmola, Dabur Honey (0409), Glucon-D and Glucose-D (1702), Horlicks, Ensure, Pediasure and Bournvita (1901, 1806 or 2106), Rooh Afza, Patanjali juices and Sat Isabgol (1211). Their rates were not established and they must carry a verify flag.

Sexual wellness should be its own category. Condoms (HSN 4014) cover Durex Extra Thin, Air and Extra Dots, Manforce flavoured 10-packs, KamaSutra, Moods and Nirodh from HLL Lifecare, and Skore from TTK Healthcare, usually in packs of 10 or 12. Cleartax lists **contraceptives and condoms among nil-rated items** ([Cleartax](https://cleartax.in/s/gst-rates)), but only a snippet was seen and no second source confirmed it, so the rows carry "0?". Lubricants (Durex Play, K-Y Jelly) and delay gels (Manforce Staylong) have no settled HSN or rate. Emergency contraceptives (i-pill, Unwanted-72) and oral pills (Mala-D, Saheli) are 3004 medicaments at 5%, but their schedule status needs checking against the pack. Sildenafil (Manforce 50, Suhagra 50) belongs in the medicine group as Schedule H. Pregnancy and ovulation test kits (Prega News, i-Can, Velocit) sit under 3822 at 5%. Sanitary pads, panty liners and menstrual cups (9619) are believed exempt since 2018, but that was not re-verified, and menstrual cups may fall under 3926 or 4014 instead.

Water and heat therapy is the least certain group, because its items scatter across five HSN headings. Rubber **hot-water bottles (2 litre) and ice bags fall under 4014** with unknown rates; electric gel heat bags, steam vaporisers and bottle sterilisers fall under **8516, probably still 18%**; electric orthopaedic heating pads and heating belts are listed under 9019 at a probable 5%; hot and cold gel packs and instant cold packs sit between 3824 and 9019. Sitz bath tubs, enema cans and pumps, ear syringes, neti pots and eye wash cups straddle 9018 (5%) and plastic headings 3922 or 3924. Waterproof plasters and shower-proof film dressings are safe at 5% under 3005. Personal and baby weighing scales (8423) were not part of the medical cut and are probably still 18%.

## What still has to be checked before loading

Four uncertainties block a production load. First, **no row was checked against a live catalogue**: brand ownership, pack sizes and strengths come from general knowledge of 1mg, PharmEasy and Netmeds-style listings, and a few makers (Playgard, Woodward's, some generics) are explicitly unconfirmed. Second, **the CBIC rate notification itself was not read**, so the unsettled items are thermometers (9025), gloves (4015), masks (6307), diapers and pads (9619), hot-water and ice bags (4014), vaporisers (8516), scales (8423), surgical spirit, hydrogen peroxide, antiseptic liquids and sanitisers (3808 versus 3004), lubricants, wipes, health drinks, glucose powders, juices, Hajmola and Eno. Third, the claimed **0% on condoms and on oxygen therapy apparatus** rests on single sources. Fourth, **schedules are salt-level defaults**: Dolo 650 is listed as H but sold OTC, cetirizine and ranitidine sit between OTC and H, and items such as oseltamivir and vitamin D3 60K need confirmation from the label.

The practical answer is to load the seed list with three GST states (confirmed, probable, unknown), let the shopkeeper's first purchase bill or distributor invoice overwrite HSN and rate, and block billing of H1 and NDPS items without a prescription entry.

## Conclusion

The medical shop is the one trade where the master list carries legal weight, not just convenience. A wrong grocery rate costs a little tax; a wrong schedule on alprazolam or an MTP kit exposes the chemist to licence action. That shifts the design priority from breadth of SKUs to quality of three fields, schedule, HSN and GST, and argues for shipping fewer, flagged rows that the shop corrects from its own invoices rather than a large unverified catalogue presented as fact.

The 2025 rate cut is the opportunity. With medicines, Ayurveda, orthopaedics and most Chapter 90 devices converging on 5%, roughly four in five seed rows can default safely, and the remaining fifth, mostly FMCG health items and water and heat therapy goods, becomes a short, explicit review queue. That queue, not the size of the list, is what will make Grocery Khata trustworthy to a chemist.
