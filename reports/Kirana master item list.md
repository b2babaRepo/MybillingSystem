# Load nine hundred kirana items, flag forty

Grocery Khata's kirana master list should hold about **900 distinct items** in roughly 45 categories: about 330 staples, spice and dry-fruit rows, about 270 packaged food, dairy and drink rows, and about 330 personal care, home care and pooja rows. Each row should carry a Hindi name, a selling unit, a list of common pack sizes, a default GST rate and an HSN code. The research notes already contain all three tables at row level. Once duplicates such as ghee, papad, pickles, noodles, ready mixes, honey and salt are merged, they come to roughly 900 unique items. GST 2.0, in force from **22 September 2025**, makes loading easier than it would have been a year earlier. Almost every food item now sits at nil or 5%. Toothpaste, soap, shampoo, hair oil, toothbrushes and shaving cream also fell to 5%. Only a handful of groups carry other rates: aerated and caffeinated drinks at 40%, and detergents, cleaners, insecticides, deodorants, batteries and foil at 18% ([Tally](https://tallysolutions.com/gst/new-gst-rates-september-2025-business-impact/); [Vyapar](https://vyaparapp.in/blog/new-gst-rates/)). The weak spots are narrower than they look. About **40 item types** have an unconfirmed rate. The HSN codes come from the standard tariff rather than from a line-by-line check against CBIC. Pack sizes reflect general market knowledge, not brand sites. So the list should load with GST as an editable default, uncertain rows flagged, and the rate check against CBIC Notification 9/2025-CT(Rate) done before release.

## GST 2.0 collapses kirana into three slabs: nil, 5% and 18%

The reform's main effect on a kirana list is simplification. Ghee, dried nuts and packed namkeen moved from 12% to 5% ([Petpooja](https://blog.petpooja.com/finance-compliance/new-gst-rates-on-food/)). Almonds, pistachios, cashews and dates did the same ([Deccan Herald](https://www.deccanherald.com/amp/story/business%2Feconomy%2Frevised-gst-rates-full-list-of-items-that-will-get-cheaper-or-costlier-3711489)). Spices, both whole and ground, and simple spice mixtures are 5%, and HSN 2103 sauces and condiments dropped from 18% to 5% ([Busy: spices](https://busy.in/gst-rates/spices.md)). Sugar (1701) and edible oils stay at 5%. Jaggery is nil when sold loose and 5% when pre-packaged and labelled ([Busy: sugar](https://busy.in/gst-rates/sugar); [Busy: jaggery](https://busy.in/gst-rates/jaggery); [GimBooks](https://www.gimbooks.com/blog/new-gst-rates-in-india-2025/amp/)). The CBIC ready reckoner confirms packaged honey at 2.5% CGST, which is 5% in total ([CBIC reckoner mirror](https://a2ztaxcorp.net/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/CBIC-GST-Ready-Reckoner-indicating-updated-Central-Goods-and-Services-Tax-CGST-rates-on-goods.pdf)). That settles a question one of the notes had left open.

Packaged food follows the same pattern. Biscuits, namkeen, chocolate, sauces, pasta and noodles, butter, cheese, juices, coffee, malt drinks and packaged water are all 5%. UHT milk, pre-packaged paneer and Indian breads are nil ([Petpooja](https://blog.petpooja.com/finance-compliance/new-gst-rates-on-food/); [Kanakkupillai](https://www.kanakkupillai.com/learn/gst-2-0-reforms/)). The one big jump is in drinks. Aerated, sugared, caffeinated and carbonated fruit drinks went from 28% plus a 12% cess to a flat **40% with no cess**, so shelf prices barely moved ([Enkash](https://www.enkash.com/resources/blog/types-of-gst-rates-in-india-new-rate-list-and-updates)). This creates a trap the app must handle. Maaza, Frooti and Slice (non-aerated fruit drinks, 2202 99) are 5%, while Appy Fizz, Coca-Cola, Sting and Red Bull are 40%. The two groups share a four-digit HSN heading, so the master list must store the rate per item, not derive it from the HSN prefix.

Personal care moved the most. Hair oil, shampoo, toothpaste, toilet soap, toothbrushes and shaving cream went from 18% to 5% ([Upstox 5% list](https://upstox.com/news/personal-finance/tax/new-gst-rates-full-list-of-items-with-5-goods-and-services-tax/article-180697)). Agarbatti is 5% ([Vajiram & Ravi](https://vajiramandravi.com/current-affairs/gst-rates-in-india/)). Detergents were **left out of the cut** and stay at 18%. The FMCG industry openly called this a miss ([Financial Express via Magzter](https://www.magzter.com/en/stories/newspaper/Financial-Express-Kochi/GST-20-DETERGENTS-OUT-OF-RATE-CUT-IN-MISS-FOR-FMCG-FIRMS); [Upstox](https://upstox.com/news/market-news/stocks/gst-2-0-gst-cuts-elude-these-key-items-analysts-and-fmcg-players-say-it-is-surprising/article-180835)). One kirana-focused rate list shows detergent at 5% ([Accountune](https://www.accountune.com/fmcg-gst-rate-list-kirana-store-2026)), but it stands against two mainstream reports, so 18% is the right default. A soap at 5% and a detergent bar at 18% often sit side by side on a shelf, which is exactly why the app should show the rate on the bill line.

| Default rate | What gets it in a kirana list |
|---|---|
| **Nil** | Salt of all kinds (2501); loose atta, rice, dal, millets and jaggery; fresh, pouch and UHT milk (0401); paneer (0406); bread and pav (1905 90); papad (likely nil, but see below) |
| **5%** | The same grains, flours and pulses when pre-packaged and labelled; sugar, boora and mishri; all edible oils and ghee; vanaspati; whole and ground spices and masala blends; dry fruits and nuts; honey; tea and coffee; biscuits, cakes and rusk; namkeen, chips and extruded snacks; noodles, pasta and vermicelli; cereals and oats; sauces, jams, pickles and ready mixes; chocolate and candy; butter, cheese and cream; juices, non-aerated fruit drinks and packaged water; toothpaste, tooth powder and toothbrushes; bath soap; shampoo and hair oil; shaving cream; agarbatti and dhoop |
| **18%** | Detergent powder, bars and liquid; fabric conditioner; dishwash; floor and toilet cleaners; phenyl; naphthalene and air fresheners; mosquito repellents, coils and insect sprays; deodorants; batteries; aluminium foil and cling wrap; plastic garbage and carry bags |
| **40%** | Aerated soft drinks (Coca-Cola, Thums Up, Pepsi, Campa, Sprite, Limca, Appy Fizz); energy drinks (Red Bull, Sting, Monster); tonic and flavoured soda |

## Staples, masala and dry fruits fill 330 rows with a loose-versus-packed rule

The staples table has 328 rows ([Busy: spices](https://busy.in/gst-rates/spices.md); [myBillBook HSN list](https://mybillbook.in/gst-rate-changes-2025-hsn-list-old-vs-new)). It pairs a generic loose row with three to eight branded rows in each group, because a real kirana sells both. **Flour and atta** (26 rows, HSN 1101 to 1108) cover wheat atta (गेहूं आटा) loose and under Aashirvaad, Fortune, Pillsbury, Patanjali and Annapurna in 1, 5 and 10 kg packs. They also include maida, suji and Bombay rava, dalia, besan, the millet flours (bajra, jowar, makki, ragi) and the fasting flours (singhara, kuttu, rajgira). **Rice** (22 rows, 1006) spans basmati grades from India Gate and Daawat down to dubar, tibar and mogra brokens. It also covers regional everyday rice: Sona Masoori, Kolam, Parmal, Ponni, Matta, idli and sella. Poha, murmura and kheel follow under 1904. **Pulses** (30 rows, 0713) list each dal in its forms: toor/arhar plain and oily, moong dhuli, chilka and sabut, masoor, urad, chana dal, kabuli and kala chana, three rajma types, lobia, safed and hari matar, moth, kulthi and panchmel. Bhuna chana, phutana and sattu sit under 1106. Most of these items need one rule rather than one rate: **nil when loose, 5% when pre-packaged and labelled**. The master list therefore needs a "packed" flag or two rate fields, rather than a single number.

Soya and dry vegetables are thinner. Soya chunks (सोया बड़ी) come loose and as Nutrela chunks, mini chunks and granules in 45 g, 200 g and 1 kg packs, plus Fortune. Sabudana comes in small and large (nylon) grades. **Oils** (25 rows) cover kachi ghani mustard (1514) loose and under Fortune, Dhara, Patanjali, Engine and P Mark. They also cover soybean (1507), sunflower and blends such as Saffola and Sundrop (1512), groundnut (1508), rice bran and til (1515), palmolein (1511), edible coconut (1513) and olive oil, in 500 ml, 1 L, 5 L and 15 L tins. Ghee (0405) comes loose and under eight brands, and vanaspati (1516) under Dalda, Fortune and Rath. **Sugar and sweeteners** (12 rows) are loose and packed sugar, boora, mishri, brown sugar, khand, gud in three forms, and honey from Dabur, Patanjali and Saffola. **Salt** (8 rows, nil) is Tata, Tata Lite, Aashirvaad, Annapurna and Catch, plus sendha, kala and khada namak.

Masala is the deepest group. **Whole spices** (30 rows) run from jeera, saunf and dhaniya (0909) through methi, ajwain, tej patta, kasuri methi and haldi (0910), laung (0907), both elaichis, jaiphal and javitri (0908), dalchini (0906), kali mirch and three kinds of dry red chilli (0904), to hing, imli, kokum and anardana. Most come in 10 to 250 g packs. **Ground spices** (19 rows) give loose and branded haldi, lal mirch, dhaniya and jeera from MDH, Everest, Catch and Tata Sampann. **Masala blends** (50 rows, 0910) form a grid of ten common blends across four brands: garam, chana, chhole, sabzi, kitchen king, pav bhaji, chaat, biryani, sambar and meat masala, from MDH, Everest, Catch and Badshah. Specials such as rajmah, shahi paneer, chicken, jaljira, tea masala, Eastern sambar, Aachi rasam and panipuri masala round out the group. **Dry fruits and nuts** (25 rows, 5%) cover kaju in W320, W240 and broken grades, copra and desiccated coconut (0801), badam, mamra, akhrot with and without shell, pista and chilgoza (0802), kishmish, munakka (0806), anjeer, khajoor, chhuhara (0804), khubani and aloo bukhara (0813), makhana and chironji, plus peanuts and six seeds (chia, alsi, pumpkin, sunflower, magaj and sabja).

## Packaged foods and drinks add 270 brand-led rows, priced by the rupee

The packaged-food table is organised around brand SKUs, because that is how shopkeepers name these items ([Tally](https://tallysolutions.com/gst/new-gst-rates-september-2025-business-impact/); [Curly Tales](https://curlytales.com/india/ct-scoop/butter-ghee-chocolates-cold-drinks-what-got-cheaper-costlier-after-gst-rate-revision/amp/)). **Biscuits, cookies, rusk and cakes** (about 50 rows, 1905 31, 1905 40 and 1905 90) cover Parle-G, Krackjack, Monaco and Hide & Seek, plus Britannia Marie Gold, Good Day, Bourbon, Tiger, 50-50 and the Toastea and cake rusks. Sunfeast, Oreo, Unibic, Priyagold, Anmol and Bisk Farm also appear. **Namkeen and snacks** (about 35 rows) are Haldiram's, Bikaji and Balaji sev, bhujia and mixes under 2106, chips under 2005 20, and extruded snacks such as Kurkure, Bingo and Cheetos under 1904 10. Many snack and confectionery items sell by **price point, not grammage**: Lay's at ₹10, 20 and 50, Kurkure at ₹5, and Dairy Milk at ₹5 to 50. Re 1 candies are another example. The pack-size field therefore has to accept "₹10" as well as "52 g". **Noodles, pasta, cereals and ready food** (about 40 rows) include Maggi, Yippee, Top Ramen, Ching's, Wai Wai, Bambino vermicelli, Quaker and Saffola oats, Kellogg's, MTR and Gits mixes, ready-to-eat meals and Knorr soups.

Drinks and dairy make up the rest. **Tea and coffee** (about 20 rows, 0902, 0901 and 2101) cover Tata Tea Premium, Gold and Agni, plus Red Label, Taj Mahal, Taaza, Wagh Bakri and Society, in 100 g to 1 kg packs. Nescafé, Bru and Continental cover coffee. **Health drinks and milk powders** list Bournvita, Horlicks, Boost, Complan, Amulya, Everyday and Sagar. **Sauces, spreads, pickles and papad** (about 30 rows) list Kissan, Maggi, Heinz and Ching's sauces, mayonnaise, jams, Nutella, peanut butter, the Mother's Recipe, Nilon's and Priya pickles, and Lijjat papad. **Chocolates and candy** (about 30 rows, 1806 and 1704) cover Cadbury, Nestlé, Ferrero and Mars bars, Parle and Perfetti candies, and gums. **Cold drinks, juices and water** (about 40 rows) carry the critical 40% versus 5% split already described, along with Bisleri, Kinley, Aquafina and Bailey water at 5%. **Dairy** (about 40 rows) lists pouch milk from Amul, Mother Dairy and the state cooperatives (Nandini, Aavin, Verka, Saras, Sudha and Heritage) at nil. Curd and lassi come next, then paneer at nil, butter, cheese, cream and condensed milk at 5%, and ice cream. **Bread and bakery** (about 10 rows) list Britannia, Modern, Harvest Gold and English Oven loaves, plus pav and bun, at nil.

## Personal and home care spans 331 rows and three tax slabs

The care table's 331 rows cover more GST variety than any other group ([Upstox what gets cheaper](https://upstox.com/news/personal-finance/tax/gst-2-0-what-gets-cheaper-and-what-gets-expensive-from-september-22-check-full-list/article-181688)). **Toothpaste** (22 rows, 3306 10 20, 5%) covers Colgate Strong Teeth, MaxFresh, Active Salt, Vedshakti and Sensitive, plus Pepsodent, Close-Up, Sensodyne, Dabur Red, Meswak, Babool, Patanjali Dant Kanti, Himalaya and Vicco, in 20 g to 300 g tubes. **Tooth powder** (3306 10 10) adds Colgate, Dabur Lal Dant Manjan, Vicco and Patanjali, all now 5% (previously 12%). **Toothbrushes** (12 rows, 9603 21, 5%) are Colgate ZigZag, Super Flexi, Slim Soft and Kids, Oral-B, Pepsodent, Ajay and Patanjali, sold singly or in 3+1 packs. **Bath soap** (23 rows, 3401 11, 5%) lists Lux, Lifebuoy, Dettol, Santoor, Cinthol, Dove, Pears, Godrej No.1, Medimix, Hamam, Patanjali, Mysore Sandal, Margo, Chandrika and Liril, in 45 g to 150 g bars and 4-bar packs. **Shampoo** (15 rows, 3305 10) covers Clinic Plus, Sunsilk, Head & Shoulders, Pantene, Dove, Tresemmé, Garnier, Himalaya, Vatika, Patanjali, Chik and Meera. The Re 1 and ₹2 sachet is its own pack size here, and it is the one that sells most. **Hair oil** (12 rows, 3305 90, 5%) runs from Parachute in packs as small as 25 ml up to a 1 L jar, plus Nihar, Dabur Amla, Bajaj Almond Drops, Navratna, Kesh King and Indulekha. Shaving cream and foam (3307 10) are 5%. Talc, skin creams, face wash, lip care, deodorant (3307 20, 18%), blades, sanitary pads (9619, nil since 2018) and diapers fill out personal care.

Home care is almost entirely 18%. **Detergent powder** (15 rows, 3402 50) covers Surf Excel, Tide, Ariel, Wheel, Rin, Ghadi, Nirma, Henko and Fena, from ₹10 sachets to 5 kg bags. **Detergent bars** (8 rows) and **liquid detergents and fabric care** (9 rows, including Comfort and Ujala) follow. **Dishwash** (10 rows) is Vim, Pril and Exo as bar, gel and powder. **Cleaners** (11 rows) include Lizol, Dettol liquid, Domex, Colin, phenyl and Harpic. Pest control (16 rows, 3808 91) covers Good Knight, All Out, Mortein, coils, HIT and Laxman Rekha. The remaining groups are batteries (8506), foil (7607), and garbage and carry bags (3923). **Pooja** goods are a real kirana category of their own, with 36 rows. Agarbatti and dhoop from Cycle, Mangaldeep, Zed Black, Moksh, Hem and Satya (3307 41) are 5%. Camphor, diyas, batti, roli, kumkum, kalava, havan samagri and gangajal have uncertain rates. Generic "local" rows in each group let a shopkeeper rename loose or unbranded stock instead of creating items from scratch.

## Forty item types and every HSN digit still need a CBIC check

The open questions are specific. No note fetched the primary CBIC notification, so every rate rests on secondary reporting, though the main slabs agree across sources. The unconfirmed rates fall into four groups. **Food edge cases:** packaged curd, lassi and chhaach (probably 5%, or nil if loose); plain soda (5% or 18%); chewing gum; papad when branded rather than loose; sabudana (1903, nil or 5%); soya chunks (2106 or 2304); makhana; hing; cornflour; oats; oilseeds sold as spices (rai, til, kalonji and khus khus under 1207); baking powder and soda; infant food; Glucon-D, Tang, Rasna and Rooh Afza; and ready-to-drink coffee or iced tea, which could be 5% or 40% depending on whether it counts as a caffeinated beverage. **Personal care edge cases:** mouthwash (18% before; possibly 5% now); face wash (5% if treated as soap, 18% if treated as skin care); skin creams and lip balm (18%, unconfirmed); talc and diapers (reported in the cut, but unverified); and razor blades. **Home and pooja:** detergent bars (3401 19 or 3402); camphor; candles; matches; LED bulbs; tissue; paper bags; and stationery. Dettol antiseptic liquid also needs a decision, because it may fall under 3004 as a medicament rather than as a cleaner. One source puts agarbatti at 12% ([IndiaFilings](https://www.indiafilings.com/learn/gst-rate-for-perfume-cosmetics-and-toiletries)), but it is an outlier against two that say 5%.

HSN codes are the second gap. The staples table gives only four-digit headings. The packaged-food table gives eight-digit codes from the standard tariff, which have not been verified item by item. The care table gives six- to eight-digit codes at the same confidence. Four-digit HSN is enough on invoices for businesses with turnover up to ₹5 crore, which covers almost every kirana. So the safe approach is to load four-digit codes as authoritative and keep longer codes as hints. Several codes conflict between the notes. Oats appear as 1104 in one note and 1104 12 or 1904 90 in another, depending on whether they are plain or masala. Maggi is listed under 1902 in one note and 1902 30 10 in another. Peanuts are listed as 1202 raw in one and 2008 11 roasted in another. These need one rule each before loading.

The third gap is pack sizes and brands. They come from general 2024–25 market knowledge rather than brand sites. FMCG firms change grammage often, and some SKUs, such as Breeze soap and Milo, may already be gone. Pack sizes should therefore load as suggestions the shop can edit, never as fixed variants. The Hindi names are shop-floor terms: brand names are written in Devanagari, and generic items use the common North Indian word (तूर/अरहर, सौंफ, मंजन). South and East Indian shops will want regional names, and no note covers them.

## Conclusion

The research changes the loading plan more than the item count. A kirana master list does not need a full tax engine. It needs three things the current catalogue lacks. The first is a **packed-versus-loose flag**, because the same dal is nil or 5% depending on packaging. The second is a **rate stored per item, not inferred from HSN**, because 2202 holds both a 5% Maaza and a 40% Thums Up, and 3401 holds both a 5% soap and a possibly 18% detergent bar. The third is a **pack-size field that accepts rupee price points** as well as weights, because sachet and ₹10 SKUs are what a kirana sells most. With those three in place, about 860 of the 900 rows can load with confidence today.

The roughly 40 flagged item types should load with their best-guess rate and a visible "CA should confirm" marker, rather than being held back. Holding them back would leave shampoo sachets and detergent missing from the most-used category. A single admin pass against Notification 9/2025-CT(Rate) would then clear the flags in one push. That pass is the only remaining research task that blocks correct GST filing. Brand pack sizes and regional names can be improved over time from what shops actually type.
