# Give every trade its own shelf map

Apna Kaarobar should keep **one `<CategoryMenu>` component for every shop type** and change only the data and two slots per trade. The component is the shared shell already designed for grocery: an icon rail, grouped tiles, sub-category chips, scoped search, filter chips, an in-menu stepper and a fixed bill bar. The data is a **per-trade tree registry** that holds departments, tiles, chips, filters, sort and synonyms. The two slots are a **trade-specific listing row** and a **trade-specific picker**: a size × colour grid for clothes, a batch/expiry row for medicines, a one-tag-one-piece row priced live from the day's gold rate for jewellery, a size ladder and shade entry for hardware and paint, and a coil/sq-mm row for electrical wire. The app is better prepared than the grocery plan assumed. Six trades already have master lists, with 958 kirana, 952 beauty, 847 medical, 337 paint, 319 hardware and 204 clothes items. Many filter fields already exist on `Product`: medicine schedule and strip size, clothes style, size and colour, serial and warranty, MRP and wholesale price. Clothes categories already encode two levels ("Women · Sarees"). Three gaps decide the order of work. **There is no per-trade configuration anywhere**: behaviour hangs off scattered `types.includes(...)` checks. **Electrical has only 14 starter rows and jewellery has no master list at all**, because its stock is tagged `JewelleryPiece` rows billed inside `JewelleryScreen`. And several attributes each trade filters on are **not stored**: medicine salt, rack and cold chain; paint base; hardware size; wire sq mm; beauty shade. The recommended sequence ships the shared shell with kirana first. Then come clothes, medical and beauty, whose data is ready. Hardware and paint follow, then electrical once a master list exists, and jewellery last, through an adapter. My own estimate is **about 25–33 developer days** in total, of which the 10–14 days already planned for kirana are the first half. Competitor menus in this report come from search snippets of retailer and vendor pages, not live screens, so labels are starting points to test with shopkeepers.

## One shell, a trade registry and two swappable slots

The grocery design carries over to every trade unchanged at the level of layout and interaction. On a phone the menu opens as a full-screen sheet over the bill, so the cart is never lost. The rail is about 80 px wide, with two-tone SVG icons and a two-line label in the app language plus English. Tiles run three per row with a photo or icon, the name and an in-stock count. A pinned "★ रोज़ के / Your usual" entry sits at the top of the rail. Inside a tile, a sticky header holds a search box scoped to that tile, a row of sibling sub-category chips and a row of filter chips. Every row carries a stepper, and a dark tray at the bottom counts each add and returns to the bill. These rules came from the grocery research: show only what the shop stocks, keep siblings one tap apart, put the important filters on one-tap chips with a bottom sheet behind them, and never dead-end a search ([Baymard](https://baymard.com/blog/ecommerce-navigation-best-practice); [Baymard promoting filters](https://baymard.com/blog/promoting-product-filters); [NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/international-shoppers-ecommerce-sites/)). None of them is grocery-specific. Job-specific chips are shared too, because they read fields that every trade's products carry: "Low stock" reads `ReorderLevel`, "Offer" checks whether `Mrp` is above the price, and "Wholesale" reads `WholesalePrice`. That gives low stock and this supplier on supplier orders, and in stock on the bill ([Product.cs:80, 116-124](backend/src/GroceryKhata.Api/Catalogue/Product.cs)).

What varies by trade is narrower than it looks. It comes to five pieces of data and two pieces of UI. The data is the tree (rail, tiles, chips), the filter set, the default sort, the synonym pack and the tile art. The UI is the row and the picker. A registry entry per trade, `TRADE_MENUS[trade] = { tree, filters, sorts, synonyms, rowKind, pickerKind, searchModes }`, captures all seven. The registry would be new, because the app has no per-trade configuration object today. Its 19 trades switch behaviour through lists such as `TRADE_TYPES`, `COMMISSION_TYPES`, `SERVICE_TYPES` and `fullPageTrades`, plus inline checks across Home, the bill screen, the product form and the store ([reports.ts:128-148](frontend/src/lib/reports.ts); [HomeScreen.tsx:121-138](frontend/src/screens/HomeScreen.tsx); [productSheet.ts:1-2](frontend/src/lib/productSheet.ts)). It can follow the same list-in-a-file pattern. The bill screen's existing trade branches already show which rows will be needed: style grouping for clothes, the prescription gate for scheduled drugs, the serial picker, the tinted paint line and the 18% clothes note above ₹2,500 ([BillScreen.tsx:66-68, 455-461, 480, 979, 1525-1536](frontend/src/screens/BillScreen.tsx)).

Shops with more than one trade (main `Type` plus `ExtraTypes`, merged by `shopTypes()`) should get **a trade switch at the top of the rail**, not a merged tree. `PickItemsScreen` already does exactly this. It builds categories per `activeTrade`, shows trade chips when a shop has several, and takes its search hint from the trade's own items "so a clothes shop isn't told to search for aata" ([PickItemsScreen.tsx:53-93, 164-206](frontend/src/screens/PickItemsScreen.tsx); [Shop.cs:156-157](backend/src/GroceryKhata.Api/Shops/Shop.cs)). A merged tree would put a kirana-plus-sweets shop's two rails into one list of 14 departments, which is past Baymard's threshold of about ten options per level.

The plumbing changes are the same for every trade. `Aisles` becomes trade-keyed and is served as `/catalogue/aisles?trade=x`. Every CSV, not just kirana's, gets the `node` column. `MasterItem.Node` and `Category.TreeKey` resolve every product, falling back from the master link to the category key, then a name rule, then "Other". Starter rows need explicit mapping. Otherwise paint's starter "Interior" and the CSV's "Interior emulsion" show as two tiles, and every other trade's starter rows land in "Other" ([StarterItems.cs:340-352](backend/src/GroceryKhata.Api/Catalogue/StarterItems.cs); [MasterItems.cs:150-172](backend/src/GroceryKhata.Api/Catalogue/MasterItems.cs)). Labels also need a fix in both directions. Kirana categories are Hindi-only while every other CSV is English-only, so each non-kirana node needs Hindi and Gujarati names written once in the tree.

## Seven trades, seven concrete menus

Each trade's menu below lists the rail, the tiles grouped under each department, the chips inside a tile, the filters, the sort, the search synonyms and the special row. Rail and tile counts come from the trade's master CSV where one exists. Hindi and regional words marked "verify" come from the researchers' own knowledge, not a source, and should be checked with two or three shopkeepers of that trade before they ship.

### Grocery and kirana keep the seven-department tree already designed

Kirana is unchanged from the grocery plan. The rail holds ★ रोज़ के, then seven departments: रोज़ का राशन, मसाले और मेवे, चाय-कॉफ़ी-पेय, नूडल्स-फ़ास्ट फ़ूड-स्नैक्स, दूध-ब्रेड, घर और देखभाल, and पूजा और बच्चे. It has about 20 category tiles. Dal, masala, dry fruits, tea, noodles and fast food each get their own tile, split out of today's merged Hindi strings ([kirana-master.csv](backend/src/GroceryKhata.Api/Catalogue/Data/kirana-master.csv)). Chips appear only in large tiles, for example तूर · मूंग · चना · उड़द under Dal. Bill filters are in stock, offer and the top brands, with pack size and price in the sheet. The default sort is most sold. Synonyms use the existing Hindi, Gujarati and Hinglish table in `lib/search.ts` (doodh→milk, haldi→turmeric), with brand words kept out of expansion ([search.ts](frontend/src/lib/search.ts); [arXiv 2208.03713](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2208.03713v1.pdf)). The row is the plain dense row: a 40 px thumbnail, the name, the pack and a −/qty/+ stepper. Kirana is the reference implementation that every other trade swaps parts into, which is why it ships first.

### Jewellery browses by metal, then body part, and every row is one tagged piece

Indian jewellers browse by product type, with a style layer below it. Tanishq splits earrings into drop, hoop, stud, pearl, jhumka and bali, and rings into engagement, promise, daily wear, solitaire and vanki ([Tanishq](https://www.tanishq.com/jewelry)). Metal, gender, occasion and collection run across that tree as facets: CaratLane's URLs combine type × metal × gender × theme ([CaratLane](https://caratlane.com/jewellery)). Coins and bars sit in their own department, browsed by weight ([Candere gold coins](https://www.candere.com/gifts/gold-coins.html); [BlueStone gold coins](https://bluestone.com/jewellery/goldcoins.html)). Silver-led GIVA promotes anklets and toe rings to the top level ([GIVA silver](https://giva.co/collections/silver-jewellery)). A counter is a metal-first room, so the rail should lead with metal and the tiles should group by where the piece is worn.

Weight bands, purity counts and colour come from Candere's and CaratLane's filter panels ([Candere 1 g bar](https://www.candere.com/gifts/gold-bar/1+gram.html); [CaratLane gold bangles](https://www.caratlane.com/jewellery/gold-bangles.html); [CaratLane 14KT rings](https://www.caratlane.us/jewelry/fashion+rings-14kt+gold.html)). BIS defines the mandatory gold grades as 14, 18, 20, 22, 23 and 24 karat, and HUID has been mandatory on hallmarked gold since April 2023 ([News on AIR](https://www.newsonair.gov.in/govt-adds-7-more-districts-in-6th-phase-of-mandatory-gold-hallmarking)). Reports conflict on whether 9K became mandatory in July 2025 ([Zee Biz](https://www.zeebiz.com/markets/commodities/news-hallmarking-of-9-carat-gold-made-mandatory-today-by-bureau-of-indian-standards-bis-hallmark-for-20-karat-22-karat-23-karat-24-karat-18-karat-14-karat-gold-373953)), so 9K (375) should stay in the list but off by default. Silver grades 925 and 800 make up about 95% of HUID-marked silver ([Deccan Herald](https://www.deccanherald.com/india/over-77-lakh-silver-jewellery-pieces-get-huid-hallmarking-since-september-2025-4127526)). That is why the silver chips can be just 999 · 925 · 800. Retail sites do not offer HUID or making type as shopper filters, so the menu keeps them counter-only, which is also how Omunim treats HUID: as a search key ([Omunim](https://omunim.com/)).

Jewellery is the one trade where the generic stepper row is wrong. Counter software is tag-centric: each piece has a barcode or RFID tag with its gross weight, net weight and purity, often printed straight from the scale ([Omunim billing](https://omunim.com/jewellery-billing-software); [Marg via SoftwareSuggest](https://www.softwaresuggest.com/marg-jewellery)). So a tile lists **pieces in stock with their weight**, not a SKU with a quantity. The price on the row is computed as net weight × the day's rate for that purity, plus making (₹/g, % or flat), wastage and stone value, plus 3% GST. It recomputes when the rate bar changes ([CaratLane price guide](https://www.caratlane.com/blog/how-to-calculate-the-price-of-your-gold-jewellery/); [Tanishq product page](https://www.tanishq.com/product/classy-ring-mangalsutra-up1919yevaa00.html)). Imitation pieces carry a fixed MRP and skip the rate. The app already stores every field this row needs (`Metal`, `Purity`, `Gross`, `Stone`, `WastagePercent`, `MakingType`, `MakingValue`, `StoneValue`, `Huid`, `FixedPrice`, `Tray`), and `jewellery.ts` already labels 916 as "22K" ([Jewellery.cs:36-81](backend/src/GroceryKhata.Api/Ledger/Jewellery.cs); [jewellery.ts:3-8, 56-59](frontend/src/lib/jewellery.ts)). The structural problem is that jewellery stock is not `Product` and its billing lives in `JewelleryScreen`'s own bill tab ([JewelleryScreen.tsx:25](frontend/src/screens/JewelleryScreen.tsx)). The menu therefore needs a `JewelleryPiece` adapter that presents pieces as items and hands picks to that tab. `Category` must also move from free text to a node key on each piece, which is a one-time mapping of the existing values.

### Hardware and paint split into two rails with size ladders and shade memory

The app treats paint and hardware as separate trades with separate CSVs, and the menu should keep them apart. A paint-and-hardware shop then uses the trade switch. Moglix keeps Hardware, Fasteners, Plumbing and Electricals as separate top-level departments, each two or three levels deep: Door Hardware holds hinges, closers, handles and drawer slides, and Latches & Bolts holds tower bolts by material and length ([Moglix Hardware](https://www.moglix.com/hardware/218000000); [Moglix Door Hardware](https://www.moglix.com/hardware/door-hardware/218110000); [Moglix Fasteners](https://www.moglix.com/fasteners/111000000); [Moglix Plumbing guide](https://www.moglix.com/buyer-guide/plumbing)). That fits the hardware CSV once its thinnest categories (Chain, rope and welding has 1 item; Door and furniture fittings has 4) are merged.

Variants live in titles across the trade, and that is why the row must be a ladder rather than 50 flat rows. A pipe fitting differs by size, SDR and pack ("Astral Elbow 90 deg CPVC, 50 mm, Pack of 50"). CPVC sizes map 1/2" = 15 mm through 2" = 50 mm under IS 15778. Tower bolts run from 6 to 18 inches in brass, SS or aluminium ([Moglix Plumbing guide](https://www.moglix.com/buyer-guide/plumbing); [Apollo Pipes CPVC](https://apollopipes.com/cpvc-plumbing-system); [Prince Pipes price list](https://www.princepipes.com/pdf/Prince-Pipes-Pricelist-Pan-India-CPVC.pdf); [Moglix Tower Bolts](https://www.moglix.com/hardware/door-hardware/latches-bolts/218111800)). Hinges are sold by the piece at ₹20–28 and also by the kilogram at ₹80, so the unit of sale varies within a single tile ([IndiaMART kabza](https://www.indiamart.com/proddetail/m-s-door-kabza-2850023703712.html); [IndiaMART bolt kabza](https://www.indiamart.com/proddetail/bolt-kabza-metal-hinges-2853452780455.html)). One vendor's own example is that "GI pipe" is ten products across sizes, prices and GST rates ([Accountune](https://accountune.com/hardware-store-billing-software)). Hindi trade names for hinges are confirmed by dictionary and trade listings ([Collins](https://www.collinsdictionary.com/dictionary/hindi-english/%E0%A4%95%E0%A4%AC%E0%A5%8D%E0%A5%9B%E0%A4%BE); [IndiaMART hinges](https://www.indiamart.com/gryphon-lock/hinges.html)). The rest of the counter vocabulary is unverified.

Paint's special flow already exists, and the menu should feed into it rather than replace it. Asian Paints sells colour by shade code: the customer brings a code to a dealer, who tints to order, and codes are specific to each product line ([Asian Paints catalogue directory](https://www.asianpaints.com/resources/tools/catalogue-directory.html)). Packs come in 1, 4, 10 and 20 L, for example Royale Wall Base Coat at ₹350, ₹1,275, ₹3,000 and ₹5,520 MRP ([Asian Paints](https://www.asianpaints.com/products/paints-and-textures/interior-walls/plain-finishes/royale-wall-base-coat.html)). So the item is product + pack, and the shade belongs on the bill line. That is exactly what the app does today. `TradeBill` writes a tinted line as "base · shade CODE", and `ShadeHistoryDto` serves "repeat last shade" per customer ([TradeBill.tsx:96, 145-154](frontend/src/screens/TradeBill.tsx); [Trades.cs:605-616](backend/src/GroceryKhata.Api/Ledger/Trades.cs)). In the menu, tapping a paint row's pack chip opens the existing tint sheet. The paint search box also accepts a shade code and lists the customer's recent lines that used it. The base field (white, pastel, deep) is not stored and was not sourced, so a base filter waits.

### Medical opens on search and treats the rail as the fallback

Every large Indian e-pharmacy keeps two trees. **Medicines** are found by search, A–Z or prescription upload, not by browsing. **Healthcare and wellness products** are browsed through about 10–15 departments, plus a "by health concern" entry. 1mg separates `/drugs/` from `/otc/` URLs, Apollo lists medicines A–Z, and Netmeds' wellness groups are Ayush, Fitness, Personal Care, Sexual Wellness, Mom & Baby, Diabetes Support, Devices, Surgical and Treatments ([1mg category tree](https://www.1mg.com/category-tree); [1mg OTC product](https://www.1mg.com/otc/go-shudh-natural-salt-otc556735); [Apollo medicine A–Z](https://www.apollopharmacy.in/medicine); [Netmeds sitemap](https://www.netmeds.com/page/site-map)). Counter software points the same way. Marg's billing search modes are Salt, Substitute, Rack and Name, plus barcode and item code, and GoFrugal sells rack/shelf/box location, a generic drug index and short-expiry blocking ([Marg drug inventory](https://margcompusoft.com/retail/drug-inventory-management-software.html); [GoFrugal pharmacy](https://www.gofrugal.com/retail/pharmacy-software/)). The medical menu should therefore open with **search focused and a mode toggle**. The rail sits behind it and groups the 24 therapy categories into nine departments.

Health concerns cut across this tree. 1mg places Diabetes under both Vitamins and Ayurveda ([1mg vitamins > diabetes](https://www.1mg.com/categories/vitamins-nutrition/top-picks-vitamins-nutrition/diabetes-4342)). So the medical tree should allow a node to have **more than one parent**: Diabetes appears under Chronic care and under Ayurveda without the product being duplicated. Hindi non-brand searches such as "खांसी की दवा" carry real query volume ([Techmagnate](https://www.techmagnate.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/05/Online-Pharmacy-Trends-India-FY_24new.pdf)), which justifies mapping concern words to tiles. Substitution needs a salt key: 1mg shows "All substitutes" with pack and maker and offers a same-composition alternative when an item is sold out, and Truemeds matches on salt + strength + form ([1mg Pantomit](https://www.1mg.com/drugs/pantomit-40mg-injection-513342); [Truemeds Play listing](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.intellihealth.truemeds&hl=en_IN)). The app has none of these: a search for salt or composition in the backend found only GST composition-scheme code, and there is no rack field ([Medical shop pharmacy plan](reports/Medical%20shop%20pharmacy%20plan.md); [Godowns.cs:15-24](backend/src/GroceryKhata.Api/Catalogue/Godowns.cs)). Schedule, batches with FEFO and the H1 screen do exist, so the first release ships schedule, expiry and batch rows, and salt and rack follow as a data project ([Batches.cs:15-34, 145-146](backend/src/GroceryKhata.Api/Catalogue/Batches.cs); [H1RegisterScreen.tsx](frontend/src/screens/H1RegisterScreen.tsx)).

The customer store needs its own rules for this trade. Schedule H labels carry "Rx" and a red-boxed warning, and H1 sales go into a separate register kept for three years ([CDSCO labelling notice](https://www.cdsco.gov.in/opencms/resources/UploadCDSCOWeb/2018/UploadPublic_NoticesFiles/labellingofcautionary_289.doc); [Indian Kanoon rule text](https://future.indiankanoon.org/doc/196956471/)). The 2018 draft e-pharmacy rules, never notified, would require a prescription upload for H/H1 and bar Schedule X and NDPS sales online ([Mondaq](https://webiis08.mondaq.com/india/food-and-drugs-law/740234/draft-rules-for-e-pharmacy-under-the-drugs-and-cosmetic-rules-1945)). A later draft amending Rule 65 would require central sanction before retailers advertise H, H1 and X drugs, though sources disagree on its date and final status ([News On AIR](https://newsonair.gov.in/govt-proposes-amendments-to-drugs-rules1945-to-strengthen-oversight-of-schedule-h-h1-and-x-drugs/); [Nishith Desai](https://www.nishithdesai.com/default.aspx?id=15644)). So the store menu should hide X items entirely, accept H and H1 only with a prescription upload, and keep scheduled drugs out of the ★ and offer tiles. Those filters belong in the registry, not in screen code.

### Clothes already has its tree in the data and needs a size-first, age-aware grid

Kids and baby clothes need an **age scale, not the adult S–XL scale**. The clothing plan already sets the rule "one size scale per category, not one global list" ([Clothing shop management plan](reports/Clothing%20shop%20management%20plan.md)). The app's `compareSizes` already orders kids' ages correctly, putting "0-3M" before "3-6M" and both before numeric and letter sizes ([styles.ts:1-57](frontend/src/lib/styles.ts)). So age labels can be stored straight in `Product.Size` with no schema change. The recommended bands are 0–3 M, 3–6 M, 6–12 M, 12–18 M and 18–24 M for babies. Many brands label kids' wear by number (20, 22, 24…) or by height. A small per-shop mapping table (size label → age band), editable on the size report screen, covers those without forcing relabelling. Adult tiles keep their own scales: S–XXL for tops, waist 28–40 for jeans and trousers, and band × cup for bras.

## Rows, filters and search are where trades really differ

Most of the per-trade cost sits in seven row kinds, not in seven menus. The listing renderer should take `rowKind` from the registry and swap only the row and its picker. Everything around them stays shared: header, chips, filters and tray.

| `rowKind` | Trades | Row shows | Tap opens | Built on |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `plain` | Kirana, hardware basics, beauty without shades | Thumb, name, pack, price, stock, stepper | — | `ItemCard` |
| `ladder` | Hardware, paint, electrical wire | Parent line + size/pack chips with stock | Unit toggle; for paint, the tint sheet | Master `PackSizes`, `TradeBill` tint line |
| `style` | Clothes, beauty shades | One style with colour dots and a size strip | Size × colour grid | `groupStyles`, `compareSizes` |
| `batch` | Medical | Name, Rx/H1 badge, rack, FEFO batch, MRP | Batch picker; H1 register capture; substitutes | `ProductBatch`, `DrugSchedule`, H1 screen |
| `piece` | Jewellery | Tag, gross/net g, karat, HUID, live price | Piece detail with price breakdown | `JewelleryPiece`, rate table |
| `serial` | Sales & service, warrantied electrical | Name, stock of units | Serial picker | Existing bill serial picker |
| `service` | Beauty salons | Service, minutes, price | Appointment slot | `IsService`, `DurationMinutes` |

Filters become a trade × job matrix. The job chips stay shared: low stock and this supplier on orders, in stock on the bill, offer in the store. Each trade then adds its own one-tap chips, chosen as the attribute the counter asks about first. That is Schedule and Expiring for medical, Size for clothes, Purity and Weight for jewellery, Size for hardware, Pack for paint, sq mm for wire and Shade for beauty. The rest go into the bottom sheet with an explicit "Show N items" button, as in the grocery design ([Baymard apply buttons](https://baymard.com/guidelines/1125-apply-buttons-in-mobile-filtering-interfaces)). The store's filter drawer, today hidden only for kirana, returns for every trade once sub-category chips replace the rail ([StoreApp.tsx:387](frontend/src/screens/store/StoreApp.tsx)). Filters that need missing fields should not be shown as empty chips. The registry declares them, and the menu hides any facet with no data in this shop, the same "no dead ends" rule that hides empty tiles.

Search stays one engine, `rank` in `lib/search.ts`, with three per-trade extensions. First, **a synonym pack per trade** replaces today's grocery-only table, with brand words kept out of expansion and zero-result queries logged so each trade's pack grows from real use. That copies the cheap half of Zepto's approach of learning from quick rewrites ([Analytics India Magazine](https://analyticsindiamag.com/ai-features/zeptos-spellchecker-fixes-errors-with-a-little-help-from-llms/)). Second, **tokenisers per trade**: fractions, inches and millimetres for hardware, sq mm for wire, and strength and form for medicine. Third, **auto-detected keys**: tag and barcode everywhere, HUID for jewellery, salt and rack for medical once stored, shade code for paint, and style code for clothes. Every tree node's three names go into the index too, so typing "kangan" or "dal" also offers "open that tile →" at the top of the results.

Steps 1–4 (about 9–10 days) put the shared menu in front of kirana shops, and they are the grocery plan with the trade dimension built in from day one. Retrofitting that dimension later would mean reworking the tree endpoint and the node column. Steps 5–8 (about 10–11 days) bring the four trades with ready CSVs onto the same shell, one row kind at a time. Steps 10–11 are where effort is least certain. Electrical's cost is mostly content, and jewellery's is mostly integration with a billing screen that was built separately. Step 12 is a data project that can run in parallel once the menu hides facets that have no data.

Several things remain unverified. Live database state was not checked, so the number of each trade's products without a master link, which therefore fall back to name rules, is unknown. Competitor menus were read from search snippets because the retailer sites were blocked, so exact labels and orders are snapshots. Many counter words are unsourced: pench, keel, batti, khatka, tooti, chatkani, tops, kanser, and the beauty Devanagari terms. Electrical facet names for fan sweep and lighting base come from the researchers' own knowledge. The GST rate on imitation jewellery (HSN 7117) conflicts across secondary sources ([Busy](https://busy.in/hsn/sub-chapter-7117/); [RegisterKaro](https://www.registerkaro.in/hsn/gst-rate-hsn-code-7117)) and must be checked before imitation tiles show tax-inclusive prices. And the final status of the Rule 65 advertising draft is unresolved.

## Conclusion

Extending the grocery menu to every trade turns out not to be seven designs. It is **one shell plus a small vocabulary of row kinds**, and most of those rows already exist somewhere in the app: style grouping on the clothes bill, the tint sheet in `TradeBill`, FEFO batches for medicines, the serial picker, and the piece fields in the jewellery ledger. They have simply never sat behind a shared browse surface. The real per-trade work is data. Clothes is ready, medical, beauty, paint and hardware need node mapping, electrical needs a list written, and jewellery needs its pieces to speak the same tree language as products. Building the registry into step 1, rather than retrofitting it after kirana ships, is the single decision that keeps the total near a month instead of doubling it.

The trades also differ in how much browsing should matter, and the registry should encode that. Kirana and clothes shops browse. A chemist searches by name and salt, and opens the rail only when stuck. A jeweller works from tags and weights, under a rate that changes every morning. Hardware counters think in sizes before names. Copying the grocery menu literally would bury the medical search box and price jewellery wrongly. Giving each trade its default entry point (search-first, scan-first or browse-first), its own first filter chip and its own row is how one design serves seven very different counters.
